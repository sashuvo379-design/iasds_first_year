Section 01 • The 7 Cost Measures

Total, Fixed, Variable, Marginal & Average Costs

🏢 Fixed Cost (FC)

Total dollar expense paid out even when output q = 0 (e.g., factory lease, interest on bank debt, property insurance). Unaffected by output changes—also called overhead or sunk costs.

⚙️ Variable Cost (VC)

Costs that vary directly with output (raw materials, assembly-line workers, factory power). By definition, VC = 0 when q = 0, and TC = FC + VC.

📈 Marginal Cost (MC)

Additional cost incurred in producing 1 extra unit of output: MC = ΔTC / Δq = ΔVC / Δq = d(TC)/dq. Independent of fixed cost!

Why MC Always Pierces AC and AVC at Their Minimums (Batting Average Rule)
1. When MC < AC, the extra unit costs less than the existing average, pulling AC DOWN.
2. When MC > AC, the extra unit costs more than the existing average, pulling AC UP.
3. When MC = AC, AC is momentarily flat—so a rising MC curve always cuts AC (and AVC) at its exact minimum point!

Section 03 • Mirror Image of Production

Why Cost Curves Are U-Shaped & 4 Classic Cost Fallacies

Mirror-Image Link: Production Determines Costs (Figure 7-3 & Mihad's Notes)
Cost curves depend on two things: (1) Factor prices and (2) The firm's production function. When labor (at wage $W$) is the variable input: MC = W / MP_L.
• Initial phase of increasing marginal product ($MP_L \uparrow$) ⇒ falling marginal cost ($MC \downarrow$).
• Once the law of diminishing returns sets in ($MP_L \downarrow$) ⇒ rising marginal cost ($MC \uparrow$), producing U-shaped MC, AVC, and AC curves!

Spot the Fallacy! (Samuelson Ch. 7 Q6 — Exam Favorites)

❌ Fallacy 1: "AC is minimized when MC is at its lowest point."

False! When MC hits its minimum and starts rising, MC is still below AC, so AC continues to fall until the rising MC curve finally catches up and crosses AC at minimum AC.

❌ Fallacy 2: "Because FC never changes, AFC is constant."

False! Since $AFC = FC / q$, dividing a constant numerator ($FC$) by larger output ($q$) causes AFC to fall continuously as a rectangular hyperbola ("spreading the overhead").

❌ Fallacy 3: "AC is rising whenever MC is rising."

False! Between minimum MC (e.g., $Q=7$ in the cubic PYQ) and minimum AC ($Q=10.5$), MC is rising while AC is still falling because $MC < AC$!

❌ Fallacy 4: "A firm minimizes costs by spending the same amount on each input."

False! Cost minimization requires equalizing marginal product per dollar ($MP_L/P_L = MP_K/P_K$), not equal total spending on each input!

Section 04 • Optimal Input Choice (Appendix 7 & Final 2024/2025 PYQs)

Least-Cost Rule, Substitution Rule & Isoquant/Isocost Lab

The Least-Cost Rule
To produce a given output at minimum cost (or maximize output for a given budget), equalize the marginal product per dollar spent on every input:
MP_L / P_L = MP_K / P_K = …  ⇔  MP_L / MP_K = P_L / P_K
The Substitution Rule (Corollary)
If the price of one factor (e.g., glue or land) falls while other factor prices stay fixed, $MP_{\text{glue}} / P_{\text{glue}}$ jumps above other inputs. The firm profits by substituting more of the cheaper factor until marginal products per dollar are equalized again!

Section 05 • Broader Economic Perspective

Economic Costs, Accounting Costs & Opportunity Cost

📒 Accounting Cost (Explicit Outlays)

Measures actual out-of-pocket monetary transactions recorded on the Income Statement (wages paid, raw materials, rent, depreciation of capital).

💡 Economic Cost (Explicit + Implicit Opportunity Cost)

Includes both explicit outlays AND implicit opportunity costs—such as the salary a small-business owner could have earned working elsewhere, or interest forgone on their own invested capital.

Section 06 • Exam Preparation

ISRT Past Exam Question (PYQ) Vault (2019–2025)

(a) Definitions & Graph: Define FC (rent/lease at q=0), VC (materials/labor), and Opportunity Cost (best forgone alternative). Plot U-shaped MC cutting U-shaped AVC and AC at their minimums, with AFC declining hyperbolically.

(b) Toy Firm (Paper, Glue, Craftsmen): Least-cost rule: $MP_{\text{paper}}/P_{\text{paper}} = MP_{\text{glue}}/P_{\text{glue}} = MP_{\text{craft}}/P_{\text{craft}}$. If $P_{\text{glue}}$ falls, $MP_{\text{glue}}/P_{\text{glue}}$ rises above the others → by the Substitution Rule, the firm uses more glue (lowering $MP_{\text{glue}}$ via diminishing returns) and less paper/craftsmen until equality is restored.

(c) Optimal Combination & Budget Decrease: Firm reaches tangency where its isocost line touches the highest attainable isoquant ($MP_1/MP_2 = P_1/P_2$). If budget decreases (prices fixed), the isocost line makes a parallel shift inward toward the origin, moving tangency to a lower isoquant (lower output and fewer inputs).

(d) Calculus Proof for $TC = Q^3 - 21Q^2 + 500Q$: $MC = 3Q^2 - 42Q + 500$; $AC = Q^2 - 21Q + 500$. Setting $MC = AC \Rightarrow 2Q^2 - 21Q = 0 \Rightarrow \mathbf{Q = 10.5}$ (for $Q > 0$). To verify minimum AC: $d(AC)/dQ = 2Q - 21 = 0 \Rightarrow Q = 10.5$, and $d^2(AC)/dQ^2 = +2 > 0$ (strict minimum, $\text{min } AC = \mathbf{389.75}$).

(a–b) U-Shape & AC/MC: Initial increasing returns ($MP \uparrow$) pull MC down; subsequent diminishing returns ($MP \downarrow$) push MC up. Rising MC pulls AC down while $MC < AC$, cuts AC at minimum AC, then pulls AC up.

(c) Isoquant/Isocost ($P_K=3, P_L=2, B=24$): Isocost equation: $2L + 3K = 24$, with intercepts $L = 12$ and $K = 8$, slope $= -P_L/P_K = -2/3$. The optimal combination is the tangency point with the $q = 100$ isoquant (e.g., $L=6, K=4$ for symmetric tech) because any other point on $q=100$ lies on a higher cost line ($TC > 24$).

(d) Profit Maximization: $\pi = TR - TC = (1400Q - 6Q^2) - (1500 + 80Q) = -6Q^2 + 1320Q - 1500$. Set $d\pi/dQ = -12Q + 1320 = 0 \Rightarrow \mathbf{Q^* = 110}$ ($d^2\pi/dQ^2 = -12 < 0$, maximum). At $Q = 110$: $MR = 1400 - 12(110) = \mathbf{80}$ and $MC = \mathbf{80}$, confirming $MC = MR$!

Functions: $MC = 3Q^2 - 42Q + 500$, $AC = Q^2 - 21Q + 500$.

Intersection ($MC = AC$) & Min AC: Occurs at $Q = 10.5$, where $\text{min } AC = 389.75$.

Minimum MC: Set $d(MC)/dQ = 6Q - 42 = 0 \Rightarrow \mathbf{Q = 7}$ ($d^2MC/dQ^2 = 6 > 0$). Minimum $MC = 3(49) - 42(7) + 500 = \mathbf{353}$. (Verify both peaks live in the Section 2 Cubic Cost Lab!)

Least-Cost Rule: $MP_{\text{flour}}/P_{\text{flour}} = MP_{\text{butter}}/P_{\text{butter}} = MP_{\text{egg}}/P_{\text{egg}} = MP_{\text{sugar}}/P_{\text{sugar}}$. Pair with the standard U-shaped AC curve pierced at its bottom by the rising MC curve.
