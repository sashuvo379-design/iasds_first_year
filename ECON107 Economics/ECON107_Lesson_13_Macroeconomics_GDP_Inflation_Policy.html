Section 01 • Measuring Macroeconomic Performance

Macroeconomic Objectives, GDP Approaches & GDP vs. NDP vs. GNP

📈 1. Output (High Level & Growth)

Measured by: Real GDP (output at constant prices, correcting for inflation) and Real GDP growth rate relative to Potential GDP.

👷 2. High Employment

Measured by: Unemployment Rate = (Unemployed ÷ Labor Force) × 100%. Goal is low involuntary unemployment near the NAIRU.

💵 3. Price Stability

Measured by: CPI (Consumer Price Index) and the GDP Deflator. Goal is low, stable, predictable inflation (1–3%/yr), avoiding hyperinflation and deflation traps.

Two Equivalent Ways to Measure GDP (Final 2024 Q7b)

🛒 1. Flow-of-Product (Expenditure) Approach

Sums total annual spending on final goods and services: GDP = C + I + G + X.

  • C (Consumption): Household spending on durables, nondurables, and services.
  • I (Gross Private Domestic Investment): Business machinery, buildings, residential housing, and inventory changes.
  • G (Government Purchases): Roads, schools, defense, judge salaries. (Excludes transfer payments!)
  • X (Net Exports): Exports minus Imports (Ex − Im).
  • Avoiding Double Counting: Count only final goods; exclude intermediate goods (flour, raw steel, chips).

💼 2. Earnings / Income (Cost) Approach

Sums all factor incomes paid out by firms to produce society's output: Wages + Rent + Interest + Corporate Profits + Indirect Taxes + Depreciation.

  • Why Both Loops Match: Profit is the automatic residual ensuring Total Earnings = Total Final Sales.
  • Avoiding Double Counting: At each production stage, count only Value Added (= Firm's Sales − Cost of intermediate materials bought from other firms).

Distinguishing GDP, NDP & GNP + Transfer Payments (Final 2025 Q7a & Q1(4))

AggregateFormula / DefinitionConcrete Example
GDP
Gross Domestic Product		 Market value of all final goods & services produced within a country's borders in a year. Garments produced inside a Dhaka factory owned by a foreign investor count in Bangladesh's GDP.
NDP
Net Domestic Product		 NDP = GDP − Depreciation (uses net investment, subtracting capital wear-and-tear). If GDP is $450B and machinery/factory wear-and-tear (depreciation) is $45B, NDP = $405B.
GNP
Gross National Product		 GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (output by a nation's citizens/residents anywhere). Earnings of a Bangladeshi engineer working in Dubai count in Bangladesh's GNP (not Bangladesh's GDP).
Why Are Government Transfer Payments Excluded from GDP? (Final 2025 Q1(4))
Transfer payments (old-age pensions, unemployment benefits, student stipends, social safety-net allowances) are cash payments made by government to individuals without receiving any current good or service in return. They redistribute existing purchasing power rather than creating new production—including them in $G$ would overstate GDP!

Section 03 • Household Spending & Aggregate Demand (Ch. 21–22)

Consumption Function, MPC, Saving & The Multiplier

Key Determinants of Consumption (Final 2025 Q7c)
1. Current Disposable Income (DI): The primary driver of household spending.
2. Permanent Income & Life-Cycle Expectations: Consumers smooth spending based on long-term income.
3. Wealth Effect: Higher stock/housing wealth boosts consumption.
4. Interest Rates & Credit Conditions.
MPC, MPS & The Keynesian Multiplier
MPC = ΔC / ΔDI (extra consumption per $1 of extra disposable income; equals the slope of the consumption function).
Since every dollar of DI is either consumed or saved (DI = C + S): MPC + MPS = 1.
Expenditure Multiplier: k = 1 / (1 − MPC) = 1 / MPS.

Section 05 • Exam Preparation

ISRT Past Exam Question (PYQ) Vault (2017–2025)

(a) GDP vs. NDP vs. GNP: GDP measures final domestic output within borders; NDP = GDP − Depreciation; GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (give factory vs. migrant worker examples).

(b) Value Added: Use the 4-stage bread table ($0.24 wheat + $0.09 milling + $0.27 baking + $0.30 retail = $0.90 = Final Bread Price), avoiding the $2.07 double-counted sum of gross sales.

(c) Consumption & MPC: Driven by disposable income, wealth, permanent income expectations, and credit. $MPC = \Delta C / \Delta DI$ is the exact slope of the consumption function $C = C_0 + MPC \cdot DI$.

(d) Policies to Boost Investment: Monetary Policy: Central bank lowers interest rates ($r \downarrow$) via open-market bond purchases, making business borrowing and capital projects cheaper. Fiscal Policy: Government lowers corporate income taxes, offers investment tax credits, and reduces structural budget deficits to increase national saving and prevent "crowding out" of private investment.

(a) Output (Real GDP), Employment (Unemployment Rate), Price Stability (CPI / Inflation Rate). (b) Product approach sums $C+I+G+X$ on final goods only; Income approach sums wages, rent, interest, profits using value added at each stage. (c) Define $C, I, G, X$. (d) Fiscal policy (government $G, T$) vs. Monetary policy (central bank money supply & interest rates)—essential for dampening business cycles, controlling inflation, and promoting long-run growth.

(i) Effect on GDP: Measured GDP increases because paid market work is recorded in national accounts, whereas unpaid housework was previously excluded.

(ii) Overstatement & Augmented Accounts: Measured GDP overstates the true rise in total output because society lost unpaid home production (cooking, childcare) when those hours shifted to market jobs. Augmented national accounts assign an imputed market value to unpaid home production, so switching from home to market work shows only the net difference in value.

(iii) Gardener Paradox: When a person pays a gardener a salary, that market transaction is counted in GDP. If they marry the gardener and the same gardening continues unpaid as household work, it disappears from market transactions—so measured GDP goes down even though the exact same gardening output is still being produced!

(a) 2017 Inflation Rate: $\pi_{2017} = 100 \times (375 - 330) / 330 = 100 \times (45 / 330) = \mathbf{13.64\%}$.

(b) C, Y, S & MPC: Disposable income splits into consumption and saving ($Y = C + S \implies S = Y - C$). If income rises by $1,000 and consumption rises by $800 (saving by $200), $MPC = 800/1000 = 0.80$ and $MPS = 0.20$.

(c) Role of Investment Policy: Investment plays a dual role: (1) in the short run, volatile investment spending is a major driver of Aggregate Demand and business cycles (amplified by the multiplier); (2) in the long run, capital accumulation expands Potential GDP and raises productivity and living standards.
