Section 01 • Market Structures (Table 9-1)

Patterns & Sources of Imperfect Competition

Acid Test for Imperfect Competition (ECON107_L11 / Fig 9-1)
Imperfect competition prevails whenever an individual seller can affect the market price of its output (a price-maker). Graphically, the acid test is a downward-sloping firm demand curve: to sell more units, the firm must lower its price.
Market Structure Sellers & Product Type Control Over Price Examples (Global & Bangladesh)
Perfect Competition Many producers; identical products None (Price-taker) Agricultural markets (rice/wheat), financial exchanges
Monopolistic Competition Many producers; differentiated products Some (quality, brand, location) Retail trade, pizza shops, personal computers, clothing brands
Oligopoly Few producers (2 to ~15); identical or differentiated Some (strategic rivalry / price wars) Steel, cars, domestic airlines in Bangladesh (Biman, US-Bangla, Novoair)
Monopoly Single seller; no close substitutes Considerable (often regulated) Electricity / water utilities (DESCO/DPDC), patented drugs, Windows OS

Two Major Sources of Market Imperfections (Final 2025 Q6a)

📉 1. Costs & Economies of Scale

When an industry has large fixed costs and decreasing average costs over a huge output range (natural monopoly, e.g., electricity grids or water pipes), a single large firm can produce far more cheaply and undersell smaller rivals, who cannot survive.

🚧 2. Barriers to Entry

  • Legal Restrictions: Patents (20-yr drug exclusivity), copyrights, government franchises/licenses, import tariffs & quotas.
  • High Cost of Entry: Billions required to develop commercial aircraft or semiconductor fabs.
  • Advertising & Product Differentiation: Heavy branding builds customer loyalty (soft drinks, smartphones).
Section 02 • Revenue Mechanics (Table 9-4 & Fig 9-3)

Marginal Revenue (MR), Twice-as-Steep Slope & Elasticity

Why MR < P for a Monopolist (and Why Linear MR Has Twice the Slope!)
To sell 1 additional unit without price discrimination, a monopolist must cut the price on ALL previous units too. Therefore: MR = P − (lost revenue on previous units) < P.
Algebraic Proof: For linear demand P = a − b·q, Total Revenue is TR = P·q = a·q − b·q². Differentiating gives MR = d(TR)/dq = a − 2b·q—same vertical intercept $a$, but exactly twice as steep a slope (−2b vs. −b)!

Section 05 • Exam Preparation

ISRT Past Exam Question (PYQ) Vault (2019–2025)

Q1(b) Bangladesh Markets: (1) Electricity distribution (e.g., DESCO, DPDC, BPDB) is a Natural / Franchise Monopoly—a single utility serves each region with no close substitute because duplicating transmission grids is prohibitively costly (huge economies of scale) and restricted by government franchise. (2) Domestic airline tickets (Biman Bangladesh, US-Bangla, Novoair, Air Astra) form an Oligopoly—a few sellers dominate the market, face high entry barriers (aircraft cost, airport slots), and each airline's fare cuts directly affect rivals.

Q6(a) Sources & Features: Two main sources: (1) Cost conditions / economies of scale (large firms undersell small ones); (2) Barriers to entry (legal patents/franchises, high entry cost, advertising/product differentiation). Key structures: Monopoly (1 seller, unique good), Oligopoly (few sellers, strategic rivalry), Monopolistic Competition (many sellers, differentiated goods).

(1) Comparison: Compare along number of sellers, product differentiation, price control, entry barriers, and long-run profits (Table 9-1).

(3) Monopoly MR = MC Graph: Plot downward-sloping demand $d$, twice-as-steep $MR$ below $d$, and U-shaped $MC, AC$. Profit maximizes at $q^*$ where $MR = MC$ (point $E$); read monopoly price $P^*$ vertically up on the demand curve $d$ (point $G$). Moving left of $q^*$ forfeits units where $MR > MC$; moving right adds units where $MC > MR$.

(4) Two-Product Solution: Invert demands: $P_1 = 100 - 2Q_1$, $P_2 = 76 - Q_2$. Total Profit $\pi = (100Q_1 - 2Q_1^2) + (76Q_2 - Q_2^2) - (3Q_1^2 + 2Q_1Q_2 + 2Q_2^2 + 55) = 100Q_1 + 76Q_2 - 5Q_1^2 - 2Q_1Q_2 - 3Q_2^2 - 55$. Set $\partial\pi/\partial Q_1 = 100 - 10Q_1 - 2Q_2 = 0$ and $\partial\pi/\partial Q_2 = 76 - 2Q_1 - 6Q_2 = 0$. Solving simultaneously yields $Q_1^* = 8, Q_2^* = 10$, and prices $P_1^* = \$84, P_2^* = \$66$ (Max Profit $\pi^* = \mathbf{\$725}$).

Fixed/sunk costs do not change with output ($d(FC)/dq = 0$), so they do not affect $MC$ or $MR$. Under the Marginal Principle, optimal decisions depend strictly on marginal future revenues ($MR$) and marginal future costs ($MC$). Notice in the Section 3 Dashboard: dragging the Fixed Cost slider changes total profit $\pi$, but leaves optimal $q_M$ and $P_M$ completely unchanged!
