Section 01 • Foundations (Index Number.pdf, Ch. 13)

Definition, Construction Problems & Consumer Price Index (CPI)

What Is an Index Number? ("The Economic Barometer")
An index number is a statistical device for measuring relative changes in a variable or group of related variables (prices, quantities, or values) over time or space, expressed as a percentage relative to a chosen base period (= 100).

🏷️ Types of Index Numbers

  • Price Index ($P_{01}$): Measures general price changes (CPI, Wholesale Price Index).
  • Quantity Index ($Q_{01}$): Measures physical volume changes (Industrial Production Index).
  • Value Index ($V_{01}$): Measures total value change ($\sum p_1 q_1 / \sum p_0 q_0 \times 100$).
  • Unweighted vs. Weighted: Weighted indices assign realistic importance ($q_0, q_1,$ or value weights) to each commodity.

🛠️ 7 Construction Problems

  1. Defining the exact purpose.
  2. Selecting a normal base period (free of war/crisis).
  3. Selecting representative commodities.
  4. Collecting accurate price/quantity quotations.
  5. Choosing an appropriate average (AM vs. GM).
  6. Assigning proper weights.
  7. Selecting the best formula.

🛒 Consumer Price Index (CPI)

Measures the average change over time in the cost of living (a fixed market basket of food, clothing, rent, fuel, transport) for a target class of consumers.

  • Uses: Adjusting wages/pensions (dearness allowance), measuring inflation, deflating nominal income, guiding monetary policy.
Section 02 • Core Formulas & Biases

The 5 Weighted Price Index Formulas & Upward/Downward Biases

Index MethodPrice Index Formula (× 100)Weights UsedBias & Practical Notes
1. Laspeyres ($L$) (Σ p₁ q₀) / (Σ p₀ q₀) × 100 Base-year quantities ($q_0$) Upward Bias Overstates inflation (ignores consumer substitution away from pricier goods). Easy/cheap to update annually!
2. Paasche ($P$) (Σ p₁ q₁) / (Σ p₀ q₁) × 100 Current-year quantities ($q_1$) Downward Bias Understates inflation (consumers already substituted toward cheaper items). Costly—needs fresh $q_1$ every year.
3. Dorbish–Bowley ($DB$) ½ (L + P) Arithmetic Mean of $L$ & $P$ Lies halfway between $L$ and $P$; always $\ge$ Fisher's Index (since $AM \ge GM$).
4. Fisher's Ideal ($F$) √(L × P) Geometric Mean of $L$ & $P$ Ideal (No Bias) Cancels upward bias of $L$ & downward bias of $P$; satisfies both TRT & FRT!
5. Marshall–Edgeworth ($ME$) [Σ p₁(q₀+q₁)] / [Σ p₀(q₀+q₁)] × 100 Average qty $(q_0+q_1)/2$ Satisfies Time Reversal Test, but fails Factor Reversal Test (so Fisher is superior).

Section 06 • Exam Preparation

ISRT Past Exam Question (PYQ) Vault (2019–2025)

(a) CPI & Accuracy Tests: CPI measures relative change in the cost of living for a representative consumer basket. Accuracy is tested via the Time Reversal Test ($P_{01} \times P_{10} = 1$), Factor Reversal Test ($P_{01} \times Q_{01} = V_{01}$), Circular Test, and Unit Test.

(b) Base Shifting & Deflating: Base shifting converts an index to a newer base year ($\text{New} = \text{Old}/\text{Old}_{\text{new base}} \times 100$). Deflating divides nominal money income by the CPI ($\times 100$) to measure real purchasing power.

(c) Careful with Base Year! Here 2025 is the base year ($p_0, q_0$) and 2020 is the current year ($p_1, q_1$)! Sums: $\sum p_0 q_0 = 8,346$; $\sum p_1 q_0 = 7,408$; $\sum p_0 q_1 = 9,868$; $\sum p_1 q_1 = 8,749$.
• Laspeyres: $(7408 / 8346) \times 100 = \mathbf{88.76}$ (prices in 2020 were 11.24% lower than in 2025).
• Paasche: $(8749 / 9868) \times 100 = \mathbf{88.66}$ (prices in 2020 were 11.34% lower than in 2025).

(1) Sums & Fisher Index (using P₁ = 120 in 2023): $\sum p_0 q_0 = 6200$, $\sum p_1 q_0 = 7310$ ($L = 117.90$), $\sum p_0 q_1 = 7250$, $\sum p_1 q_1 = 8525$ ($P = 117.59$). Fisher's Ideal Index $F = \sqrt{117.90 \times 117.59} = \mathbf{117.74}$. Time Reversal: $F_{01} \times F_{10} = 1.17745 \times 0.84930 = \mathbf{1.0000}$ ✓.

(2) Avoiding Biases: Laspeyres uses fixed base quantities (ignoring substitution → upward bias); Paasche uses current quantities (over-weighting cheaper items → downward bias). Taking their geometric mean balances both opposing biases.

(3) Misrecording $Q_2 = 35$ (instead of 25): Increases Good 2's weight in Paasche (Good 2 had a sharp +25% price rise from 20→25), pulling Paasche UP from 117.59 to 117.78 and biasing Fisher upward from 117.74 to 117.84! (Verify live in the Section 3 Lab!)

(4) Improving Real-Time Accuracy: Use barcode/scanner transaction data, web scraping of e-commerce prices, automated chaining/frequent weight updates, and hedonic quality adjustments.
