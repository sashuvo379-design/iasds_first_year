Section 01 • Finding the Balance

Market Equilibrium & The Market-Clearing Price

Definition of Market Equilibrium (ECON107_L4 / Samuelson & Nordhaus)
A market equilibrium occurs at the price where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied (the intersection of the D and S curves). At this market-clearing price, all buy and sell orders are filled, there are no shortages or surpluses, and there is no tendency for the price to rise or fall.

📦 Price Too High (P > P*)

QS > QD → Surplus (Excess Supply). Unsold inventories pile up; sellers cut prices to clear stock, pushing price downward toward P*.

⚖️ Equilibrium (P = P*)

QD = QS → Market Clears. Consumers willingly buy the exact amount firms willingly sell. Price pressure is neutral.

🔥 Price Too Low (P < P*)

QD > QS → Shortage (Excess Demand). Long queues form; frustrated buyers bid prices upward toward P*.

Section 05 • Conceptual Depth & Data Science Link

Rationing by Prices & Algorithmic Dynamic Pricing

🎯 Rationing by Prices & The Accounting Identity Trap

In an accounting sense, recorded purchases always equal recorded sales—even during a shortage! Economic equilibrium specifically means desired quantity demanded equals desired quantity supplied. Competitive prices automatically ration scarce goods to those willing to pay the market-clearing price without queues or black markets.

🤖 Dynamic Pricing Algorithms (Final 2024 Q3(c))

Platforms like Uber (surge pricing) and Amazon adjust prices in real time using machine learning and live demand/supply telemetry. This challenges traditional static equilibrium models by: (1) replacing slow price adjustment with continuous micro-equilibria; (2) introducing personalized/time-varying price discrimination rather than a single market price; and (3) reacting to predictive demand spikes before shortages even form.

ISRT Past Exam Question (PYQ) Vault (2017–2025)

(i) Computer Market: Computer chips are a key input. Lower chip costs reduce production costs, shifting the computer supply curve rightward (S → S'). Result: equilibrium price of computers falls (P ↓) and equilibrium quantity rises (Q ↑).

(ii) Typewriter Market: Computers and typewriters are close substitutes. As computers become much cheaper, consumers switch away from typewriters, shifting the typewriter demand curve leftward (D → D'). Result: equilibrium price falls (P ↓) and equilibrium quantity falls (Q ↓).

(a) Equilibrium & Why Price Won't Settle Higher: Equilibrium comes at the intersection of D and S where QD = QS. If price were set above P*, QS > QD, creating an unsold surplus. To clear perishable unsold curd, sellers cut prices, pushing market price back down to P*.

(b) Shocks: (i) Milk price rises: Milk is a key raw input for curd → production cost rises → Supply shifts left (S → S') → P* ↑, Q* ↓. (ii) Ice-cream price falls: Ice-cream is a substitute dessert → buyers switch from curd to cheaper ice-cream → Demand shifts left (D → D') → P* ↓, Q* ↓.

(i) Rise in Beef Burger Price: Beef burgers are a substitute for chicken burgers. Demand for chicken burgers shifts rightward (D → D') → P* ↑, Q* ↑.

(ii) Rise in Employee Wages: Labor is a production input. Higher wages raise marginal costs, shifting supply of chicken burgers leftward (S → S') → P* ↑, Q* ↓.

Original Equilibrium: At P = $6, QD = 20 and QS = 20 → P* = $6, Q* = 20 pizzas.

If Demand Triples at Each Price: New QD' at P=$10 is 0; at P=$8 is 30 (which equals QS = 30!). Thus the new equilibrium is P* = $8, Q* = 30 pizzas. (You can verify this in the Algebraic Solver above!)

If Price Is Initially Set at $4 (original market): At P = $4, QD = 30 while QS = 10 → Shortage of 20 pizzas. Excess demand puts upward pressure on price until it rises to $6.

Case 1 — Immigration Alone (Fig 3-10a): An influx of workers shifts the labor supply curve rightward (S → S') while labor demand stays fixed. Result: equilibrium employment rises (L ↑) and equilibrium wage falls (W ↓).

Case 2 — Immigration to Growing Cities (Fig 3-10b): Immigrants typically move to booming cities where labor demand is also shifting rightward (D → D'). Because both S and D shift right simultaneously, employment rises sharply (L ↑↑), while the net effect on wages is ambiguous (often staying nearly unchanged or even rising slightly).

(b) Fishing Net Price Increases: Higher input cost shifts supply of fish leftward (S → S') → P* ↑, Q* ↓.

(c) Average Income Increases: Fish is a normal good; higher income shifts demand rightward (D → D') → P* ↑, Q* ↑.
