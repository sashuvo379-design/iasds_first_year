Section 01 • The Benchmark Model

The Cobb-Douglas Production Function

General vs. Strict Cobb-Douglas Form
General Form: Q = A K^α L^β (where $A > 0$ is total factor productivity/technology, $\alpha > 0, \beta > 0$).
Strict (Linearly Homogeneous) Form: Q = A K^α L^(1 − α) where $\alpha + \beta = 1$ (Constant Returns to Scale).

📐 Marginal Products & Diminishing Returns

  • MP_K = ∂Q/∂K = α(Q/K)
  • MP_L = ∂Q/∂L = β(Q/L)
  • Second derivatives: $\partial^2 Q/\partial K^2 = \alpha(\alpha-1)Q/K^2 < 0$ whenever $\alpha < 1$ (diminishing returns to $K$, and similarly for $L$ when $\beta < 1$).

⚖️ Output Elasticities & Scale

  • $\alpha$ = Capital elasticity of output (%ΔQ per 1% ΔK) and Capital's share of income under CRS.
  • $\beta$ = Labor elasticity of output (%ΔQ per 1% ΔL).
  • Returns to Scale: $\alpha+\beta > 1$ (IRS), $=1$ (CRS), $<1$ (DRS).

🔄 Elasticity of Substitution & Growth

  • Limitation: Cobb-Douglas forces the elasticity of substitution between $K$ and $L$ to be identically $\sigma = 1$.
  • Growth Accounting (Ch. 26):
    g(Q) = g(A) + α·g(K) + (1−α)·g(L)
Section 02 • Generalizing Technology (Final 2017, 2019, 2023 & 2025)

The CES Production Function & Isoquant Morph Lab

Constant Elasticity of Substitution (CES) Function (Arrow, Chenery, Minhas & Solow)
Q = A [ δ K^(−ρ) + (1 − δ) L^(−ρ) ]^(−1/ρ)
• A > 0: Efficiency / technology parameter  |  • 0 < δ < 1: Distribution parameter  |  • ρ > −1: Substitution parameter.
Elasticity of Substitution: σ = 1 / (1 + ρ) — constant along an isoquant, but free to take ANY positive value (unlike Cobb-Douglas which forces $\sigma = 1$)!

Advantages of CES over Cobb-Douglas (Final 2019 Q6b):
  • Allows $\sigma$ to be estimated from data rather than forced equal to 1.
  • Nests linear ($\sigma=\infty$), Cobb-Douglas ($\sigma=1$), and Leontief ($\sigma=0$) as special limiting cases.
Section 03 • Product Exhaustion (Final 2019 Q6a,c)

Homogeneous Functions & Euler's Theorem

Homogeneous Function of Degree r
A function $Z = f(K, L)$ is homogeneous of degree $r$ if multiplying every input by $t > 0$ scales output by $t^r$:
f(tK, tL) = t^r · f(K, L)
When $r = 1$, it is linearly homogeneous (Constant Returns to Scale).
Euler's Theorem & Product Exhaustion
If $Q = f(K, L)$ is homogeneous of degree $r$, then:
K · (∂Q/∂K) + L · (∂Q/∂L) = r · Q
For $r = 1$ (CRS) under perfect competition (where factors are paid their marginal products), $K \cdot MP_K + L \cdot MP_L = Q$—total output is exactly exhausted with zero surplus or deficit!

Section 04 • Step-by-Step Math Solver (Final 2017, 2024 & Mihad's Notes)

Constrained Optimization: Lagrange Multiplier & Least-Cost Solver

Maximize Q = A · Kα · Lβ subject to budget PK K + PL L = B using the Lagrangian ℒ(K, L, λ) = A K^α L^β + λ(B − P_K K − P_L L). Notice how setting $\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial K = 0$ and $\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial L = 0$ immediately yields the Least-Cost Rule: $MP_K / P_K = MP_L / P_L = \lambda$!

Section 05 • High-Scoring Derivations

Step-by-Step Formal Proofs for the Final Exam

Step 1 (Take Natural Log): Start with $Q = A[\delta K^{-\rho} + (1-\delta)L^{-\rho}]^{-1/\rho}$. Divide by $A$ and take $\ln$:
ln(Q/A) = − ln[δ K^(−ρ) + (1−δ) L^(−ρ)] / ρ
Step 2 (Check Limit as ρ → 0): As $\rho \to 0$, $K^0 = L^0 = 1$, so the numerator $\to -\ln[\delta + 1 - \delta] = -\ln(1) = 0$ and denominator $\to 0$ (indeterminate $0/0$ form).
Step 3 (Apply L'Hôpital's Rule — differentiate top & bottom w.r.t. ρ):
Recall $\frac{d}{d\rho}(K^{-\rho}) = -K^{-\rho} \ln K$. Denominator derivative is $1$. Numerator derivative is:
− [ −δ K^(−ρ) ln K − (1−δ) L^(−ρ) ln L ] / [ δ K^(−ρ) + (1−δ) L^(−ρ) ]
Step 4 (Evaluate at ρ = 0): Substituting $\rho = 0$ (so $K^0 = L^0 = 1$):
lim_{ρ→0} ln(Q/A) = [ δ ln K + (1−δ) ln L ] / 1 = ln( K^δ · L^(1−δ) )
Step 5 (Exponentiate): Q = A · K^δ · L^(1−δ) — which is the exact linearly homogeneous Cobb-Douglas production function! (Q.E.D.)
Step 1 (Scale both inputs by t > 0): Q(tK, tL) = A [ δ (tK)^(−ρ) + (1−δ) (tL)^(−ρ) ]^(−1/ρ)
Step 2 (Factor out t^(−ρ) inside brackets): Q(tK, tL) = A [ t^(−ρ) · ( δ K^(−ρ) + (1−δ) L^(−ρ) ) ]^(−1/ρ)
Step 3 (Apply outer exponent −1/ρ): Q(tK, tL) = ( t^(−ρ) )^(−1/ρ) · A [ δ K^(−ρ) + (1−δ) L^(−ρ) ]^(−1/ρ)
Step 4 (Simplify): Since $(- \rho)(-1/\rho) = +1$, we get Q(tK, tL) = t¹ · Q(K, L) = t · Q(K, L). Thus CES is homogeneous of degree 1 ⇒ Constant Returns to Scale! (Q.E.D.)
Step 1 (First Partial Derivative — Marginal Product of K): Differentiating $Q = A[\delta K^{-\rho} + (1-\delta)L^{-\rho}]^{-1/\rho}$ w.r.t. $K$ using the chain rule gives:
MP_K = ∂Q/∂K = δ · A^(−ρ) · (Q / K)^(1 + ρ) > 0
Step 2 (Second Partial Derivative w.r.t. K):
∂²Q/∂K² = δ · A^(−ρ) · (1 + ρ) · (Q/K)^ρ · [ (K·MP_K − Q) / K² ]
Step 3 (Sign Analysis for K > 0, L > 0, ρ > −1): By Euler's theorem for CRS ($r=1$), $K \cdot MP_K + L \cdot MP_L = Q \implies K \cdot MP_K - Q = -L \cdot MP_L < 0$. Since $\delta > 0, A^{-\rho} > 0,$ and $1 + \rho > 0$, we obtain $\partial^2 Q / \partial K^2 < 0$ (and symmetrically $\partial^2 Q / \partial L^2 < 0$) for all positive inputs! Thus CES always exhibits diminishing returns to each input. (Q.E.D.)
Section 06 • Exam Preparation

ISRT Past Exam Question (PYQ) Vault (2017–2025)

Generalization: CES has elasticity of substitution $\sigma = 1/(1+\rho)$. As $\rho \to 0$ ($\sigma \to 1$), L'Hôpital's rule yields Cobb-Douglas $Q = A K^\delta L^{1-\delta}$. As $\rho \to \infty$ ($\sigma \to 0$), the smaller of $K$ or $L$ dominates the bracket, yielding Leontief fixed-proportions $Q = A \min(K, L)$.

CRS Proof: Show $Q(tK, tL) = t^{(-\rho)(-1/\rho)} Q(K, L) = t Q(K, L)$ (Section 5 Proof 2).

ML-Driven Production: Violates static technology (models self-improve with data), rival inputs (datasets/weights are non-rival with near-zero replication cost), and diminishing returns (data network effects can yield persistent increasing returns).

(a) Optimization: Using Lagrange/Least-Cost: $MP_K/4 = MP_L/5 \Rightarrow (0.4/0.6)(L/K) = 4/5 \Rightarrow (2/3)(L/K) = 4/5 \Rightarrow L = 1.2 K$. Budget: $4K + 5(1.2K) = 110 \Rightarrow 10K = 110 \Rightarrow \mathbf{K^* = 11}$, $\mathbf{L^* = 13.2}$, $Q^* = 11^{0.4} \cdot 13.2^{0.6} \approx \mathbf{12.27}$.

(b) & (c): Yes, $\partial^2 Q/\partial K^2 < 0$ for all $K, L > 0$ and $\rho > -1$ (Section 5 Proof 3). Prove C-D as $\rho \to 0$ via logs and L'Hôpital's rule (Section 5 Proof 1).

(a) Euler's Theorem: Assuming a linearly homogeneous production function ($r=1$) and competitive factor markets ($P_K = MP_K, P_L = MP_L$), Euler's theorem gives $K \cdot MP_K + L \cdot MP_L = Q$ (total product is exactly exhausted by factor payments).

(c)(i) $Z = X^2 - XY + 2Y^2$: $f(tX, tY) = t^2(X^2 - XY + 2Y^2) = t^2 Z \Rightarrow$ homogeneous of degree $r = 2$. Partials: $Z_X = 2X - Y, Z_Y = -X + 4Y$. Check: $X(2X-Y) + Y(-X+4Y) = 2X^2 - 2XY + 4Y^2 = \mathbf{2Z}$ ✓.

(c)(ii) $Z = A^\alpha B^\beta$: $f(tA, tB) = t^{\alpha+\beta} Z \Rightarrow$ homogeneous of degree $r = \alpha + \beta$. Partials: $Z_A = \alpha A^{\alpha-1} B^\beta, Z_B = \beta A^\alpha B^{\beta-1}$. Check: $A \cdot Z_A + B \cdot Z_B = \alpha A^\alpha B^\beta + \beta A^\alpha B^\beta = \mathbf{(\alpha + \beta)Z}$ ✓.

Distinction: Strict C-D ($Q = A K^\alpha L^{1-\alpha}$) restricts elasticity of substitution to $\sigma = 1$, whereas CES ($Q = A[\delta K^{-\rho} + (1-\delta)L^{-\rho}]^{-1/\rho}$) allows any constant $\sigma = 1/(1+\rho) > 0$. Strict C-D CRS Proof: $Q(tK, tL) = A(tK)^\alpha (tL)^{1-\alpha} = t^{\alpha+1-\alpha} A K^\alpha L^{1-\alpha} = t Q(K, L)$. Combine with Section 5 Proof 2 for CES.
