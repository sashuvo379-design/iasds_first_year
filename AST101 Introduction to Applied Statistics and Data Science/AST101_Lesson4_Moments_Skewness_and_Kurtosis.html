Section 01

Raw Moments, Arbitrary-Point Moments & Central Moments

Moments are statistical parameters that capture the complete quantitative profile of a distribution using its first four powers: 1st (location/mean), 2nd (spread/variance), 3rd (asymmetry/skewness), and 4th (tail-weight & peakedness/kurtosis). Two distributions can share the same mean and variance yet differ dramatically in skewness and kurtosis.

1. Raw Moments (About Origin 0)
μ'r = (1/n) Σ xir
Grouped: (1/N) Σ fi xir

Notice μ'1 = x̄ (Arithmetic Mean). Always raise to the power r first, sum, then divide by n.

2. Moments About Arbitrary Value a
μ'r(a) = (1/n) Σ (xi − a)r
Mean: x̄ = a + μ'1(a)

When a = 0, these reduce to raw moments about the origin. Extremely common in ISRT exam calculations!

3. Central Moments (About Mean x̄)
μr = (1/n) Σ (xi − x̄)r
μ1 = 0  |  μ2 = σ² (Variance)

Independent of origin, but dependent on scale: if d = (x − a)/c, then μr(x) = cr μr(d).

Master Conversion Formulas: Raw (or About a) → Central Moments Final 2025 Q6(b)

Because central moments are shift-invariant, the exact same four formulas convert moments about any arbitrary point a (including a = 0) into central moments:

μ1 = 0
μ2 = μ'2 − (μ'1)²
μ3 = μ'3 − 3 μ'2 μ'1 + 2 (μ'1)³
μ4 = μ'4 − 4 μ'3 μ'1 + 6 μ'2 (μ'1)² − 3 (μ'1)⁴
Section 02

Grouping Error & Sheppard's Correction

When computing moments from a grouped frequency distribution, we assume every observation in a class is concentrated at its class midpoint. This simplification introduces a small grouping error. For continuous, unimodal distributions with equal class width c that taper smoothly to zero at both tails, Sheppard's Correction adjusts the even central moments:

μ1(corrected) = μ1(uncorrected) = 0   (No correction needed)
μ2(corrected) = μ2(uncorrected) − c² / 12
μ3(corrected) = μ3(uncorrected)   (No correction needed)
μ4(corrected) = μ4(uncorrected) − (c² / 2) · μ2(uncorrected) + (7 c⁴) / 240
Exam Reminder In the μ4 formula, always plug in μ2(uncorrected)—not the already-corrected μ2! Only even moments (μ2 and μ4) receive Sheppard's correction.
Section 03

Measures of Skewness & Kurtosis

Skewness measures the degree and direction of asymmetry (departure from symmetry). Kurtosis measures the degree of peakedness or flatness relative to the standard normal curve. All coefficients below are pure, unit-free numbers independent of origin and scale.

Four Measures of Skewness

MeasureFormulaRangeInterpretation
1. Pearson's Coefficient SkP = (Mean − Mode)/σ = 3(Mean − Me)/σ Usually (−3, 3) 0 = symmetric; >0 right-skewed; <0 left-skewed
2. Bowley's (Quartile) SkB = (Q3 + Q1 − 2Me) / (Q3 − Q1) [−1, +1] Useful for open-ended tables; outlier-resistant
3. Kelley's (Decile) SkK = (D1 + D9 − 2Me) / (D9 − D1) [−1, +1] Uses middle 80% of data (P10 to P90)
4. Method of Moments β1 = μ3² / μ2³  |  γ1 = μ3 / μ23/2 β1 ≥ 0; γ1 ∈ ℝ Use γ1 for sign! β1 loses direction because μ3 is squared

Two Measures of Kurtosis

1. Moment Coefficient (Pearson's β2 & γ2)
β2 = μ4 / μ2²   |   γ2 = β2 − 3
  • Mesokurtic (Normal): β2 = 3 or γ2 = 0
  • Leptokurtic (Slender / Peaked): β2 > 3 or γ2 > 0
  • Platykurtic (Flat-topped / Wide): β2 < 3 or γ2 < 0
2. Percentile Coefficient of Kurtosis (K)
K = [½ (Q3 − Q1)] / (P90 − P10)   (0 < K ≤ 0.5)
  • Mesokurtic: K ≈ 0.263
  • Leptokurtic: K > 0.263
  • Platykurtic: K < 0.263

Section 06 • High-Yield Proofs

Proofs & Derivations Vault (4 Core Theorems)

Theorem 1 Final 2025 Q6(b) Derive μ₂, μ₃, and μ₄ in terms of Raw Moments μ'r
Write xi − x̄ = (xi − a) − (x̄ − a) = (xi − a) − μ'1, where μ'r = (1/n) Σ(xi − a)r.
For r = 2: μ2 = (1/n) Σ[(xi − a) − μ'1]² = (1/n) Σ[(xi − a)² − 2μ'1(xi − a) + (μ'1)²]
= μ'2 − 2μ'1(μ'1) + (μ'1)² = μ'2 − (μ'1)².
For r = 3: μ3 = (1/n) Σ[(xi − a)³ − 3(xi − a)²μ'1 + 3(xi − a)(μ'1)² − (μ'1)³]
= μ'3 − 3μ'2μ'1 + 3(μ'1)³ − (μ'1)³ = μ'3 − 3μ'2μ'1 + 2(μ'1)³.
For r = 4: Expanding binomial (A − B)⁴ = A⁴ − 4A³B + 6A²B² − 4AB³ + B⁴ and averaging:
μ4 = μ'4 − 4μ'3μ'1 + 6μ'2(μ'1)² − 4(μ'1)⁴ + (μ'1)⁴ = μ'4 − 4μ'3μ'1 + 6μ'2(μ'1)² − 3(μ'1)⁴. [Proved]
Theorem 2 1st Incourse 2025 Q6 Central Moments Depend on Scale but NOT on Origin: μr(x) = cr μr(d)
Let di = (xi − a) / c ⇒ xi = a + c·di, and x̄ = a + c·d̄.
Subtracting gives xi − x̄ = (a + c·di) − (a + c·d̄) = c(di − d̄). Notice the origin shift 'a' cancels completely!
μr(x) = (1/n) Σ(xi − x̄)r = (1/n) Σ[c(di − d̄)]r = cr · [(1/n) Σ(di − d̄)r] = cr · μr(d).
Because 'a' is absent and 'c' remains, central moments are independent of origin and dependent on scale. [Proved]
Theorem 3 Final 2017 Q8(c) Skewness (β₁) and Kurtosis (β₂) are Independent of BOTH Origin & Scale
From Theorem 2, for di = (xi − a)/c: μ2(x) = c²μ2(d), μ3(x) = c³μ3(d), μ4(x) = c⁴μ4(d).
For β₁: β1(x) = μ3²(x) / μ2³(x) = [c³μ3(d)]² / [c²μ2(d)]³ = [c⁶ μ3²(d)] / [c⁶ μ2³(d)] = β1(d).
For β₂: β2(x) = μ4(x) / μ2²(x) = [c⁴μ4(d)] / [c²μ2(d)]² = [c⁴ μ4(d)] / [c⁴ μ2²(d)] = β2(d).
Both origin (a) and scale (c) cancel out completely. Hence β₁ and β₂ are invariant under linear transformations. [Proved]
Theorem 4 Universal Inequality: For ANY Distribution, β₂ ≥ β₁ + 1
For any real constants a, b, c, a sum of squares is always non-negative:
(1/n) Σ [ a(xi − x̄)² + b(xi − x̄) + c ]² ≥ 0
Expand the square and take the average term by term (using μ1 = 0):
a²μ4 + b²μ2 + c² + 2ab·μ3 + 2bc·(0) + 2ca·μ2 ≥ 0 --- (i)
Substitute a = 1, b = −μ3 / μ2, and c = −μ2 into (i):
μ4 + (μ3² / μ2²)·μ2 + μ2² + 2(1)(−μ3 / μ2)μ3 + 2(−μ2)(1)μ2 ≥ 0
Simplify: μ4 + (μ3² / μ2) + μ2² − 2(μ3² / μ2) − 2μ2² ≥ 0 ⇒ μ4 − (μ3² / μ2) − μ2² ≥ 0
Divide every term by μ2² (> 0): (μ4 / μ2²) − (μ3² / μ2³) − 1 ≥ 0 ⇒ β2 − β1 − 1 ≥ 0 ⇒ β2 ≥ β1 + 1. [Proved]

2nd Incourse 2025 • Q2(a),(c) [4 Marks] Shape Commenting & Finding All 4 Central Moments

Part (a): Comment on the shape for:

  • (i) γ1 = 0, γ2 = 0: Symmetrical and Mesokurtic (normal-like peakedness).
  • (ii) γ1 = −2.11, γ2 = −2.01: Strongly negatively (left) skewed and platykurtic (Note: mathematically impossible for a real distribution because β2 = 0.99 < β1 + 1 = 5.45, though students should still interpret the signs!).
  • (iii) γ1 = 0.99, γ2 = 0.5: Positively (right) skewed and leptokurtic (more peaked than normal).

Part (c): Given μ3² = 5625, γ1 = −0.6, γ2 = 0.3. Find μ1, μ2, μ3, μ4.

  • μ1 = 0 (always true for central moments).
  • Since γ1 = −0.6 < 0, μ3 must be negative: μ3 = −√5625 = −75.
  • From γ1 = μ3 / μ23/2: −0.6 = −75 / μ23/2 ⇒ μ23/2 = 125 ⇒ μ2 = 1252/3 = 25.
  • From γ2 = β2 − 3 = 0.3 ⇒ β2 = 3.3: μ4 = β2 · μ2² = 3.3 × 25² = 3.3 × 625 = 2062.5.
2nd Incourse 2024 • Q3 [4.5 Marks] Doubled Salary: New CV & Chebyshev 70% Interval

Question: Monthly salary (Tk lac) has Mean = 2, μ3 = 2, γ1 = 2, γ2 = 5. Salary is doubled. (a) Find CV of the new salary. (b) What limit contains at least 70% of employees' new salary?

Worked Solution:

  • Step 1 (Find old σ): γ1 = μ3 / μ23/2 ⇒ 2 = 2 / μ23/2 ⇒ μ23/2 = 1 ⇒ μ2 = 1 ⇒ σ = 1 Tk lac.
  • Step 2 (Double salary: Y = 2X): New Mean = 2 × 2 = 4 Tk lac; New SD = 2 × 1 = 2 Tk lac.
  • (a) New CV: CV = (2 / 4) × 100% = 50% (scaling by a positive constant leaves CV unchanged!).
  • (b) At least 70% (Chebyshev — distribution is skewed, NOT normal!):
    1 − 1/k² = 0.70 ⇒ 1/k² = 0.30 ⇒ k = √(10/3) ≈ 1.8257.
    Interval: 4 ± 1.8257(2) = 4 ± 3.6515 = (0.35, 7.65) Tk lac.
Final 2017 • Q5 [12 Marks] Male vs. Female Income Shape, Under/Over-Reporting & USD Conversion

Given ('000 Tk): Male (n=600): x̄=30, s=4, m3=50, m4=255. Female (n=400): x̄=25, s=3, m3=−35, m4=222.

  • (a) Variability (CV): Male CV = (4/30)×100 = 13.33%; Female CV = (3/25)×100 = 12.00% → Male income is slightly more variable.
  • (b) Shape:
    • Male: γ1 = 50 / 4³ = +0.781 (positively skewed); β2 = 255 / 4⁴ = 0.996 < 3 (platykurtic).
    • Female: γ1 = −35 / 3³ = −1.296 (negatively skewed); β2 = 222 / 3⁴ = 2.741 < 3 (platykurtic).
  • (c) Correcting reporting bias (Male +5, Female −2 in '000 Tk): Real Male Mean = 35, SD = 4; Real Female Mean = 23, SD = 3.
  • (d) Effect on shape: Adding/subtracting constants is a pure origin shift. Because β1, γ1, and β2 are independent of origin, the shape of each group's distribution does not change at all.
  • (e) Converting to USD (÷ 80): Both Mean and SD are divided by 80 (scale change), while CV, skewness, and kurtosis remain unchanged.
