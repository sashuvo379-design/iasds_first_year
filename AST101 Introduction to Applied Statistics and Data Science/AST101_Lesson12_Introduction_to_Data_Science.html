Section 01

What is Data Science? DIKW Pyramid & Related Disciplines

Data Science is an interdisciplinary field combining statistical methods & mathematics, computer science (Python, R, SQL, distributed systems), domain knowledge, and visualization & communication to extract non-obvious, useful patterns from data and support evidence-based decisions.

Section 03

Big Data (The Five Vs), Data Taxonomies & Domain Data

Big Data refers to datasets whose size, arrival speed, diversity, or complexity make them difficult to store, manage, or process with conventional tools (like an ordinary spreadsheet). It is commonly described by the Five Vs:

1. Volume

Massive scale of records (e.g., online marketplace transactions).

2. Velocity

Rapid speed of generation & real-time checks (e.g., payment fraud).

3. Variety

Diverse formats: tables, images, audio, doctors' notes, logs.

4. Veracity

Reliability, noise & uncertainty (e.g., GPS signal interference).

5. Value

Actionable usefulness—forecasting demand, cutting costs, saving lives.

Key Data Distinctions (L12 Slides 18–21)

Structure
  • Structured: Fixed tabular schema (SQL tables, sales records).
  • Semi-structured: Identifiable fields/tags, no rigid table (JSON, XML, server logs).
  • Unstructured: No fixed schema (free text, photos, audio, video).
Raw vs. Derived
  • Raw: Direct measurements (height, weight, room temp).
  • Derived: Computed from raw data (average salary, variance, BMI = weight / height²—often far more predictive than height or weight alone!).
Captured vs. Exhaust
  • Captured: Collected intentionally for analysis (surveys, experiments).
  • Exhaust: By-product of another process (clickstreams, device type, timestamps, metadata).

Section 05 • ISO 8000 & Accountability

Data Quality (8 Dimensions & Strategies) & Data Governance

Data Quality is the degree to which data is suitable for its intended purpose ("fitness for use", standardized in the ISO 8000 series)—it is much broader than accuracy alone! Its 8 Dimensions are: Accuracy, Completeness, Consistency, Uniqueness, Validity, Timeliness, Integrity, and Representativeness.

Critical Rule (L12 Slide 32): Never Delete Outliers Blindly! Suppose a temperature sensor (normal range 0–100°C) logs 25.1, 25.3, 250.7, 25.4°C. That 250.7°C reading could be a sensor malfunction, a unit-conversion error, OR a genuine exceptional event (like a fire!). Always investigate the source and context before correcting or excluding.

Data Governance & Accountability Roles

Data Governance is the system of policies, responsibilities, decision authority, standards, and controls governing how data is managed and used across its entire life cycle (covering Ownership, Privacy/Confidentiality, Access Control/Security, Legal & Ethical Compliance, Metadata, Lineage/Provenance, Retention/Disposal, and Sharing/Reuse). It is not just storage or security—it is the accountability framework around them.

Governance RoleCore Responsibility
Data OwnerAccountable for high-level decisions about a dataset and its permitted uses
Data StewardMaintains variable definitions, data quality practices, and appropriate day-to-day handling
Data CustodianManages technical storage, access permissions, backups, and IT infrastructure
Data Analyst / ScientistUses data strictly within approved purposes and documents all transformations/models
Privacy / Compliance OfficerAdvises on privacy laws, consent, and regulatory requirements
Data SubjectThe individual person whose personal data is collected and processed

Final 2024 • Q2(a)–(f) [12 Marks] Complete Data Science Exam Question & Model Answers
  • (a) What is data science? Mention two common applications. [2]: Data science is an interdisciplinary field combining statistics, computing, mathematics, domain expertise, and communication to extract actionable insights from data. Applications: (1) Spam filtering / movie & product recommendation systems; (2) Credit-card fraud detection or medical disease risk prediction.
  • (b) Differentiate structured vs. unstructured data with one example each. [2]: Structured data follow a fixed tabular schema of rows and columns (e.g., a relational database of student exam scores or sales records). Unstructured data have no predefined tabular schema (e.g., free-text customer reviews, medical X-ray images, or audio/video files).
  • (c) Define Big Data & mention one distinguishing characteristic. [2]: Big Data refers to datasets whose scale, speed, or diversity exceed the capacity of traditional storage and analysis tools. Distinguishing characteristic: Volume (massive size), Velocity (high-speed real-time streams), or Variety (mixing tables, text, images, and sensor logs).
  • (d) What is data preprocessing and why is it important? [2]: Data preprocessing (data preparation/wrangling) is cleaning, deduplicating, handling missing values, standardizing units/formats, and transforming raw data into an analysis-ready dataset. It is critical because raw data often contain errors, inconsistencies, and bias ("garbage in, garbage out").
  • (e) Name two tools/languages commonly used in data science. [2]: Python and R (also SQL, Hadoop, Excel, Tableau, SPSS, SAS).
  • (f) Role of data visualization & one common technique. [2]: Visualization converts complex numbers into graphical form so patterns, trends, distributions, and outliers pop out immediately and can be communicated clearly to stakeholders. Common technique: Scatter plot, histogram, box plot, or interactive dashboard.
1st Incourse 2023 • Q1(a),(b) [2.5 Marks] Role of Statistics & Alignment of Statistics, Applied Statistics & Data Science
  • (a) Role of Statistics in Data Science: Statistics provides the mathematical backbone for reasoning about variation and uncertainty—covering sampling design, EDA, estimation, hypothesis testing, regression modeling, and evaluating whether detected patterns are genuine or due to chance.
  • (b) Are Statistics, Applied Statistics, and Data Science aligned? How? Yes! Theoretical Statistics develops the mathematical laws and theorems; Applied Statistics applies those methods to solve real-world problems in medicine, economics, and science; and Data Science expands applied statistics by integrating computer science, big-data engineering, machine learning, and data governance to extract end-to-end insights from massive, often unstructured datasets.
