Section 01

Attributes & Contingency Tables (r × c)

An attribute is a qualitative characteristic or quality marked by its presence or absence (or category membership) in members of a population—such as gender, smoking status, blood group, or passing an exam.

A Contingency Table (also called a cross-classification table or crosstab) organizes frequency counts of a sample cross-classified by two categorical variables: variable X with r rows and variable Y with c columns, yielding r × c cells. The simplest contingency table is a 2 × 2 table for two dichotomous (binary) attributes.

Symmetric vs. Asymmetric Measures
  • Symmetric Measures: Give the exact same value if the roles of X and Y are swapped (it does not matter which is independent or dependent). Examples: Yule's Q, Yule's Y, Odds Ratio (OR), Pearson's r, χ², Φ, V.
  • Asymmetric Measures: Change value when X and Y are reversed because direction matters. Examples: Somers' D, Regression slope β̂, Correlation ratio η.
Section 02 • Final 2024 & Final 2025 Q8(a)

Association vs. Correlation

In statistics, an association is any relationship between two measured quantities that renders them statistically dependent. Rather than saying two categorical variables are "dependent," statisticians conventionally say they are associated.

DimensionAssociationCorrelation
ScopeBroad: any statistical dependence between two variablesSpecial case of association: specifically linear (or monotonic) relationship
Variable TypesMainly used for qualitative / categorical variables in contingency tablesUsed for quantitative (interval/ratio) or ranked variables
Key MeasuresYule's Q, Yule's Y, Odds Ratio (OR), χ², Φ, Pearson's C, Tschuprow's T, Cramér's VKarl Pearson's r, Spearman's rs
DirectionMany measures (χ², C, T, V) measure strength only (no sign)Always reports both direction (+/−) and strength in [−1, +1]
Golden Exam Sentence (Mihad's Notes) "Every correlation is an association, but not every association is a correlation."
Bonus (Final 2020 Q8a): A contingency table cross-classifies counts of categorical attributes, whereas a correlation table (bivariate frequency distribution) cross-classifies counts of two quantitative variables grouped into numerical class intervals.
Section 03

Measures for 2×2 Tables: Yule's Q, Yule's Y & Odds Ratio (OR)

1. Yule's Q (Association)
Q = (ad − bc) / (ad + bc)
  • Range: [−1, +1] (Symmetric).
  • Q = 0 ⇒ no association.
  • Q = ±1 ⇒ complete/strongest association.
  • Note: Equals +1 or −1 whenever any single cell is 0!
2. Yule's Y (Colligation)
Y = (√(ad) − √(bc)) / (√(ad) + √(bc))
  • Also called Coefficient of Colligation.
  • Range: [−1, +1] (Symmetric).
  • Same sign as Q, but always smaller in magnitude (|Y| ≤ |Q|) for moderate associations.
3. Odds Ratio (OR)
Odds(E) = P(E) / [1 − P(E)]
OR = (a/b) / (c/d) = (a/c) / (b/d) = ad / bc
  • Range: [0, ∞) (Symmetric: OR(A|B) = OR(B|A)).
  • OR = 1 ⇒ independence (no association).
  • OR > 1 ⇒ positive association; OR < 1 ⇒ negative.
How to Interpret OR < 1 on the Exam (L10 Slide 28) For Film Attendance vs. High Grades (a=30, b=70, c=70, d=30), OR = (30×30)/(70×70) = 0.184:
1) "The odds of getting a high grade are 0.184 times as large for film attendees compared to non-attendees."
2) Equivalently (taking the reciprocal 1 / 0.184 = 5.44): "The odds of getting a high grade are 5.44 times higher for students who did NOT attend campus films compared to those who did."

Proofs & Identities Vault (OR, Yule's Q & Yule's Y)

Identity 1 Final 2022 Q8(b) & Final 2025 Q8(b) [3 Marks] Prove Q = (OR − 1) / (OR + 1)
Start from Yule's Q = (ad − bc) / (ad + bc), where OR = ad / bc (assuming bc ≠ 0).
Divide both numerator and denominator by bc:
Q = [ (ad − bc) / bc ] / [ (ad + bc) / bc ] = [ (ad / bc) − 1 ] / [ (ad / bc) + 1 ] = (OR − 1) / (OR + 1). [Proved]
Identity 2 Final 2024 Q8(c) Prove Y = (√OR − 1) / (√OR + 1)
Start from Yule's Y = (√(ad) − √(bc)) / (√(ad) + √(bc)).
Divide both numerator and denominator by √(bc):
Y = [ √(ad/bc) − 1 ] / [ √(ad/bc) + 1 ] = (√OR − 1) / (√OR + 1). [Proved]
Identity 3 Link Between Y, Q, and OR (L10 Slide 30): Y = (1/Q) − 2√OR / (OR − 1)
Multiply numerator and denominator of Y = (√OR − 1) / (√OR + 1) by (√OR − 1):
Y = (√OR − 1)² / [ (√OR + 1)(√OR − 1) ] = (OR − 2√OR + 1) / (OR − 1)
Split into two fractions: Y = (OR + 1) / (OR − 1) − 2√OR / (OR − 1).
Since Q = (OR − 1) / (OR + 1), its reciprocal is 1/Q = (OR + 1) / (OR − 1).
Therefore: Y = (1 / Q) − [ 2√OR / (OR − 1) ]. [Proved]

Final 2025 • Q8(c) [7 Marks] Preparatory Workshop vs. Admission Test Success

Problem: Among 120 students who attended a workshop, 84 passed (so 36 failed). Among 80 who did not attend, 36 passed (so 44 failed). Total n = 200.

  • Table: a = 84, b = 36, c = 36, d = 44 (Row totals: 120, 80; Col totals: 120, 80).
  • Odds Ratio: OR = (84 × 44) / (36 × 36) = 3696 / 1296 = 2.852. (The odds of passing are 2.85 times higher for workshop attendees than for non-attendees.)
  • Yule's Q: Q = (3696 − 1296) / (3696 + 1296) = 2400 / 4992 = +0.4808 (verify: (2.852 − 1)/(2.852 + 1) = 0.4808).
  • Chi-Square (χ²): χ² = 200(2400)² / (120 × 80 × 120 × 80) = 12.50.
  • Comment: Positive, moderate association between attending the workshop and passing the admission test.
  • Part (2) — Why does this NOT prove causation? [2 Marks]: This is an observational study, not a randomized controlled experiment. Students self-selected into the workshop; confounding variables (such as higher motivation, stronger prior academic background, or more study hours) could explain both workshop attendance and higher pass rates. Association does not imply causation!
Final 2024 • Q8(c) [6 Marks] Tuition Fee Increase vs. Gender (N = 600)

Problem: 600 students surveyed; 200 support tuition increase (400 against). 50 out of 200 female students were against (so 150 female support). Find the remaining cells, compute Yule's Y and OR, and verify Y = (√OR − 1)/(√OR + 1).

  • Reconstructing the 2×2 Table by subtraction from totals:
    Female Support a = 200 − 50 = 150; Female Against b = 50 (Row 1 = 200).
    Male Support c = 200 − 150 = 50; Male Against d = 400 − 50 = 350 (Row 2 = 400).
  • Odds Ratio: OR = (150 × 350) / (50 × 50) = 52,500 / 2,500 = 21.0.
  • Yule's Y (Colligation): Y = (√52500 − √2500) / (√52500 + √2500) = (229.129 − 50) / (229.129 + 50) = +0.6417.
  • Verification: (√21 − 1) / (√21 + 1) = (4.5826 − 1) / (4.5826 + 1) = 3.5826 / 5.5826 = 0.6417 ✓
  • Comment: Strong positive association between gender (being female) and supporting the tuition decision (female students had 21 times higher odds of supporting than male students).
