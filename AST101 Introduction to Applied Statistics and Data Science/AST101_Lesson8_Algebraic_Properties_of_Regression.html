Algebraic Properties of Regression & Correlation

Connect correlation and regression algebraically—master origin & scale transformations of β̂ and α̂, see why the two regression lines intersect at (x̄, ȳ) with r = ±√(β̂xy·β̂yx), and prove R² = r² in simple linear regression.

Section 01

The 5 Core Algebraic Properties at a Glance

Using Dr. Masud's notation from L7_Algebraic_properties.pdf: let β̂xy denote the slope when regressing Y on X (Y = α̂xy + β̂xy X), and β̂yx denote the slope when regressing X on Y (X = α̂yx + β̂yx Y).

PropertyMathematical StatementExam Takeaway
1. Two-Point Exact Fit For n = 2 distinct points, r = ±1 and β̂ = (y₁−y₂)/(x₁−x₂) Zero residual error (SSE = 0); exact straight line through both points.
2. Origin & Scale Effect For U = a₁ + b₁X, V = a₂ + b₂Y:
β̂uv = (b₂ / b₁) · β̂xy		 β̂ is independent of origin, dependent on scale (cancels if b₁ = b₂).
α̂ depends on both origin and scale (even if b₁ = b₂!).
3. Asymmetry & Same Sign β̂xy ≠ β̂yx, but sign(β̂xy) = sign(β̂yx) = sign(rxy) Both lines cross at (x̄, ȳ). They coincide only when |r| = 1!
4. Geometric Mean Link rxy² = β̂xy · β̂yx  ⇒  rxy = ±√(β̂xy · β̂yx) r is the geometric mean of the two slopes (taking their common sign). Also β̂xy = r (sy/sx).
5. R² Equals r² (SLR) R² = SSR / SST = rxy² In Simple Linear Regression, the proportion of Y-variation explained equals Pearson's r².
Classic Exam Sanity Check Can a dataset have β̂xy = +1.5 and β̂yx = −0.4? NO! They must share the same sign.
Can a dataset have β̂xy = 1.8 and β̂yx = 0.7? NO! Because their product is 1.8 × 0.7 = 1.26 > 1, which violates r² = β̂xy · β̂yx ≤ 1!

Complete Proofs & Derivations Vault (5 Core Theorems)

Theorem 1 Effect of Origin & Scale on β̂ (Independent of Origin, Dependent on Scale)
Let ui = a1 + b1xi and vi = a2 + b2yi (b1, b2 ≠ 0). Then ū = a1 + b1x̄ and v̄ = a2 + b2ȳ.
Deviations: ui − ū = b1(xi − x̄) and vi − v̄ = b2(yi − ȳ). (Origin shifts a1, a2 vanish!)
β̂uv = Σ(ui − ū)(vi − v̄) / Σ(ui − ū)² = [ b1b2 Σ(xi − x̄)(yi − ȳ) ] / [ b1² Σ(xi − x̄)² ] = (b2 / b1) · β̂xy.
Thus β̂ does not depend on origin (a1, a2), but depends on the ratio of scales (b2/b1). [Proved]
Theorem 2 Effect of Origin & Scale on α̂ (Depends on BOTH Origin and Scale)
α̂uv = v̄ − β̂uv ū = (a2 + b2ȳ) − (b2/b1)β̂xy (a1 + b1x̄)
= a2 + b2(ȳ − β̂xyx̄) − (b2/b1)a1β̂xy = a2 − (b2/b1)a1β̂xy + b2α̂xy.
Even when b1 = b2 = b (same scale change on both variables), α̂uv = a2 − a1β̂xy + b·α̂xy, which still depends on both origin and scale! [Proved]
Theorem 3 Final 2019 Q7(c),(d) Asymmetry (β̂xy ≠ β̂yx) & Identical Sign with r
Correlation: rxy = Sxy / √(Sxx·Syy) = Syx / √(Syy·Sxx) = ryx ⇒ Symmetric.
Regression slopes: β̂xy = Sxy / Sxx and β̂yx = Sxy / Syy. Because Sxx ≠ Syy in general, β̂xy ≠ β̂yx (Asymmetric).
Same Sign: In all three formulas (rxy, β̂xy, β̂yx), the denominators √(SxxSyy), Sxx, and Syy are strictly positive sums of squares. Therefore, the sign of all three quantities is determined solely by the shared numerator Sxy = Σ(xi − x̄)(yi − ȳ)! [Proved]
Theorem 4 Correlation Coefficient is the Geometric Mean of the Two Regression Slopes: rxy = ±√(β̂xy · β̂yx)
β̂xy × β̂yx = [ Σ(xi − x̄)(yi − ȳ) / Σ(xi − x̄)² ] × [ Σ(xi − x̄)(yi − ȳ) / Σ(yi − ȳ)² ]
= [ Σ(xi − x̄)(yi − ȳ) ]² / [ Σ(xi − x̄)² · Σ(yi − ȳ)² ] = rxy².
Taking the square root (preserving the common sign of β̂xy and β̂yx): rxy = ±√(β̂xy · β̂yx). [Proved]
Theorem 5 For Simple Linear Regression, R² = r²
R² = SSR / SST = Σ(ŷi − ȳ)² / Σ(yi − ȳ)².
Since ŷi = α̂ + β̂xi and ȳ = α̂ + β̂x̄, we have ŷi − ȳ = β̂(xi − x̄).
Substitute into SSR: Σ(ŷi − ȳ)² = β̂² Σ(xi − x̄)² = [ Sxy / Sxx ]² · Sxx = Sxy² / Sxx.
Divide by SST = Syy: R² = Sxy² / (Sxx · Syy) = r². [Proved]

Final 2022 • Q7(b) [4 Marks] Finding the Reverse Regression Line (X on Y) from R² = 0.87

Question: Fitted regression of Y on X is Ŷi = −12 + 1.74 xi. For the regression of X on Y (X̂i = α̂yx + β̂yx yi), the fitted value of X at y = 8 is X̂ = 7, and R² = 0.87. Find α̂yx and β̂yx.

Worked Solution:

  • Step 1 (Use R² = r² = β̂xy · β̂yx):
    1.74 × β̂yx = 0.87 ⇒ β̂yx = 0.87 / 1.74 = 0.50.
  • Step 2 (Substitute y = 8, X̂ = 7):
    7 = α̂yx + 0.50(8) = α̂yx + 4 ⇒ α̂yx = 3.0.
  • Answer: X̂i = 3 + 0.50 yi.
Final 2024 • Q1(e)–(f) Income Doubled (U = 2X) & Expenditure Tripled (V = 3Y)

Given: r = 0.79 and Expenditure (Y) = 5654 + 0.88 × Income (X). In 2025, U = 2X and V = 3Y.

  • (e) Variation in Expenditure explained: R² = r² = (0.79)² = 0.6241 → 62.41% of the variation in expenditure is explained by income.
  • (f1) New correlation coefficient: Since both scale factors (+2 and +3) are positive, ruv = +0.79 (unchanged).
  • (f2) Slope of new Expenditure (V) on Income (U): β̂uv = (b₂/b₁)β̂xy = (3/2)(0.88) = 1.32; Intercept α̂uv = 3(5654) = 16,962 ⇒ V̂ = 16962 + 1.32 U.
  • (f3) Slope of new Income (U) on Expenditure (V): First, original reverse slope β̂yx = r² / β̂xy = 0.6241 / 0.88 = 0.7092. Scaling U = 2X and V = 3Y gives β̂vu = (2/3)(0.7092) = 0.4728 (note: the intercept of the reverse line additionally requires x̄ or ȳ).
