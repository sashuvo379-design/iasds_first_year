Section 01

Sum of Squares Decomposition & Coefficient of Determination (R²)

Once we fit an OLS line ŷi = α̂ + β̂xi, we need to quantify how much of the total spread in Y our model actually explains. The total variation around the mean splits cleanly into two orthogonal components:

Total Sum of Squares (SST)
SST = Σ (yi − ȳ)²

Total variation of observed Y values around their overall mean ȳ.

Regression SS (SSR)
SSR = Σ (ŷi − ȳ)²

Explained variation attributable to the linear influence of X on Y.

Error SS (SSE)
SSE = Σ (yi − ŷi)²

Unexplained variation due to noise and factors other than X (minimized by OLS).

Fundamental Identity:   SST = SSR + SSE
Coefficient of Determination:   R² = SSR / SST = 1 − (SSE / SST) = β̂² [ Σx² − (Σx)²/n ] / [ Σy² − (Σy)²/n ]
Exam Interpretation of R² (Final 2019 Q1d) 0 ≤ R² ≤ 1. Multiply by 100: "100·R²% of the total variation in the dependent variable Y is explained by the fitted regression model (variation in X)."
Slide Cholesterol Example: SSR = 70.77, SST = 194.4 ⇒ R² = 70.77 / 194.4 = 0.364 → 36.4% of the variation in cholesterol level is explained by weight (leaving 63.6%, or SSE = 123.63, unexplained).
Section 02

Standard Error of the Regression (SER) & Slope Uncertainty

While R² gives a unit-free percentage, it does not tell you how far off your predictions are in the original units of Y. For prediction precision, we use two standard error measures:

1. Standard Error of the Regression (s / SER)
s = √[ SSE / (n − 2) ] = √[ (1 / (n − 2)) Σ (yi − ŷi)² ]
  • Measures the average distance that observed values fall from the regression line (in the original units of Y).
  • Smaller s is better (tighter predictions). Approximately 95% of observations fall within ±2s of the fitted line.
  • Cholesterol Example (n = 10): s = √(123.63 / 8) = 3.93.
2. Standard Error of the Slope SE(β̂)
SE(β̂) = s / √[ Σ (xi − x̄)² ]
  • Quantifies the uncertainty in the estimated slope β̂.
  • Decreases when residual noise s is small OR when the predictor X is spread widely (large Sxx).
  • Cholesterol Example: SE(β̂) = 3.93 / √(138.5) = 0.334.
Section 03

Extrapolation & Why It Can Produce Absurd Predictions

Extrapolation is using a fitted regression equation to predict Y for values of the independent variable X outside the range of the observed X-data. Even a model with an impressive R² = 0.914 can fail dramatically outside its observed domain because linearity may break down (or the slope may flatten).

Section 04 • 2nd Incourse '24 & Finals '22, '24

Outliers, Influential Observations & Leverage (hii)

1. Outlier (Vertical)

An atypical, abnormal observation that does not follow the regular pattern of the data (large residual in the Y-direction). Because OLS squares residuals, outliers can pull the line and distort r.

2. Influential Observation

An observation is influential if removing it markedly changes the regression results (slope β̂, intercept α̂, or R²).

3. High-Leverage Point (Horizontal)

An extreme point in the X-direction (far from x̄). Leverage depends ONLY on X (never on Y!) and acts like a long lever arm that can tilt the regression line.

Leverage of Observation i (SLR):   hii = (1 / n) + [ (xi − x̄)² / Σ(xj − x̄)² ]
High-Leverage Cutoff Rule:   hii ≥ 2 / n   (Note: 1/n ≤ hii ≤ 1, and Σ hii = 2 always!)
Key Distinction: High Leverage ≠ Automatically Influential! A point far out in X has high leverage (high potential to tilt the line). If it lies right along the existing trend line, removing it barely changes β̂ (high leverage, low influence). If it deviates vertically from the trend, it becomes strongly influential!

Section 06 • High-Yield Proofs

Proofs & Derivations Vault

Proof 1 Sum of Squares Decomposition: SST = SSE + SSR
Start with SST = Σ(yi − ȳ)². Add and subtract ŷi inside the parenthesis:
SST = Σ [ (yi − ŷi) + (ŷi − ȳ) ]² = Σ(yi − ŷi)² + Σ(ŷi − ȳ)² + 2 Σ(yi − ŷi)(ŷi − ȳ).
Examine the cross-product term (writing ei = yi − ŷi and ŷi = α̂ + β̂xi):
2 Σ ei(ŷi − ȳ) = 2 Σ eiŷi − 2ȳ Σ ei = 2 Σ ei(α̂ + β̂xi) − 2ȳ Σ ei
= 2α̂ Σ ei + 2β̂ Σ eixi − 2ȳ Σ ei.
From the OLS normal equations (Lesson 7), Σ ei = 0 and Σ eixi = 0! Therefore the entire cross-product term equals 0.
Hence: Σ(yi − ȳ)² = Σ(yi − ŷi)² + Σ(ŷi − ȳ)² ⇒ SST = SSE + SSR. [Proved]
Proof 2 Computational Formula for R² in Terms of β̂, Sxx, and Syy
Since ŷi = α̂ + β̂xi and ȳ = α̂ + β̂x̄, we have ŷi − ȳ = β̂(xi − x̄).
Thus SSR = Σ(ŷi − ȳ)² = β̂² Σ(xi − x̄)² = β̂² [ Σxi² − (Σxi)² / n ].
Dividing by SST = Σ(yi − ȳ)² = Σyi² − (Σyi)² / n gives:
R² = SSR / SST = { β̂² [ Σxi² − (Σxi)²/n ] } / [ Σyi² − (Σyi)²/n ]. [Proved]

Final 2024 Q7(c), Final 2022 Q7(c) & 2nd Incourse 2024 Outliers, Influential Points & Leverage [3 Marks]

Question: In regression analysis, what is (i) an outlier and (ii) an influential observation? How do you quantify and interpret the leverage of a point?

Model Solution:

  • (i) Outlier: An abnormal, infrequent observation that deviates far from the general pattern of the data (large vertical residual in Y), which can distort the OLS line because squared errors weight extreme residuals heavily.
  • (ii) Influential Observation: A data point whose removal markedly alters the fitted regression equation (slope β̂, intercept α̂, or R²).
  • Leverage (hii): Measures how far an observation's predictor value xi lies from the center x̄ (depends only on X):
    hii = (1/n) + (xi − x̄)² / Σ(xj − x̄)².
    Interpretation: Ranges from 1/n to 1. Traditionally, a point with hii ≥ 2/n is flagged as a high-leverage point—meaning it has strong potential to tilt the regression line and should be inspected for influence.
Final 2019 • Q1(d) [1 Mark] Coefficient of Determination & Its Importance

Model Solution: The Coefficient of Determination (R² = SSR / SST) is the proportion of total variation in the dependent variable Y that is explained by the variation in the independent variable X via the fitted regression model. It is important because it provides a unit-free, standardized goodness-of-fit score between 0 and 1 showing how effectively the regression model explains and predicts Y.
