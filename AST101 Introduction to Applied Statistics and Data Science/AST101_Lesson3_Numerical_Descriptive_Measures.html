Section 01

Measures of Central Tendency (Location)

Measures of central tendency identify the center or most typical value of a distribution. Choosing the right average depends on the scale of measurement, presence of outliers, and mathematical context (rates vs. growth vs. counts).

Arithmetic Mean (AM)
Ungrouped: x̄ = (Σx) / n
Grouped: x̄ = (Σm·f) / n
Weighted: x̄w = (Σw·x) / Σw

Uses every value, unique, least sampling variability. Weakness: Sensitive to outliers; cannot be computed for open-ended classes.

Geometric Mean (GM)
GM = (x1·x2···xn)1/n
log(GM) = (1/n) Σ log(xi)

Best for compounding growth rates, percentages, ratios, and index numbers (e.g., GDP growth, inflation). Requires all values > 0.

Harmonic Mean (HM)
HM = n / Σ(1 / xi)
Grouped: HM = N / Σ(fi / mi)

Best for averaging rates/speeds over equal distances (e.g., Dhaka to Sylhet in 4 equal quarters — 1st Incourse 2025 Q9).

Median (Q2)
Grouped:
Me = L + [(N/2 − fc) / fm] × c

Middle value of ordered data. Resistant to outliers and usable on open-ended distributions & ordinal data.

Mode
Grouped (Equal Width):
Mo = Lmod + [Δ1 / (Δ1 + Δ2)] × c

Most frequent value. Here Δ1 = f1 − f0 and Δ2 = f1 − f2. Valid even for nominal data; may not be unique.

k% Trimmed Mean

Sort data, drop k% from EACH end (total dropped = 2k%), and average the remaining middle values. Eliminates tail outliers while keeping more information than the median.

Mean, Median & Mode Order by Distribution Shape (1st Incourse 2025 Q7)
  • Symmetric (Bell-shaped): Mean = Median = Mode
  • Positively (Right) Skewed: Mode < Median < Mean (right-tail outliers pull the Mean up)
  • Negatively (Left) Skewed: Mean < Median < Mode (left-tail outliers pull the Mean down)
Section 02

Measures of Dispersion, CV, Empirical Rule & Chebyshev

Two datasets can share the exact same mean (e.g., 40 years) yet have completely different spreads. Dispersion measures quantify how tightly or widely observations cluster around the center.

MeasureFormulaOrigin & Scale Effect
RangeMax − MinIndependent of origin; depends on scale
Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD)(1/n) Σ |xi − x̄|Independent of origin; depends on scale
Population Variance (σ²)Σ(xi − μ)² / NIndependent of origin; multiplied by c²
Sample Variance (s²)Σ(xi − x̄)² / (n − 1) = [Σx² − (Σx)²/n] / (n − 1)Independent of origin; multiplied by c²
Standard Deviation (s or σ)√(Variance)Independent of origin; multiplied by |c|
Coefficient of Variation (CV)CV = (s / x̄) × 100%Unit-free relative spread (compares different units/means)
Exam Favorite: Linear Transformation Rule (1st Incourse 2025 Q3) If every score x is transformed via y = a + b·x (e.g., dividing marks by 4 and adding 4 bonus points: y = 4 + 0.25x):
New Mean: ȳ = a + b·x̄ (affected by BOTH origin a and scale b)  |  New SD: sy = |b| · sx (affected ONLY by scale b, NOT by adding/subtracting a!).

Chebyshev's Theorem vs. Empirical Rule (Interactive Calculator)

Chebyshev's Theorem (ANY Shape)

For any distribution (skewed, bimodal, unknown) and any k > 1, at least (1 − 1/k²) of observations lie within μ ± kσ.

Milestones: k = 1.5 → ≥55.6% | k = 2 → ≥75% | k = 2.5 → ≥84% | k = 3 → ≥88.9%.

Empirical Rule (Bell-Shaped ONLY)
  • μ ± 1σ: ≈ 68% (16% in each tail!)
  • μ ± 2σ: ≈ 95% (2.5% in each tail!)
  • μ ± 3σ: ≈ 99.7% (0.15% in each tail!)

Section 03

Measures of Position, Fractional Rank, Depth & Modified Box-Plots

Quartiles, Deciles, Percentiles & Z-Score
  • Quartiles: Divide ordered data into 4 parts (Q1 = P25, Q2 = Me, Q3 = P75). IQR = Q3 − Q1.
  • k-th q-Quantile: [k(n + 1) / q]th ordered value (q = 4 for quartiles, 10 for deciles, 100 for percentiles).
  • Grouped Decile / Percentile: Dx = L + [(xn/10 − cf)/f]·c  |  Px = L + [(xn/100 − cf)/f]·c.
  • Standard Score (z-score): z = (x − x̄) / s. Tells how many SDs a value lies above/below the mean (used to compare scores across exams like STA101 vs Math257 — Final 2024 Q5d).
Rank, Fractional Ranking & Depth
  • Rank (R): Position of a value when data are sorted in ascending order (1 to n).
  • Fractional Ranking (for ties): Tied values all receive the average of the positions they occupy (e.g., if four 24s occupy positions 10, 11, 12, 13, each gets rank (10+11+12+13)/4 = 11.5).
  • Depth of a data point: Distance from the nearest extreme (min or max):
    Depth = min(Rank, n + 1 − Rank)

5-Number Summary, 7-Number Summary & Modified Box-Plot Rules

Summaries & Fences

  • 5-Number Summary: (Min, Q1, Median, Q3, Max)
  • 7-Number Summary: (Min, P10, Q1, Median, Q3, P90, Max) (or 2nd/9th deciles/octiles depending on convention; ISRT 2nd Incourse 2022 uses Min, D1, Q1, Q2, Q3, D9, Max).
  • Lower Inner Fence (LIF): Q1 − 1.5 × IQR
  • Upper Inner Fence (UIF): Q3 + 1.5 × IQR
  • Lower / Upper Outer Fences: Q1 − 3 × IQR and Q3 + 3 × IQR

Adjacent Values & Outlier Classification

  • Lower Adjacent Value: Smallest actual observation ≥ LIF.
  • Upper Adjacent Value: Largest actual observation ≤ UIF.
  • Whiskers extend from the box (Q1, Q3) to the adjacent values (NOT to the fences!).
  • Mild Outlier: Outside inner fences, inside outer fences (marked ○ or *).
  • Extreme Outlier: Outside outer fences (marked ★).

Section 05 • Prottasha & Mihad Notes

Proofs & Derivations Vault (9 Core Theorems)

Every theorem below is directly from Prottasha's Proof Notes, Mihad's Class Notes, and past ISRT Final Exams. Try deriving each proof on paper before clicking to reveal the steps.

Theorem 1 Sum of deviations from the Arithmetic Mean is zero: Σ(xi − x̄) = 0
Given x̄ = (1/n) Σxi ⇒ Σxi = n·x̄
Σ(xi − x̄) = Σxi − Σx̄ = n·x̄ − n·x̄ = 0. [Proved]
Theorem 2 Sum of squared deviations is minimized about the Mean: Σ(xi − x̄)² < Σ(xi − a)² (a ≠ x̄)
Add & subtract x̄ inside the square:
Σ(xi − a)² = Σ[(xi − x̄) + (x̄ − a)]²
= Σ(xi − x̄)² + 2(x̄ − a) Σ(xi − x̄) + Σ(x̄ − a)²
Since Σ(xi − x̄) = 0, the middle term vanishes:
Σ(xi − a)² = Σ(xi − x̄)² + n(x̄ − a)²
Because a ≠ x̄, n(x̄ − a)² > 0 strictly ⇒ Σ(xi − x̄)² < Σ(xi − a)². [Proved]
Theorem 3 Mean & Median of the first n natural numbers (1, 2, ..., n) are both (n + 1) / 2
Mean: x̄ = (1 + 2 + ... + n) / n = [n(n + 1) / 2] / n = (n + 1) / 2.
Median (n odd): The ((n + 1)/2)th term is directly (n + 1) / 2.
Median (n even): Average of (n/2)th and (n/2 + 1)th terms = [(n/2) + (n/2 + 1)] / 2 = (n + 1) / 2. [Proved]
Theorem 4 Variance of the first n natural numbers is (n² − 1) / 12
σ² = (1/n) Σx² − (x̄)² = [n(n + 1)(2n + 1) / (6n)] − [(n + 1) / 2]²
= [(n + 1)(2n + 1) / 6] − [(n + 1)² / 4]
= (n + 1) · [2(2n + 1) − 3(n + 1)] / 12 = (n + 1)(4n + 2 − 3n − 3) / 12 = (n + 1)(n − 1) / 12 = (n² − 1) / 12. [Proved]
Theorem 5 Origin & Scale Effect on Mean (x̄ = c·d̄ + a) and Standard Deviation (sx = |c|·sd)
Let di = (xi − a) / c, where a = origin shift and c = scale factor (c ≠ 0). Then xi = a + c·di.
Mean: x̄ = (1/n) Σ(a + c·di) = (na)/n + c·(Σdi / n) = a + c·d̄. ⇒ Depends on BOTH origin and scale.
SD: xi − x̄ = (a + c·di) − (a + c·d̄) = c(di − d̄). Notice 'a' cancels out!
sx = √[ Σ(xi − x̄)² / (n − 1) ] = √[ c² · Σ(di − d̄)² / (n − 1) ] = |c| · sd. ⇒ Depends ONLY on scale, NOT on origin. [Proved]
Theorem 6 Final 2024 Q5(b) [5 Marks] Prove AM ≥ GM ≥ HM and AM × HM = GM² (for a, b > 0)
Let a, b > 0. Then AM = (a + b)/2, GM = √(ab), HM = 2 / (1/a + 1/b) = 2ab / (a + b).
Part 1 (AM ≥ GM): Since (a − b)² ≥ 0 ⇒ (a + b)² − 4ab ≥ 0 ⇒ (a + b)² ≥ 4ab ⇒ (a + b)/2 ≥ √(ab) ⇒ AM ≥ GM.
Part 2 (GM ≥ HM): Since (1/a − 1/b)² ≥ 0 ⇒ (1/a + 1/b)² ≥ 4/(ab) ⇒ (1/a + 1/b) ≥ 2/√(ab) ⇒ √(ab) ≥ 2 / (1/a + 1/b) ⇒ GM ≥ HM. Hence AM ≥ GM ≥ HM.
Identity: AM × HM = [(a + b)/2] × [2ab / (a + b)] = ab = (√(ab))² = GM². [Proved]
Theorem 7 Computational (Shortcut) Variance Formula: s² = [Σx² − (Σx)²/n] / (n − 1)
Σ(xi − x̄)² = Σ(xi² − 2x̄·xi + x̄²) = Σxi² − 2x̄ Σxi + n·x̄²
Since Σxi = n·x̄: = Σxi² − 2n·x̄² + n·x̄² = Σxi² − n·x̄² = Σxi² − n·(Σxi / n)² = Σxi² − (Σxi)² / n.
Dividing both sides by (n − 1) gives s² = [Σxi² − (Σxi)² / n] / (n − 1). [Proved]
Theorem 8 Final 2024 Q4(d) [4 Marks] Geometric Proof of Grouped Mode Formula from a Histogram
In the modal bar of width c, let AC = f1 − f0 = Δ1 and BD = f1 − f2 = Δ2. Draw diagonals AD and BC intersecting at P; drop a perpendicular from P to the x-axis at distance x from Lmod (and c − x from Lmod + c).
Triangles △PAC and △PBD are similar (∠PAC = ∠PDB, ∠PCA = ∠PBD, vertical angles at P):
(Horizontal altitude of △PAC) / (Horizontal altitude of △PBD) = AC / BD ⇒ x / (c − x) = Δ1 / Δ2
Cross-multiply: Δ2·x = Δ1(c − x) ⇒ x(Δ1 + Δ2) = Δ1·c ⇒ x = [Δ1 / (Δ1 + Δ2)] · c.
Therefore, Mode = Lmod + x = Lmod + [Δ1 / (Δ1 + Δ2)] · c. [Proved]
Theorem 9 1st Incourse 2023 Q4(a) For two numbers X1, X2, Population SD = Half the Range
Mean μ = (X1 + X2) / 2. Deviations: X1 − μ = (X1 − X2)/2, X2 − μ = −(X1 − X2)/2.
σ² = [(X1 − μ)² + (X2 − μ)²] / 2 = [ (X1 − X2)²/4 + (X1 − X2)²/4 ] / 2 = (X1 − X2)² / 4.
Taking square root: σ = |X1 − X2| / 2 = Range / 2. [Proved]

1st Incourse 2025 • Q3 [2 Marks] Linear Transformation of Marks (Divide by 4, Add 4)

Question: Original marks (out of 100) have Mean = 73.5 and SD = 16.75. Tutor converts them to 'out of 25' by dividing by 4, then adds a bonus of 4 to each mark. Find the new Mean and SD.

Worked Solution: Let Y = (X / 4) + 4 = 0.25·X + 4.

  • New Mean: Ȳ = 0.25(73.5) + 4 = 18.375 + 4 = 22.375.
  • New SD: Adding 4 shifts origin only (no effect on SD); dividing by 4 scales SD: sY = |0.25| × 16.75 = 4.1875.
1st Incourse 2025 • Q4 [3 Marks] Fractional Rank & Depth of 13 and 24

Data (n = 15): 1, 13, 22, 24, 7, 5, 37, 24, 42, 24, 13, 12, 3, 24, 23.

Worked Solution: Sort in ascending order:
1(1), 3(2), 5(3), 7(4), 12(5), 13(6), 13(7), 22(8), 23(9), 24(10), 24(11), 24(12), 24(13), 37(14), 42(15)

  • For 13: Occupies positions 6 and 7 → Fractional Rank = (6 + 7)/2 = 6.5. Depth = min(6.5, 16 − 6.5) = min(6.5, 9.5) = 6.5.
  • For 24: Occupies positions 10, 11, 12, 13 → Fractional Rank = (10+11+12+13)/4 = 11.5. Depth = min(11.5, 16 − 11.5) = min(11.5, 4.5) = 4.5.
1st Incourse 2025 • Q5 [4 Marks] Adjacent Values, Fences & Outliers from 5-Number Summary

Given (n = 50): Min = 9.7, Q1 = 19.675, Q2 = 23.275, Q3 = 25.85, Max = 36.3.

Worked Solution:

  • IQR = 25.85 − 19.675 = 6.175;   1.5 × IQR = 9.2625;   3 × IQR = 18.525.
  • Inner Fences: LIF = 19.675 − 9.2625 = 10.4125;   UIF = 25.85 + 9.2625 = 35.1125.
  • Outer Fences: LOF = 19.675 − 18.525 = 1.15;   UOF = 25.85 + 18.525 = 44.375.
  • Comment on Outliers: Since Min = 9.7 < LIF (10.4125) and Max = 36.3 > UIF (35.1125), both 9.7 and 36.3 are potential mild outliers (neither exceeds the outer fences). Because individual interior points are not listed, whiskers are bounded by the inner fences (10.4125 and 35.1125) as limits for the adjacent values.
1st Incourse 2025 • Q8 & Q9 [6 Marks] Chebyshev's Theorem & Harmonic Mean Average Speed
  • Q8 (Chebyshev, Mean = 75, Variance = 100 ⇒ σ = 10, Interval (60, 90)):
    Distance from mean = 90 − 75 = 75 − 60 = 15 ⇒ k = 15 / 10 = 1.5.
    Minimum proportion = 1 − 1/k² = 1 − 1/(1.5)² = 1 − 4/9 = 5/9 ≈ 0.5556 (55.56%).
  • Q9 (Dhaka to Sylhet, 4 equal quarters at 45, 75, 80, 50 km/hr):
    Equal distances ⇒ use Harmonic Mean:
    HM = 4 / (1/45 + 1/75 + 1/80 + 1/50) = 4 / (0.02222 + 0.01333 + 0.01250 + 0.02000) = 4 / 0.068056 ≈ 58.78 km/hr.
