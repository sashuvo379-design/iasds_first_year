Matrix Square Roots (Cholesky) & Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)

Welcome to the summit of Linear Algebra! First, take the “square root” of symmetric positive definite matrices via Cholesky ($A = LL^T$) and Spectral ($A^{1/2} = P\Lambda^{1/2}P^T$) factorizations. Then generalize diagonalization to every $m\times n$ matrix on Earth with the Singular Value Decomposition ($A = U\Sigma V^T$)—powering PCA, image compression, and numerically stable computing.

The Defining Equation of SVD (MATH106_SVD): $A\mathbf{v}_i = \sigma_i\mathbf{u}_i$. Every matrix $A$—even rectangular and singular—sends one orthonormal set ($\mathbf{v}_1,\dots,\mathbf{v}_k$) to a scaled copy of another orthonormal set ($\mathbf{u}_1,\dots,\mathbf{u}_k$)!
✓Compute Cholesky factor $L$ ($A = LL^T$) column-by-column
✓Compute symmetric square roots $A^{1/2}$ & statistical whitening $\Sigma^{-1/2}(X-\mu)$
✓Compute singular values $\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i(A^T A)}$ and full SVD $A = U\Sigma V^T$
✓Express $A$ as a sum of rank-1 pieces $\sum \sigma_i\mathbf{u}_i\mathbf{v}_i^T$ for compression

SQUARE ROOTS 2. Cholesky ($A = LL^T$) & Spectral Square Root ($A^{1/2}$)

2.1 Cholesky Decomposition ($A = LL^T$)

Theorem: Every symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrix $A$ has a unique lower triangular factor $L$ with positive diagonal entries such that $A = LL^T$.

Column-by-Column Algorithm: $$\ell_{jj} = \sqrt{a_{jj} - \sum_{k=1}^{j-1} \ell_{jk}^2}, \qquad \ell_{ij} = \frac{1}{\ell_{jj}}\left(a_{ij} - \sum_{k=1}^{j-1} \ell_{ik}\ell_{jk}\right) \;\;(i > j)$$
  • PD Detector: If the term under any square root is $\le 0$, then $A$ is not positive definite!
  • Fast Determinant: $\det(A) = (\det L)^2 = (\ell_{11}\ell_{22}\cdots\ell_{nn})^2$.
  • Statistics: To simulate $X \sim N(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \Sigma)$, draw $Z \sim N(\mathbf{0}, I)$ and set $X = \boldsymbol{\mu} + LZ$.

2.2 Spectral Square Root ($A^{1/2}$) & Whitening

From $A = P\Lambda P^T$ with every $\lambda_i > 0$ (since $A$ is SPD), define the unique symmetric positive definite square root:

$$A^{1/2} = P\Lambda^{1/2}P^T = \sum_{i=1}^n \sqrt{\lambda_i}\,\mathbf{e}_i\mathbf{e}_i^T \implies A^{1/2}A^{1/2} = A$$ $$A^{-1/2} = P\Lambda^{-1/2}P^T \implies A^{-1/2} A A^{-1/2} = I$$
Statistical Whitening (Standardization): If $\text{Cov}(X) = \Sigma$, then $Z = \Sigma^{-1/2}(X - \boldsymbol{\mu})$ has mean $\mathbf{0}$ and covariance $\Sigma^{-1/2}\Sigma\Sigma^{-1/2} = I$ (uncorrelated unit-variance components)!

SVD 3. Singular Value Decomposition ($A = U\Sigma V^T$) & Compression

3.1 The 6-Step Hand Recipe for SVD

For any $m \times n$ matrix $A$ of rank $k$:

  1. Form symmetric $A^T A$ (or $AA^T$, whichever is smaller).
  2. Find eigenvalues $\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_n \ge 0$; set singular values $\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i}$.
  3. Find orthonormal eigenvectors $\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_n$ of $A^T A$; place as columns of $V$ ($n\times n$).
  4. For each nonzero $\sigma_i$ ($i \le k$), compute $\mathbf{u}_i = \dfrac{1}{\sigma_i} A\mathbf{v}_i$.
  5. Extend $\{\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_k\}$ to an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^m$; place in $U$ ($m\times m$).
  6. Build $m\times n$ diagonal $\Sigma$ with $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_k$ on the main diagonal: $A = U\Sigma V^T$.
Critical Exam Warning (MATH106_SVD Slide 17): Never compute $\mathbf{u}_i$ independently as eigenvectors of $AA^T$ without checking signs—always use $\mathbf{u}_i = \frac{1}{\sigma_i}A\mathbf{v}_i$ so $A\mathbf{v}_i = \sigma_i\mathbf{u}_i$ matches!

3.2 Reduced SVD & Low-Rank Data Compression

Dropping zero rows/columns of $\Sigma$ gives the Reduced SVD ($A = U_1 \Sigma_1 V_1^T$) and the rank-1 expansion:

$$A = \sigma_1 \mathbf{u}_1 \mathbf{v}_1^T + \sigma_2 \mathbf{u}_2 \mathbf{v}_2^T + \dots + \sigma_k \mathbf{u}_k \mathbf{v}_k^T$$

Because $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_k > 0$, truncating after the first $r$ terms gives the optimal rank-$r$ approximation to $A$! For a $200\times 500$ image ($100{,}000$ pixels), a rank-20 SVD stores only $20(200 + 500 + 1) = 14{,}020$ numbers—an 86% reduction!

Interactive Proofs: $N(A) = N(A^T A)$ & Why $A = U\Sigma V^T$ Always Works MATH106_SVD Slides 7 & 20 · Anton Thm 9.4.1
Part 1: Prove $A$ and $A^T A$ have the exact same null space (hence same rank!)
($\Rightarrow$) If $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$, then $A^T A\mathbf{x} = A^T\mathbf{0} = \mathbf{0}$.
($\Leftarrow$) If $A^T A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$, multiply on the left by $\mathbf{x}^T$: $$\mathbf{x}^T A^T A \mathbf{x} = (A\mathbf{x})^T(A\mathbf{x}) = \|A\mathbf{x}\|^2 = 0 \implies A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}.$$
Part 2: Why eigenvalues of $A^T A$ are nonnegative & $\{A\mathbf{v}_i\}$ is orthogonal
$A^T A$ is symmetric, so $A^T A = VDV^T$ with orthonormal $\mathbf{v}_i$. Then $\lambda_i = \mathbf{v}_i^T A^T A\mathbf{v}_i = \|A\mathbf{v}_i\|^2 \ge 0$. For $i \neq j$: $(A\mathbf{v}_i)\cdot(A\mathbf{v}_j) = \mathbf{v}_i^T(A^T A\mathbf{v}_j) = \lambda_j(\mathbf{v}_i^T\mathbf{v}_j) = 0$.
Part 3: Normalize to $\mathbf{u}_i = A\mathbf{v}_i / \sigma_i$ and assemble
For $i \le k$ ($\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i} > 0$), $A\mathbf{v}_i = \sigma_i\mathbf{u}_i$; for $i > k$, $A\mathbf{v}_i = \mathbf{0}$. In matrix form: $AV = U\Sigma \implies \mathbf{A = U\Sigma V^T}$ (since $V^{-1} = V^T$). $\blacksquare$

Problem 1: Finding Singular Values Final 2025 Q2(b) (3 Marks) · Final 2020 Q1(b) (5 Marks)

(a) Find the singular values of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ (Final 2025 Q2b).
(b) Find the singular values of $B = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ (Final 2020 Q1b).

Quick check for (b) — enter $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$ in descending order:
Solution:
• (a) $A^T A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$.
Characteristic eq.: $\lambda^2 - 3\lambda + 1 = 0 \implies \lambda = \dfrac{3 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2}$.
Singular values: $\sigma_1 = \sqrt{\dfrac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}} = \dfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2} \approx \mathbf{1.618}$, $\sigma_2 = \sqrt{\dfrac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2} \approx \mathbf{0.618}$.

• (b) $B^T B = \begin{bmatrix} 9 & 0 \\ 0 & 16 \end{bmatrix} \implies \lambda_1 = 16, \lambda_2 = 9 \implies \mathbf{\sigma_1 = 4,\; \sigma_2 = 3}$ (always ordered $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2$!).
Problem 2: Full SVD of a Non-Square $3\times 2$ Matrix MATH106_SVD Slides 15–18 · Anton Ex. 2

Find a full singular value decomposition $A = U\Sigma V^T$ of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

Hint: $A^T A$ is $2\times 2$ (smaller than $3\times 3$ $AA^T$!). Find $\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2$, compute $\mathbf{u}_1, \mathbf{u}_2$ via $\frac{1}{\sigma_i}A\mathbf{v}_i$, then find a unit $\mathbf{u}_3 \perp \mathbf{u}_1, \mathbf{u}_2$ to complete $U$!
Solution:
1. $A^T A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \implies \lambda_1 = 3, \lambda_2 = 1 \implies \mathbf{\sigma_1 = \sqrt{3},\; \sigma_2 = 1}$.
2. Orthonormal eigenvectors of $A^T A$: $\mathbf{v}_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf{v}_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$.
3. Left singular vectors via $\mathbf{u}_i = \frac{1}{\sigma_i}A\mathbf{v}_i$: $$\mathbf{u}_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{u}_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}.$$ 4. Extend to $\mathbb{R}^3$: solving $2x_1+x_2+x_3=0$ and $-x_2+x_3=0$ gives $\mathbf{u}_3 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.
5. Assemble $A = [\mathbf{u}_1\;\mathbf{u}_2\;\mathbf{u}_3] \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{3} & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} [\mathbf{v}_1\;\mathbf{v}_2]^T$. ✓
Problem 3: Cholesky Decomposition ($A = LL^T$) by Hand MATH106_cholesky Slides 4–5

Compute the lower triangular Cholesky factor $L$ of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 2 & -2 \\ 2 & 10 & 2 \\ -2 & 2 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$ and verify $LL^T = A$.

Solution:
• Col 1: $\ell_{11} = \sqrt{4} = 2$, $\ell_{21} = 2/2 = 1$, $\ell_{31} = -2/2 = -1$.
• Col 2: $\ell_{22} = \sqrt{10 - 1^2} = 3$, $\ell_{32} = \frac{2 - (-1)(1)}{3} = 1$.
• Col 3: $\ell_{33} = \sqrt{6 - (-1)^2 - 1^2} = \sqrt{4} = 2$.
Result: $L = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$, and $\det(A) = (2\cdot 3\cdot 2)^2 = 144$.
Problem 4: Symmetric Spectral Square Root $A^{1/2} = P\Lambda^{1/2}P^T$ Strang 6.5 Q22 · MATH106_cholesky Slide 6

Compute the symmetric positive definite square root $A^{1/2}$ of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 4 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$ and verify $(A^{1/2})^2 = A$.

Solution:
1. Eigenvalues of $A$: $\lambda_1 = 9$ ($\sqrt{9}=3$) with $\mathbf{e}_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$; $\lambda_2 = 1$ ($\sqrt{1}=1$) with $\mathbf{e}_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$.
2. $A^{1/2} = P\Lambda^{1/2}P^T = \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}}$.
3. Check: $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 4 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix} = A$. ✓
