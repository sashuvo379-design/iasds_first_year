3 Core Theory, Type I vs. Type II Guide & Exam Traps

1. Fubini's Theorem (Rectangles, Sec. 14.1)

If $f(x, y)$ is continuous on the rectangle $R = [a, b] \times [c, d]$, then the order of integration can be freely swapped:

$$\iint_R f(x, y)\,dA = \int_a^b \int_c^d f(x, y)\,dy\,dx = \int_c^d \int_a^b f(x, y)\,dx\,dy$$

When $f(x, y) \ge 0$, this double integral equals the exact 3D volume under the surface $z = f(x, y)$ above $R$.

2. Area & Average Value via Double Integrals

Setting $f(x, y) = 1$ gives the exact 2D area of $R$, which normalizes the average value of any surface $f$ over $R$:

$$\text{Area}(R) = \iint_R 1\,dA, \qquad f_{\text{ave}} = \frac{1}{\text{Area}(R)}\iint_R f(x, y)\,dA$$

3. Type I (Vertically Simple — $dy\,dx$)

Bounded left/right by constants $a \le x \le b$ and bottom/top by functions $g_1(x) \le y \le g_2(x)$:

$$\iint_R f(x, y)\,dA = \int_a^b \int_{g_1(x)}^{g_2(x)} f(x, y)\,dy\,dx$$

Scan vertically (bottom $\to$ top) while holding $x$ fixed.

4. Type II (Horizontally Simple — $dx\,dy$)

Bounded bottom/top by constants $c \le y \le d$ and left/right by functions $h_1(y) \le x \le h_2(y)$:

$$\iint_R f(x, y)\,dA = \int_c^d \int_{h_1(y)}^{h_2(y)} f(x, y)\,dx\,dy$$

Scan horizontally (left $\to$ right) while holding $y$ fixed.

⚠ 4 High-Yield ISRT Exam Traps

Trap 1: Outer Limits MUST Be Constants The outer integral's limits must ALWAYS be pure numbers ($a$ to $b$, or $c$ to $d$). Putting $x$ or $y$ in the outer limits leaves a variable in your final numeric answer!
Trap 2: Never Just Swap Non-Rectangular Limits You can only swap limits directly on a rectangle $[a,b]\times[c,d]$. On a general region $R$, you must sketch $R$, invert the boundary curves, and re-scan in the new direction.
Trap 3: Hidden Non-Elementary Integrands Integrands like $\sin(y^3)$, $e^{y^2}$, or $\frac{\sin x}{x}$ have no elementary antiderivative. Seeing one in the inner integral is a direct signal to reverse the order of integration!
Trap 4: Unnecessary Multi-Piece Splits Always check both orders before integrating! On the Final 2023 Q5(a) trapezoid, Type I ($dy\,dx$) requires 3 integrals, whereas Type II ($dx\,dy$) needs only 1 integral.
