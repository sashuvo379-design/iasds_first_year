Section 3 • Exam-Ready Foundations

Core Theory & Essential Formulas

Exact hypotheses, definitions, and summation identities required for full marks in ISRT MATH 105 exams.

1. Area Function & Antiderivative Method

Let $f$ be a non-negative and continuous function on $[a, b]$. If $A(x)$ denotes the area under the graph of $y = f(t)$ over the interval $[a, x]$ (where $x \in [a, b]$), then:

$$A'(x) = f(x) \quad \text{and} \quad A(a) = 0$$

Thus $A(x)$ is an antiderivative of $f(x)$ whose value vanishes when the interval collapses to the single point $x = a$.

⚠ Classic ISRT Exam Trap When solving $A(a) = 0$ yields two roots (e.g., $A(x) = x^2 - x \implies a = 0$ or $a = 1$), you must check the non-negativity hypothesis $f(x) \ge 0$ for $x \ge a$. Since $f(x) = 2x - 1$ is negative on $[0, \frac{1}{2})$, $a = 0$ is invalid and only $a = 1$ is accepted!

2. Net Signed Area vs. Total Area

When $f(x)$ takes both positive and negative values on $[a, b]$, the definite integral no longer measures total geometric area—it computes the difference of areas:

$$\text{Net Signed Area} = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx = A_{\text{above}} - A_{\text{below}}$$ $$\text{Total Area} = \int_a^b |f(x)|\,dx = A_{\text{above}} + A_{\text{below}}$$

Quick Example: For $f(x) = x$ on $[-1, 1]$, the triangle below on $[-1, 0]$ has area $\frac{1}{2}$ and the triangle above on $[0, 1]$ has area $\frac{1}{2}$.

Net Signed = 0.5 - 0.5 = 0  |  Total Area = 0.5 + 0.5 = 1

3. Riemann Sums & Regular Partitions

Divide $[a, b]$ into $n$ equal subintervals of width $\Delta x = \frac{b-a}{n}$ with partition points $x_k = a + k\Delta x$ ($k = 0, 1, \dots, n$). Choose a sample point $x_k^* \in [x_{k-1}, x_k]$:

$$\text{Left: } x_k^* = a + (k-1)\Delta x$$ $$\text{Right: } x_k^* = a + k\Delta x$$ $$\text{Midpoint: } x_k^* = a + \left(k - \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\Delta x$$

The exact net signed area is the limit of the Riemann sum: $$A = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{k=1}^{n} f(x_k^*)\,\Delta x = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx.$$

1st In-course 2023 Q1 & Final 2023 Q1(a) • 3 Marks Area Function
The area under the graph of $f$ over the interval $[a, x]$ is given by $A(x) = x(x - 1)$. Use the method of antiderivatives to find $f(x)$, and determine the valid value of $a$.
Hint: Expand $A(x) = x^2 - x$, differentiate to get $f(x) = A'(x)$, solve $A(a) = 0$ for candidate values of $a$, and reject any root where $f(x)$ dips below the $x$-axis on $[a, x]$.
Step 1: Differentiate to find $f(x)$
By the Antiderivative Method, $A'(x) = f(x)$. Expanding $A(x) = x^2 - x$: $$f(x) = A'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}(x^2 - x) = 2x - 1.$$
Step 2: Solve $A(a) = 0$ for candidate lower limits
Since the area over the single-point interval $[a, a]$ is zero: $$A(a) = a(a - 1) = 0 \implies a = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad a = 1.$$
Step 3: Check non-negativity $f(x) \ge 0$
The area function requires $f(x) \ge 0$ for all $x \ge a$: $$2x - 1 \ge 0 \iff x \ge \frac{1}{2}.$$
Step 4: Select the valid lower limit $a$
If $a = 0$, then $f(x) = 2x - 1 < 0$ on $[0, \frac{1}{2})$, which violates non-negativity. For $a = 1$, $f(x) \ge 1 > 0$ for all $x \ge 1$. $$\therefore\; f(x) = 2x - 1 \quad \text{and} \quad a = 1.$$

1st In-course 2025 Q1 • 3 Marks Area Function
Use elementary geometry to find the area function $A(x)$ under the graph of $f(t) = 3t - 3$ over the interval $[2, x]$ (for $x \ge 2$), and confirm that $A'(x) = f(x)$.
Hint: Evaluate $f(2)$ and $f(x)$. Because both heights are positive, the region is a trapezoid with width $(x - 2)$.
Step 1: Identify the geometric shape
At the left endpoint $t = 2$, the height is $h_1 = f(2) = 3(2) - 3 = 3$. At the right endpoint $t = x$, the height is $h_2 = f(x) = 3x - 3$. Since $f$ is linear and positive on $[2, x]$, the region is a trapezoid.
Step 2: Apply the trapezoid area formula
With horizontal base (width) $w = x - 2$: $$A(x) = \frac{1}{2}(h_1 + h_2)\cdot w = \frac{1}{2}\big(3 + (3x - 3)\big)(x - 2) = \frac{3}{2}x(x - 2).$$
Step 3: Simplify $A(x)$
$$A(x) = \frac{3}{2}x^2 - 3x.$$
Step 4: Confirm $A'(x) = f(x)$ and $A(2) = 0$
$$A'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{3}{2}x^2 - 3x\right) = 3x - 3 = f(x), \qquad A(2) = \frac{3}{2}(4) - 6 = 0.$$

Final 2024 Q1(a) • 2 Marks Area Function
Geometrically determine the area function $A(x)$ for $f(t) = 1 + t$ over $[-1, x]$ ($x \ge -1$), and justify that $A'(x) = f(x)$.
Hint: Notice that $f(-1) = 0$, so the region starts at a point on the $x$-axis and forms a right triangle!
Step 1: Determine base and height of the triangle
Since $f(-1) = 1 + (-1) = 0$ and $f(x) = 1 + x \ge 0$, the region under $y = 1 + t$ over $[-1, x]$ is a right triangle with: $$\text{Base } b = x - (-1) = x + 1, \qquad \text{Height } h = f(x) = x + 1.$$
Step 2: Compute $A(x)$ geometrically
$$A(x) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot b \cdot h = \frac{1}{2}(x + 1)^2 = \frac{1}{2}x^2 + x + \frac{1}{2}.$$
Step 3: Justify $A'(x) = f(x)$
Differentiating with respect to $x$: $$A'(x) = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 2(x + 1) = 1 + x = f(x), \quad \text{and} \quad A(-1) = \frac{1}{2}(0)^2 = 0.$$

1st In-course 2024 Q2 • 5 Marks Riemann Sums
Find the net signed area between the curve $y = x^2$ and the $x$-axis over the interval $[-1, 1]$ using the midpoint of each subinterval and the standard summation limits.
Hint: Set $\Delta x = \frac{2}{n}$ and $x_k^* = -1 + \left(k - \frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{2}{n} = -1 + \frac{2k - 1}{n}$. Expand $(x_k^*)^2$ carefully before grouping powers of $k$.
Step 1: Partition width & midpoint formula
With $a = -1, b = 1$: $$\Delta x = \frac{1 - (-1)}{n} = \frac{2}{n}, \qquad x_k^* = -1 + \left(k - \frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{2}{n} = -1 + \frac{2k - 1}{n}.$$
Step 2: Expand $f(x_k^*) = (x_k^*)^2$
$$f(x_k^*) = \left(-1 + \frac{2k-1}{n}\right)^2 = 1 - \frac{2(2k-1)}{n} + \frac{(2k-1)^2}{n^2}$$ $$= 1 + \frac{2}{n} + \frac{1}{n^2} - \left(\frac{4}{n} + \frac{4}{n^2}\right)k + \frac{4}{n^2}k^2.$$
Step 3: Multiply by $\Delta x = \frac{2}{n}$ and sum from $k = 1$ to $n$
$$R_n = \sum_{k=1}^n f(x_k^*)\Delta x = 2\left(1 + \frac{2}{n} + \frac{1}{n^2}\right)\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n 1 - 8\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{k=1}^n k + 8\left(\frac{1}{n^3}\sum_{k=1}^n k^2\right).$$
Step 4: Evaluate the limit as $n \to \infty$
Using $\lim \frac{1}{n}\sum 1 = 1$, $\lim \frac{1}{n^2}\sum k = \frac{1}{2}$, and $\lim \frac{1}{n^3}\sum k^2 = \frac{1}{3}$: $$A = 2(1)(1) - 8(1)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) + 8\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) = 2 - 4 + \frac{8}{3} = \frac{2}{3}.$$

Final 2023 Q1(b) • 4 Marks Riemann Sums
Find the net signed area between the curve $y = x^2 - 1$ and the $x$-axis over the interval $[0, 2]$ using the right endpoint of each subinterval.
Hint: Here $a = 0$, so $x_k^* = \frac{2k}{n}$ is super clean. Separate the sum into $\sum k^2$ and $\sum 1$.
Step 1: Set up $\Delta x$ and right endpoint $x_k^*$
$$\Delta x = \frac{2 - 0}{n} = \frac{2}{n}, \qquad x_k^* = a + k\Delta x = \frac{2k}{n}.$$
Step 2: Write the $k$-th rectangle area
$$f(x_k^*)\Delta x = \left[\left(\frac{2k}{n}\right)^2 - 1\right]\frac{2}{n} = \left(\frac{4k^2}{n^2} - 1\right)\frac{2}{n} = \frac{8k^2}{n^3} - \frac{2}{n}.$$
Step 3: Form the Riemann sum $R_n$
$$R_n = \sum_{k=1}^n \left(\frac{8k^2}{n^3} - \frac{2}{n}\right) = 8\left(\frac{1}{n^3}\sum_{k=1}^n k^2\right) - 2\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n 1\right).$$
Step 4: Take $n \to \infty$
$$A = \lim_{n \to \infty} R_n = 8\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) - 2(1) = \frac{8}{3} - 2 = \frac{2}{3}.$$

1st In-course 2023 Q2 • 5 Marks Riemann Sums
Find the net signed area between the curve $y = x^3$ and the $x$-axis over the interval $[-1, 1]$ using the right endpoint of each subinterval.
Hint: Expand $(u - 1)^3 = u^3 - 3u^2 + 3u - 1$ with $u = \frac{2k}{n}$. Because $x^3$ is an odd function on $[-1, 1]$, expect the final limit to cancel out to $0$!
Step 1: Partition width and right endpoints
$$\Delta x = \frac{1 - (-1)}{n} = \frac{2}{n}, \qquad x_k^* = -1 + \frac{2k}{n} = \frac{2k}{n} - 1.$$
Step 2: Expand $f(x_k^*) = (x_k^*)^3$
$$f(x_k^*) = \left(\frac{2k}{n} - 1\right)^3 = \frac{8k^3}{n^3} - \frac{12k^2}{n^2} + \frac{6k}{n} - 1.$$
Step 3: Multiply by $\Delta x = \frac{2}{n}$ and sum
$$R_n = 16\left(\frac{1}{n^4}\sum_{k=1}^n k^3\right) - 24\left(\frac{1}{n^3}\sum_{k=1}^n k^2\right) + 12\left(\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{k=1}^n k\right) - 2\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n 1\right).$$
Step 4: Take the limit as $n \to \infty$
$$A = 16\left(\frac{1}{4}\right) - 24\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) + 12\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) - 2(1) = 4 - 8 + 6 - 2 = 0.$$

Final 2023 Q1(c) • 2 Marks Net vs. Total Area
Determine the net signed area and the total area between the curve $y = f(x)$ and the interval $[a, d]$ on the $x$-axis shown below. Give the integral formulations for both areas.
Hint: Subtract areas below the $x$-axis for Net Signed Area, and add all magnitudes (using $|f(x)|$) for Total Area.
Step 1: Net Signed Area formulation & value
$$\text{Net Signed Area} = \int_a^d f(x)\,dx = A_{[a,b]} - A_{[b,c]} + A_{[c,d]}$$ $$= 10 - 94 + 9 = -75.$$
Step 2: Total Area formulation & value
$$\text{Total Area} = \int_a^d |f(x)|\,dx = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx - \int_b^c f(x)\,dx + \int_c^d f(x)\,dx$$ $$= 10 + 94 + 9 = 113.$$
