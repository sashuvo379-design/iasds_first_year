3 Core Theory, Curve Gallery & Exam Traps

1. Polar ↔ Cartesian Conversions & Non-Uniqueness

Unlike Cartesian coordinates, every point in the plane has infinitely many polar representations:

$$x = r\cos\theta, \qquad y = r\sin\theta$$ $$r^2 = x^2 + y^2, \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{y}{x}$$ $$(r, \theta) \equiv (r, \theta \pm 2k\pi) \equiv (-r, \theta + \pi)$$

When $r < 0$, walk $|r|$ units in the opposite direction ($\theta + \pi$) from the pole.

2. Polar Area Formulas (Section 10.3)

Summing thin circular sectors of area $\Delta A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\Delta\theta$:

$$\text{Single Curve: } A = \frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta r^2\,d\theta = \frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta [f(\theta)]^2\,d\theta$$ $$\text{Between Two Curves: } A = \frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta \left(r_{\text{outer}}^2 - r_{\text{inner}}^2\right)d\theta$$

Here $\alpha \le \theta \le \beta$ must sweep the region exactly once without overlapping.

⚠ 4 High-Yield ISRT Exam Traps

Trap 1: Odd-n Rose Double-Tracing Integrating odd-$n$ roses ($r = \sin 3\theta$, $r = \cos 3\theta$) from $0$ to $2\pi$ doubles the true area! Always find pole tangents ($r = 0$) for 1 petal and multiply by $n$, or integrate over $[0, \pi]$.
Trap 2: Missing Pole Intersections Solving $r_1(\theta) = r_2(\theta)$ may miss the pole $(0,0)$ because curves reach the pole at different angles (e.g., $r = 2\sin\theta$ hits the pole at $\theta = 0$, while $r = 2\cos\theta$ hits it at $\theta = \pi/2$).
Trap 3: Squaring the Difference For the area between two polar curves, always use $r_{\text{outer}}^2 - r_{\text{inner}}^2$, NEVER $(r_{\text{outer}} - r_{\text{inner}})^2$!
Trap 4: Negative $r$ on Limaçons On $r = \frac{1}{2} + \cos\theta$, when $r < 0$ ($\frac{2\pi}{3} < \theta < \frac{4\pi}{3}$), the curve traces the inner loop. Because $r^2 \ge 0$, integrating $0$ to $2\pi$ adds the inner loop area instead of subtracting it!
