Section 3 • Exam-Ready Foundations

3. Core Theory, Decision Matrix & ISRT Exam Traps

The perpendicular ($\perp$) vs. parallel ($\parallel$) cross-section distinction, the master 4-case formula matrix, and the 4 classic ISRT exam pitfalls.

Method of Disks & Washers (Section 6.2)

Cross-sections are taken perpendicular ($\perp$) to the axis of revolution. Each thin rectangular strip rotates into a flat circular disk (no gap) or washer ring (gap between region and axis):

$$V = \int_a^b \pi\Big(\big[R_{\text{out}}\big]^2 - \big[R_{\text{in}}\big]^2\Big)\,du$$

Integration variable: Matches the axis direction! Horizontal axis ($x$-axis or $y=c$) $\implies dx$. Vertical axis ($y$-axis or $x=c$) $\implies dy$.

Method of Cylindrical Shells (Section 6.3)

Cross-sections are taken parallel ($\parallel$) to the axis of revolution. Each thin strip rotates into a hollow cylindrical sleeve of circumference $2\pi r$, height $h$, and thickness $du$:

$$V = \int_a^b 2\pi \cdot (\text{radius } r)\cdot(\text{height } h)\,du$$

Integration variable: Opposite to the axis direction! Vertical axis ($y$-axis or $x=c$) $\implies dx$. Horizontal axis ($x$-axis or $y=c$) $\implies dy$.

Master 4-Case Formula Table (Coordinate & Shifted Axes)

Axis of Revolution Disk / Washer Method ($\perp$ to Axis) Cylindrical Shell Method ($\parallel$ to Axis)
$x$-axis ($y = 0$) $dx$: $\displaystyle \pi\int_a^b \big([f_{\text{out}}(x)]^2 - [g_{\text{in}}(x)]^2\big)\,dx$ $dy$: $\displaystyle 2\pi\int_c^d y\,\big[w_{\text{right}}(y) - v_{\text{left}}(y)\big]\,dy$
$y$-axis ($x = 0$) $dy$: $\displaystyle \pi\int_c^d \big([w_{\text{out}}(y)]^2 - [v_{\text{in}}(y)]^2\big)\,dy$ $dx$: $\displaystyle 2\pi\int_a^b x\,\big[f_{\text{top}}(x) - g_{\text{bot}}(x)\big]\,dx$
Horizontal ($y = c$) $dx$: $\displaystyle \pi\int_a^b \big(|f_{\text{out}}(x) - c|^2 - |g_{\text{in}}(x) - c|^2\big)\,dx$ $dy$: $\displaystyle 2\pi\int_{y_1}^{y_2} |y - c|\,\big[w_{\text{right}}(y) - v_{\text{left}}(y)\big]\,dy$
Vertical ($x = c$) $dy$: $\displaystyle \pi\int_{y_1}^{y_2} \big(|w_{\text{out}}(y) - c|^2 - |v_{\text{in}}(y) - c|^2\big)\,dy$ $dx$: $\displaystyle 2\pi\int_a^b |x - c|\,\big[f_{\text{top}}(x) - g_{\text{bot}}(x)\big]\,dx$

⚠ 4 High-Yield ISRT Exam Traps

Trap 1: $(R - r)^2 \ne R^2 - r^2$

Always square the outer radius $R_{\text{out}}$ and inner radius $R_{\text{in}}$ separately before subtracting! Writing $\pi\int (R_{\text{out}} - R_{\text{in}})^2$ computes the wrong cross-sectional area.

Trap 2: Negative Radius on Shifted Axes

Radii must be non-negative distances: always compute Top − Bottom or Right − Left. Around $x = 9$ (with $x \le 9$), the shell radius is $9 - x$, NOT $x - 9$.

Trap 3: Mixing Up $dx$ and $dy$

Disk/Washer integrates along the axis's own variable ($y=c \to dx$, $x=c \to dy$). Cylindrical Shells integrates along the opposite variable ($x=c \to dx$, $y=c \to dy$)!

Trap 4: Half-Height vs. Full-Height Curves

For symmetric curves like the torus circle $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$, the upper half is $y = +\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$ and lower half is $y = -\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$, so the full vertical shell height is $2\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$, NOT $\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$!

Final 2024 Q1(b) & 2nd Incourse 2025 Q1(a) 2 Marks • Conceptual
Briefly explain the difference between the method of disks (or washers) and the method of cylindrical shells in terms of how cross sections are taken.
Hint: Contrast the orientation of the slicing planes/rectangles relative to the axis of revolution: perpendicular ($\perp$) vs. parallel ($\parallel$).
Exam-Ready Answer
• Method of Disks / Washers: Cross sections (thin slices) are taken perpendicular ($\perp$) to the axis of revolution. Revolving each perpendicular strip produces a flat circular disk (or washer ring if there is a gap), with volume element $dV = \pi(R_{\text{out}}^2 - R_{\text{in}}^2)\,du$.

• Method of Cylindrical Shells: Cross sections (thin strips) are taken parallel ($\parallel$) to the axis of revolution. Revolving each parallel strip produces a thin hollow cylindrical shell, with volume element $dV = 2\pi r h\,du$.
Final 2025 Q4(b) & 2nd Incourse 2025 Q1(b) 4–5 Marks • Vertical Shifted Axis $x = 9$
Find the volume of the solid that results when the region enclosed by $y = \sqrt{x}$, $y = 0$, and $x = 9$ is revolved about the line $x = 9$.
Hint: Both methods are clean! For Disks ($dy$): $R(y) = 9 - y^2$ on $[0, 3]$. For Shells ($dx$): $r(x) = 9 - x$ and $h(x) = \sqrt{x}$ on $[0, 9]$.
Method 1: Disk Method ($dy$, $\perp$ to $x = 9$)
Rewriting $y = \sqrt{x}$ as $x = y^2$ ($0 \le y \le 3$), there is no gap between the region and $x = 9$, so $R(y) = 9 - y^2$: $$V = \pi\int_0^3 (9 - y^2)^2\,dy = \pi\int_0^3 (81 - 18y^2 + y^4)\,dy = \pi\left[81y - 6y^3 + \frac{y^5}{5}\right]_0^3$$ $$= \pi\left(243 - 162 + \frac{243}{5}\right) = \pi\left(81 + \frac{243}{5}\right) = \frac{648\pi}{5}.$$
Method 2: Cylindrical Shell Method ($dx$, $\parallel$ to $x = 9$)
For $0 \le x \le 9$, shell radius is $r(x) = 9 - x$ and height is $h(x) = \sqrt{x}$: $$V = 2\pi\int_0^9 (9 - x)\sqrt{x}\,dx = 2\pi\int_0^9 (9x^{1/2} - x^{3/2})\,dx = 2\pi\left[6x^{3/2} - \frac{2}{5}x^{5/2}\right]_0^9$$ $$= 2\pi\left(6\cdot 27 - \frac{2}{5}\cdot 243\right) = 2\pi\left(162 - \frac{486}{5}\right) = \frac{648\pi}{5} \approx 407.15.$$
Final 2025 Q4(c) & Final 2024 Q4(c) 4 Marks • Horizontal Shifted Axis $y = -2$
Use cylindrical shells to find the volume of the solid generated when the region $R$ under $y = x^2$ and above the $x$-axis over $[0, 2]$ is revolved about the line $y = -2$.
Hint: Horizontal shells ($dy$) run over $y \in [0, 4]$ with radius $r(y) = y - (-2) = y + 2$ and horizontal length $h(y) = 2 - \sqrt{y}$.
Step 1: Cylindrical Shell Setup ($dy$, $\parallel$ to $y = -2$)
Since $y = x^2 \implies x = \sqrt{y}$ for $y \in [0, 4]$, a horizontal strip at height $y$ has radius $r = y - (-2) = y + 2$ and length $h = 2 - \sqrt{y}$: $$V = 2\pi\int_0^4 (y + 2)(2 - \sqrt{y})\,dy = 2\pi\int_0^4 \left(2y - y^{3/2} + 4 - 2y^{1/2}\right)dy$$ $$= 2\pi\left[y^2 - \frac{2}{5}y^{5/2} + 4y - \frac{4}{3}y^{3/2}\right]_0^4 = 2\pi\left(16 - \frac{64}{5} + 16 - \frac{32}{3}\right) = \frac{256\pi}{15}.$$
Step 2: Washer Verification ($dx$, $\perp$ to $y = -2$)
With $R_{\text{out}} = x^2 + 2$ and $R_{\text{in}} = 2$ on $[0, 2]$: $$V = \pi\int_0^2 \left[(x^2 + 2)^2 - 2^2\right]dx = \pi\int_0^2 (x^4 + 4x^2)\,dx = \pi\left[\frac{x^5}{5} + \frac{4x^3}{3}\right]_0^2 = \pi\left(\frac{32}{5} + \frac{32}{3}\right) = \frac{256\pi}{15}.$$
2nd Incourse 2025 Q2 4 Marks • Torus Volume
Formulate an integral (and evaluate as bonus) to find the volume of the torus obtained by revolving the circle $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$ about the line $x = b$, where $b > a > 0$.
Hint: For Shells ($dx$), radius is $b - x$ and full vertical height is $2\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}$ over $[-a, a]$. Note that $x\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}$ is an odd function on $[-a, a]$!
Formulation 1: Cylindrical Shells ($dx$) + Full Evaluation
For $x \in [-a, a]$, shell radius is $r(x) = b - x$ and full height is $h(x) = \sqrt{a^2-x^2} - (-\sqrt{a^2-x^2}) = 2\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}$: $$V = \int_{-a}^{a} 2\pi(b - x)\cdot 2\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}\,dx = 4\pi b\int_{-a}^{a} \sqrt{a^2 - x^2}\,dx - 4\pi\underbrace{\int_{-a}^{a} x\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}\,dx}_{=\,0\text{ (odd function)}}$$ Since $\int_{-a}^{a}\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\pi a^2$ (area of a semicircle of radius $a$): $$V = 4\pi b\left(\frac{\pi a^2}{2}\right) = 2\pi^2 a^2 b.$$
Formulation 2: Washer Method ($dy$)
For $y \in [-a, a]$, $R_{\text{out}} = b + \sqrt{a^2 - y^2}$ and $R_{\text{in}} = b - \sqrt{a^2 - y^2}$: $$V = \pi\int_{-a}^{a} \left[\left(b + \sqrt{a^2 - y^2}\right)^2 - \left(b - \sqrt{a^2 - y^2}\right)^2\right]dy = 4\pi b\int_{-a}^{a} \sqrt{a^2 - y^2}\,dy = 2\pi^2 a^2 b.$$
2nd Incourse 2020/2021 Q2 5 Marks • Dual Disk & Shell Proof
Consider the region $R$ bounded by $y = \cos x$, $y = 0$, $x = 0$, and $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$. Find the volume of the solid obtained by revolving $R$ about the $y$-axis using both the Disk Method and the Shell Method.
Hint: Shell ($dx$) uses 1 pass of IBP on $\int_0^{\pi/2} x\cos x\,dx$. Disk ($dy$) gives $\pi\int_0^1 (\cos^{-1}y)^2\,dy$ — substitute $y = \cos t$ to turn it into $\pi\int_0^{\pi/2} t^2\sin t\,dt$!
Method 1: Cylindrical Shell Method ($dx$)
$$V = 2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2} x\cos x\,dx = 2\pi\big[x\sin x + \cos x\big]_0^{\pi/2} = 2\pi\left[\left(\frac{\pi}{2} + 0\right) - (0 + 1)\right] = \pi^2 - 2\pi.$$
Method 2: Disk Method ($dy$)
Since $x = \cos^{-1}y$ for $y \in [0, 1]$: $$V = \pi\int_0^1 (\cos^{-1}y)^2\,dy.$$ Let $y = \cos t \implies dy = -\sin t\,dt$ ($y=0 \Rightarrow t=\frac{\pi}{2}$, $y=1 \Rightarrow t=0$): $$V = \pi\int_0^{\pi/2} t^2\sin t\,dt = \pi\big[-t^2\cos t + 2t\sin t + 2\cos t\big]_0^{\pi/2} = \pi\big[(0 + \pi + 0) - (0 + 0 + 2)\big] = \pi^2 - 2\pi.$$
ISRT Exam Q1(a) — Dual Shifted Axes 10 Marks • Washer + Shell
The region $R$ is bounded by $y = e^{-x}$, $y = \sqrt{x + 1}$, $x = 0$, and $x = 1$. Write the integral and find the volume $V$ of the solid obtained by revolving $R$ about:
(i) $y = -1$ using the Washer Method &qquad (ii) $x = -1$ using the Shell Method.
Hint: On $[0, 1]$, $\sqrt{x+1} \ge e^{-x}$. For (i) $R_{\text{out}} = \sqrt{x+1}+1$ and $R_{\text{in}} = e^{-x}+1$. For (ii) $r(x) = x + 1$ and $h(x) = \sqrt{x+1} - e^{-x}$.
Part (i): Revolved about $y = -1$ (Washer Method, $dx$)
$$V = \pi\int_0^1 \left[\left(\sqrt{x+1} + 1\right)^2 - \left(e^{-x} + 1\right)^2\right]dx = \pi\int_0^1 \left(x + 1 + 2(x+1)^{1/2} - e^{-2x} - 2e^{-x}\right)dx$$ $$= \pi\left[\frac{x^2}{2} + x + \frac{4}{3}(x+1)^{3/2} + \frac{1}{2}e^{-2x} + 2e^{-x}\right]_0^1 = \pi\left[\left(\frac{3}{2} + \frac{8\sqrt{2}}{3} + \frac{1}{2e^2} + \frac{2}{e}\right) - \left(\frac{4}{3} + \frac{1}{2} + 2\right)\right]$$ $$= \pi\left(\frac{8\sqrt{2} - 7}{3} + \frac{1}{2e^2} + \frac{2}{e}\right).$$
Part (ii): Revolved about $x = -1$ (Shell Method, $dx$)
$$V = 2\pi\int_0^1 (x + 1)\left(\sqrt{x+1} - e^{-x}\right)dx = 2\pi\int_0^1 (x+1)^{3/2}\,dx - 2\pi\int_0^1 (x+1)e^{-x}\,dx$$ $$= 2\pi\left[\frac{2}{5}(x+1)^{5/2}\right]_0^1 - 2\pi\big[-(x+2)e^{-x}\big]_0^1 = 2\pi\left[\frac{8\sqrt{2}-2}{5} - \left(2 - \frac{3}{e}\right)\right]$$ $$= 2\pi\left(\frac{8\sqrt{2} - 12}{5} + \frac{3}{e}\right).$$
Class Notes (Ashraful & Adib Ex 40) Horizontal Shifted Axis $y = 3$
Find the volume of the solid obtained when the region enclosed by $y = \sqrt{x}$, $y = 0$, and $x = 9$ is revolved about the line $y = 3$ using the Washer Method.
Hint: Because the axis $y = 3$ lies above the region, the outer boundary is $y = 0$ ($R_{\text{out}} = 3$) and the inner boundary is $y = \sqrt{x}$ ($R_{\text{in}} = 3 - \sqrt{x}$)!
Step 1: Identify Outer & Inner Radii from $y = 3$
On $x \in [0, 9]$, $0 \le \sqrt{x} \le 3$: $$R_{\text{out}} = 3 - 0 = 3, \qquad R_{\text{in}} = 3 - \sqrt{x}.$$
Step 2: Evaluate Washer Integral ($dx$)
$$V = \pi\int_0^9 \left[3^2 - (3 - \sqrt{x})^2\right]dx = \pi\int_0^9 \left(9 - (9 - 6\sqrt{x} + x)\right)dx = \pi\int_0^9 \left(6x^{1/2} - x\right)dx$$ $$= \pi\left[4x^{3/2} - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^9 = \pi\left(4\cdot 27 - \frac{81}{2}\right) = \pi\left(108 - \frac{81}{2}\right) = \frac{135\pi}{2}.$$
Class Notes (Adib & Ashraful Ex 12) Shell Method + $u$-Substitution
Use cylindrical shells to find the volume of the solid generated when the region under $y = e^{x^2}$ over $[1, \sqrt{3}]$ above the $x$-axis is revolved about the $y$-axis.
Hint: Shell ($dx$) introduces the factor $x$ in $2\pi x e^{x^2}\,dx$, which makes the substitution $u = x^2$ ($du = 2x\,dx$) effortless!
Step 1: Cylindrical Shell Setup ($dx$)
For $x \in [1, \sqrt{3}]$, radius $r(x) = x$ and height $h(x) = e^{x^2}$: $$V = \int_1^{\sqrt{3}} 2\pi x\, e^{x^2}\,dx.$$
Step 2: Substitute $u = x^2$ and evaluate
Let $u = x^2 \implies du = 2x\,dx$. Limits: $x = 1 \Rightarrow u = 1$, $x = \sqrt{3} \Rightarrow u = 3$: $$V = \pi\int_1^3 e^u\,du = \pi\big[e^u\big]_1^3 = \pi\left(e^3 - e\right) \approx 54.56.$$
