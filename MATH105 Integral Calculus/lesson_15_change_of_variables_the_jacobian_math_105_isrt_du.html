3 Core Theory, 2D & 3D Formulas & ISRT Exam Traps

1. 2D Change of Variables

If $T(u,v) = (x(u,v), y(u,v))$ maps $S$ one-to-one onto $R$ with continuous partials:

$$\iint_R f(x, y)\,dA_{xy} = \iint_S f\big(x(u,v), y(u,v)\big)\left|\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}\right|dA_{uv}$$

The factor $|J|$ compensates for local area stretching or shrinking.

2. Jacobian & Inverse Property

$$J = \frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} = \det\begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{\partial x}{\partial u} & \dfrac{\partial x}{\partial v} \\[8pt] \dfrac{\partial y}{\partial u} & \dfrac{\partial y}{\partial v} \end{pmatrix} = x_u y_v - x_v y_u$$ $$\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} = \left(\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y)}\right)^{-1}$$

3. 3D Unification & Ellipsoids

All earlier coordinate systems are special cases of the Jacobian!

$$\text{Polar: } |J| = r, \qquad \text{Cylindrical: } |J| = r$$ $$\text{Spherical: } |J| = \rho^2\sin\phi$$ $$\text{Ellipsoid } (au, bv, cw): |J| = abc$$

⚠ 4 High-Yield ISRT Exam Traps

Trap 1: Forgetting the Absolute Value Bars $|J|$ In Final 2025 & 2023 Q7(a), $J = -\frac{1}{8}$. You must take the absolute value $|J| = +\frac{1}{8}$, otherwise your final integral will have the wrong sign!
Trap 2: Forgetting to Invert $\partial(u,v)/\partial(x,y)$ When using the Inverse Jacobian shortcut (e.g., $\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y)} = -2u$), don't multiply by $2u$ — take the reciprocal $|J| = \frac{1}{2u}$!
Trap 3: Leaving $x$ and $y$ Inside the $uv$-Integral Every occurrence of $x$ and $y$ in the integrand AND in $|J|$ must be rewritten purely in terms of $u$ and $v$ before integrating with respect to $du\,dv$.
Trap 4: Forgetting $r$ When Chaining Ellipse → Polar In Final 2024 Q7(b), $x=3u, y=4v$ gives $dA_{xy} = 12\,dA_{uv}$. When you next switch $S$ (unit disk) to polar coordinates, $dA_{uv} = r\,dr\,d\theta$, so the total area element is $12r\,dr\,d\theta$!
