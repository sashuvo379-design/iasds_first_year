3 Core Theory, Conversion Cheat Sheet & ISRT Exam Traps

1. Polar Double Integral Theorem (Section 14.3)

If $R$ is a simple polar region bounded by $\alpha \le \theta \le \beta$ and $r_1(\theta) \le r \le r_2(\theta)$, and $f$ is continuous on $R$:

$$\iint_R f(x, y)\,dA = \int_\alpha^\beta \int_{r_1(\theta)}^{r_2(\theta)} f(r\cos\theta, r\sin\theta)\,r\,dr\,d\theta$$

Setting $f = 1$ gives the polar area formula: $\displaystyle A = \iint_R 1\,dA = \int_\alpha^\beta \int_{r_1(\theta)}^{r_2(\theta)} r\,dr\,d\theta = \frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta [r_2^2 - r_1^2]\,d\theta$.

2. Cartesian → Polar Conversion Dictionary

$$x = r\cos\theta,\quad y = r\sin\theta,\quad x^2 + y^2 = r^2,\quad dA = r\,dr\,d\theta$$
Cartesian BoundaryPolar Equivalent
Vertical line $x = a$$r\cos\theta = a \implies r = a\sec\theta$
Horizontal line $y = b$$r\sin\theta = b \implies r = b\csc\theta$
Shifted circle $(x-a)^2 + y^2 = a^2$$x^2+y^2 = 2ax \implies r = 2a\cos\theta$
Diagonal line $y = x$ (Q1)$\tan\theta = 1 \implies \theta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$

⚠ 4 High-Yield ISRT Exam Traps

Trap 1: Forgetting the Factor $r$ in $dA$ Always replace $dx\,dy$ by $r\,dr\,d\theta$, never $dr\,d\theta$! Missing $r$ breaks both the geometry and your $u$-substitution (e.g., $\frac{r}{1+r^2}$ or $r e^{-r^2}$).
Trap 2: Assuming $r$ Always Starts at $0$ $r$ starts at $0$ only when the pole $(0,0)$ is inside or on the boundary of $R$. Outside $r=2$ or right of $x=1$, your lower limit is $r_1(\theta) = 2$ or $r_1(\theta) = \sec\theta$!
Trap 3: Double-Counting Odd-Petal Roses In Final 2023 Q5(b) ($r = \sin 3\theta$), integrating $\theta$ from $0$ to $2\pi$ traces every petal twice! Integrate 1 petal on $[0, \pi/3]$ and multiply by $3$.
Trap 4: Wrong $\theta$-Limits on Shifted Circles The shifted circle $(x-1)^2+y^2=1$ ($r = 2\cos\theta$) lies entirely in Q1 & Q4 and is traced once on $[-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}]$ (NOT $[0, 2\pi]$). Above $y = x$, the limits are $\frac{\pi}{4} \le \theta \le \frac{\pi}{2}$.
