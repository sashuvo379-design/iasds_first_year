3 Core Theory, Exact Exam Assumptions & Traps

1. Green's Theorem & Mandatory 4-Point Assumptions

$$\oint_C \big(f(x,y)\,dx + g(x,y)\,dy\big) = \iint_R \left(\frac{\partial g}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)dA$$

State all 4 exact hypotheses whenever an ISRT exam asks for assumptions on $C, R, f, g$:

  • 1. Region $R$: $R$ is a simply connected plane region (connected with no holes).
  • 2. Boundary $C$: $C$ is a simple, closed, piecewise smooth boundary curve of $R$.
  • 3. Orientation: $C$ is oriented counterclockwise (positively oriented, keeping $R$ on the left).
  • 4. Functions $f, g$: $f(x,y)$ and $g(x,y)$ are continuous and have continuous first-order partial derivatives on an open region containing $R$.

2. Area Formulas & Multiply Connected Regions

Choosing $f, g$ so that $\frac{\partial g}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 1$ yields the 3 line-integral Area formulas:

$$A = \oint_C x\,dy = -\oint_C y\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\oint_C (x\,dy - y\,dx)$$

Extended Green's Theorem (Region $R$ with a hole): Keep $R$ on your left as you walk along every boundary component (outer $C_{\text{out}}$ CCW, inner hole $C_{\text{in}}$ CW):

$$\oint_{C_{\text{out}}\,(\text{CCW})} (f\,dx+g\,dy) + \oint_{C_{\text{in}}\,(\text{CW})} (f\,dx+g\,dy) = \iint_R \left(\frac{\partial g}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)dA$$

⚠ 4 High-Yield ISRT Exam Traps

Trap 1: Clockwise Orientation Trap Green's Theorem assumes counterclockwise orientation! If $C$ is oriented clockwise, you must multiply the double integral by $-1$.
Trap 2: Applying Green's Across a Singularity In Final 2025 Q8(b), $\frac{\partial g}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 0$ everywhere except $(0,0)$. Blindly concluding $\oint_C = 0$ when $C$ encloses $(0,0)$ is wrong — you must punch out a tiny CW circle $C_a$, giving $2\pi$!
Trap 3: Reversing the Partial Derivatives The integrand is $\mathbf{\frac{\partial g}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}}$ (second component w.r.t. $x$, minus first component w.r.t. $y$). Reversing it flips the sign of your entire answer!
Trap 4: Losing Easy Marks on Assumptions ISRT finals (2023, 2024, 2025) dedicate 2–3 full marks purely to stating assumptions on $C, R, f, g$. Never omit "piecewise smooth", "counterclockwise", or "continuous first-order partials"!
