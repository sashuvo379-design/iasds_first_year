3 Core Theory, Volume Elements & ISRT Exam Traps

1. Rectangular $(x, y, z)$

$$dV = dz\,dy\,dx$$ $$\iiint_G f(x,y,z)\,dV = \iint_R \left[\int_{g_1(x,y)}^{g_2(x,y)} f\,dz\right]dA$$

Ranges: $x, y, z \in \mathbb{R}$. Best for boxes, planes, tetrahedra, and linear wedges.

2. Cylindrical $(r, \theta, z)$

$$x = r\cos\theta,\quad y = r\sin\theta,\quad z = z$$ $$r^2 = x^2 + y^2,\qquad dV = r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta$$

Ranges: $r \ge 0,\; 0 \le \theta \le 2\pi,\; z \in \mathbb{R}$. Polar coordinates in $xy$ + vertical height $z$.

3. Spherical $(\rho, \theta, \phi)$

$$x = \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta,\quad y = \rho\sin\phi\sin\theta$$ $$z = \rho\cos\phi,\quad \rho^2 = x^2+y^2+z^2$$ $$dV = \rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$$

Ranges: $\rho \ge 0,\; 0 \le \theta \le 2\pi,\; 0 \le \phi \le \pi$ ($\phi$ measured from $+z$-axis).

Decision Matrix: When to Choose Rectangular vs. Cylindrical vs. Spherical

Coordinate System Ideal Solid Geometry Tell-Tale Integrand Clues ISRT Exam Benchmarks
Rectangular Boxes, coordinate planes, tetrahedra, linear wedges Polynomials $12xy^2z^3$, $z$, separable in $x, y, z$ Class Notes Ex 1 (Box), Ex 2 (Wedge)
Cylindrical Circular cylinders, paraboloids ($z=x^2+y^2$), solids symmetric around the $z$-axis Expressions with $x^2 + y^2$ and separate $z$ Final 2025 Q6b ($z=x^2+y^2$ & $z=9$), 2nd Incourse 2025 Q5
Spherical Spheres centered at origin, ice-cream cones ($\phi = \phi_0$), hemispheres, octants Expressions with $x^2+y^2+z^2$ (e.g., $e^{-(x^2+y^2+z^2)^{3/2}}$, $\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}$) Final 2025 Q6c, 2nd Incourse 2025 Q6, Final 2018 Q8a

⚠ 4 High-Yield ISRT Exam Traps

Trap 1: Anton $(\rho, \theta, \phi)$ Notation ISRT follows Howard Anton's order $(\rho, \theta, \phi)$: $\theta$ is the horizontal azimuthal angle in the $xy$-plane, and $\phi$ is the vertical polar angle from the $+z$-axis (physics books often swap $\theta$ and $\phi$!).
Trap 2: Forgetting the Jacobian Factor Never write $dz\,dr\,d\theta$ or $d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$! Cylindrical requires $r$ ($dV = r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta$) and Spherical requires $\rho^2\sin\phi$ ($dV = \rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$).
Trap 3: Half-Ball / Quarter-Ball $\theta$-Limits Always check the signs of $x$ and $y$! In Final 2025 Q6c, $y \in [0, \sqrt{1-x^2}]$ ($y \ge 0$) gives $0 \le \theta \le \pi$. In 2nd Incourse 2025 Q6, $x \in [0, \sqrt{9-y^2}]$ ($x \ge 0$) gives $-\frac{\pi}{2} \le \theta \le \frac{\pi}{2}$ (NOT $0$ to $2\pi$!).
Trap 4: Cone-Sphere Intersection Radius For $x^2+y^2+z^2 = 1$ and $z = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ (2nd Incourse 2025 Q5), substituting $z = r$ gives $r^2 + r^2 = 1 \implies 2r^2 = 1 \implies r = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ (NOT $r = 1$!).
