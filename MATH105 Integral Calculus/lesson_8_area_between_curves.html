Section 03 • Exam-Ready Foundations

Core Theory, Decision Guide & ISRT Exam Traps

The two area integral formulas, a fast $dx$ vs. $dy$ decision table, the Birth/Death Rate area interpretation (Final 2024 Q2a), and the 4 classic ISRT exam traps.

1. Vertical Slicing ($dx$ — Top minus Bottom)

If $f_{\text{top}}(x) \ge g_{\text{bottom}}(x)$ are continuous on $[a, b]$, slice vertically with width $dx$ and height $[f_{\text{top}}(x) - g_{\text{bottom}}(x)]$:

$$A = \int_a^b \big[f_{\text{top}}(x) - g_{\text{bottom}}(x)\big]\,dx$$

Limits $a, b$ are $x$-coordinates of the leftmost and rightmost intersections. If curves cross at $x = c \in (a, b)$, split into $\int_a^c + \int_c^b$.

2. Horizontal Slicing ($dy$ — Right minus Left)

If $w_{\text{right}}(y) \ge v_{\text{left}}(y)$ are continuous on $[c, d]$, slice horizontally with thickness $dy$ and length $[w_{\text{right}}(y) - v_{\text{left}}(y)]$:

$$A = \int_c^d \big[w_{\text{right}}(y) - v_{\text{left}}(y)\big]\,dy$$

Limits $c, d$ are $y$-coordinates of the lowest and highest intersections. Ideal whenever equations are naturally given as $x = w(y)$.

3. Quick Decision Guide: When to Choose $dx$ vs. $dy$

Diagnostic Criterion Choose Vertical Strips ($dx$) Choose Horizontal Strips ($dy$)
Curve Equations Given explicitly as $y = f(x)$ (e.g., $y = 9 - (x/2)^2$, $y = e^{2x}$) Given as $x = w(y)$ or sideways parabolas ($x = y^2$, $y^2 = 4x$)
Boundary Switches Every vertical line hits the same top curve and same bottom curve Every horizontal line hits the same right curve and same left curve
Intersection Solving Set $f(x) = g(x)$ to find $x$-limits $[a, b]$ Set $w(y) = v(y)$ to find $y$-limits $[c, d]$
ISRT Exam Examples Final 2017 Q7a ($e^{2x}, e^x$), 2nd Incourse 2022 Q3 ($9-(x/2)^2, 6-x$) Final 2024 Q4b ($y^2=4x, y=2x-4$), 2nd Incourse 2024 Q2 ($x=y^2, y=x-2$)
Applied Interpretation • Final 2024 Q2(a)

Area Between Rate-of-Change Curves

Let $b(t)$ be the birth rate and $d(t)$ be the death rate (in millions of people/year). Since $P'(t) = b(t) - d(t)$ is the net rate of population growth, the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus gives:

$$A = \int_{1960}^{2010} \big[b(t) - d(t)\big]\,dt = \int_{1960}^{2010} P'(t)\,dt = P(2010) - P(1960)$$

Meaning: The shaded area $A$ represents the net increase in population (in millions of people) over the 50-year period from 1960 to 2010.

⚠ 4 High-Yield ISRT Exam Traps

Trap 1: Integrating Across a Crossing

Integrating $\int_0^2 (1-x^2)\,dx$ straight across the crossing at $x=1$ gives $-\frac{2}{3}$ (net signed difference) instead of the total enclosed area $2$. Always solve $f(x) = g(x)$ inside $[a, b]$ and split!

Trap 2: Forcing $dx$ on Sideways Parabolas

In $x = y^2$ vs. $y = x - 2$, forcing $dx$ forces 2 messy radical integrals ($\int_0^1 2\sqrt{x}\,dx + \int_1^4 [\sqrt{x}-(x-2)]\,dx$). Switching to $dy$ gives 1 clean polynomial integral $\int_{-1}^2 [(y+2)-y^2]\,dy$!

Trap 3: Misreading $x = \sqrt{y}$ as $y = \sqrt{x}$

In Final 2025 Q4(a), the curve is $x = \sqrt{y}$, which means $y = x^2$ ($x \ge 0$), NOT $y = \sqrt{x}$! Squaring carefully avoids losing all 4 marks.

Trap 4: Subtracting in the Wrong Order

Always subtract Top − Bottom for $dx$ and Right − Left for $dy$. If your final area comes out negative, you reversed the subtraction order!

Final 2025 Q4(a) 4 Marks • Vertical Bisector $x = k$
Find a vertical line $x = k$ that divides the area enclosed by $x = \sqrt{y}$, $x = 2$, and $y = 0$ into two equal parts.
Hint: Carefully rewrite $x = \sqrt{y}$ as $y = x^2$ (for $x \ge 0$). Find total area over $[0, 2]$, then set $\int_0^k x^2\,dx = \frac{1}{2}A_{\text{total}}$.
Step 1: Rewrite curve and compute Total Area
The equation $x = \sqrt{y}$ is equivalent to $y = x^2$ for $x \ge 0$. Over $x \in [0, 2]$ above $y = 0$: $$A_{\text{total}} = \int_0^2 x^2\,dx = \left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^2 = \frac{8}{3}.$$
Step 2: Set left-half area equal to $\frac{1}{2}A_{\text{total}} = \frac{4}{3}$
$$A_1(k) = \int_0^k x^2\,dx = \frac{k^3}{3} = \frac{4}{3} \implies k^3 = 4 \implies k = \sqrt[3]{4} \approx 1.5874.$$
Final 2024 Q4(b) 4 Marks • Horizontal Slicing ($dy$)
Find the area of the region enclosed by $y^2 = 4x$ and $y = 2x - 4$.
Hint: Express both curves as $x(y)$: $x_{\text{left}} = \frac{y^2}{4}$ and $x_{\text{right}} = \frac{y+4}{2}$. Solve $\frac{y^2}{4} = \frac{y+4}{2}$ for $y$-limits!
Step 1: Express as $x(y)$ and find intersection $y$-values
$$x = \frac{y^2}{4} \quad \text{and} \quad x = \frac{y + 4}{2}.$$ Setting them equal: $\frac{y^2}{4} = \frac{y + 4}{2} \implies y^2 - 2y - 8 = 0 \implies (y - 4)(y + 2) = 0 \implies y = -2,\; 4$.
Intersections: $(1, -2)$ and $(4, 4)$.
Step 2: Integrate (Right − Left) with respect to $y$
$$A = \int_{-2}^{4} \left(\frac{y + 4}{2} - \frac{y^2}{4}\right)dy = \left[\frac{y^2}{4} + 2y - \frac{y^3}{12}\right]_{-2}^{4}$$ $$= \left(4 + 8 - \frac{64}{12}\right) - \left(1 - 4 + \frac{8}{12}\right) = \left(12 - \frac{16}{3}\right) - \left(-3 + \frac{2}{3}\right) = 15 - 6 = 9.$$
2nd Incourse 2024 Q2 / Ashraful Notes 4 Marks • Horizontal Slicing ($dy$)
Find the area of the region enclosed by $x = y^2$ and $y = x - 2$.
Hint: Rewrite the line as $x = y + 2$. Solve $y^2 = y + 2$ to get $y \in [-1, 2]$, then integrate $[(y + 2) - y^2]\,dy$ in one clean step!
Step 1: Find intersections in terms of $y$
Setting $y^2 = y + 2 \implies y^2 - y - 2 = 0 \implies (y - 2)(y + 1) = 0 \implies y = -1,\; 2$.
Intersection points: $(1, -1)$ and $(4, 2)$. On $[-1, 2]$, $x_{\text{right}} = y + 2 \ge x_{\text{left}} = y^2$.
Step 2: Evaluate the single $dy$-integral
$$A = \int_{-1}^{2} \big[(y + 2) - y^2\big]\,dy = \left[\frac{y^2}{2} + 2y - \frac{y^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{2}$$ $$= \left(2 + 4 - \frac{8}{3}\right) - \left(\frac{1}{2} - 2 + \frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{10}{3} - \left(-\frac{7}{6}\right) = \frac{27}{6} = \frac{9}{2}.$$
Final 2024 Q2(a) 3 Marks • Rate-of-Change Area
The curves model birth rates $b(t)$ and death rates $d(t)$ (in millions of people per year) for a country over $[1960, 2010]$ (with $b(t) > d(t)$). What does the area $A$ between the curves over $[1960, 2010]$ represent? Justify using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
Hint: Recall that $\int_{1960}^{2010} b(t)\,dt$ is total births and $\int_{1960}^{2010} d(t)\,dt$ is total deaths. What is their difference by FTC?
Step 1: Express $A$ as a definite integral
Since $b(t) \ge d(t)$ on $[1960, 2010]$: $$A = \int_{1960}^{2010} \big[b(t) - d(t)\big]\,dt = \int_{1960}^{2010} b(t)\,dt - \int_{1960}^{2010} d(t)\,dt.$$
Step 2: Apply Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (Net Change)
Let $P(t)$ be the country's population at year $t$ (in millions). Assuming negligible net migration, $P'(t) = b(t) - d(t)$. By FTC Part I: $$A = \int_{1960}^{2010} P'(t)\,dt = P(2010) - P(1960).$$ Therefore, $A$ represents the net increase in population (in millions) from 1960 to 2010.
2nd Incourse 2022 Q3 5 Marks • Vertical Slicing ($dx$)
Draw the region bounded above by $f(x) = 9 - \left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^2$ and below by $g(x) = 6 - x$, and find its area.
Hint: Set $9 - \frac{x^2}{4} = 6 - x$, multiply by $4$ to get $x^2 - 4x - 12 = 0$, and integrate $[f(x) - g(x)]\,dx$ over $[-2, 6]$.
Step 1: Find intersection points
$$9 - \frac{x^2}{4} = 6 - x \implies \frac{x^2}{4} - x - 3 = 0 \implies x^2 - 4x - 12 = 0 \implies (x - 6)(x + 2) = 0.$$ Thus $x = -2$ (giving $(-2, 8)$) and $x = 6$ (giving $(6, 0)$). The parabola opens downward with vertex $(0, 9)$.
Step 2: Integrate (Top − Bottom) over $[-2, 6]$
$$A = \int_{-2}^{6} \left[\left(9 - \frac{x^2}{4}\right) - (6 - x)\right]dx = \int_{-2}^{6} \left(3 + x - \frac{x^2}{4}\right)dx$$ $$= \left[3x + \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{12}\right]_{-2}^{6} = (18 + 18 - 18) - \left(-6 + 2 + \frac{2}{3}\right) = 18 + \frac{10}{3} = \frac{64}{3}.$$
Final 2017 Q7(a) 5 Marks • Exponential Curves ($dx$)
Sketch the region enclosed by $y = e^x$, $y = e^{2x}$, $x = 0$, and $x = \ln 2$, and find its area.
Hint: For $x > 0$, $e^{2x} > e^x$. At $x = 0$ both pass through $(0, 1)$; at $x = \ln 2$, $e^x = 2$ and $e^{2x} = 4$.
Step 1: Identify upper and lower curves on $[0, \ln 2]$
Since $2x \ge x$ for all $x \ge 0$, $y = e^{2x}$ lies above $y = e^x$ on $[0, \ln 2]$ (meeting at $(0, 1)$, and reaching $( \ln 2, 4)$ and $(\ln 2, 2)$ on the right).
Step 2: Evaluate the $dx$-integral
$$A = \int_0^{\ln 2} \left(e^{2x} - e^x\right)dx = \left[\frac{1}{2}e^{2x} - e^x\right]_0^{\ln 2}$$ $$= \left(\frac{1}{2}\cdot 4 - 2\right) - \left(\frac{1}{2}\cdot 1 - 1\right) = (2 - 2) - \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{2}.$$
Class Notes (Adib & Ashraful, Anton 6.1 Ex 35) Horizontal Bisector $y = k$
Find a horizontal line $y = k$ that divides the area enclosed by $y = x^2$ and $y = 9$ into two equal parts.
Hint: Total area is $\int_{-3}^3 (9 - x^2)\,dx = 36$. For the lower region bounded by $y = x^2$ and $y = k$, integrate $2\sqrt{y}\,dy$ from $0$ to $k$ (or $k - x^2$ from $-\sqrt{k}$ to $\sqrt{k}$) and set equal to $18$.
Step 1: Compute Total Area
Intersections of $y = x^2$ and $y = 9$ are $(-3, 9)$ and $(3, 9)$: $$A_{\text{total}} = \int_{-3}^{3} (9 - x^2)\,dx = \left[9x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-3}^{3} = (27 - 9) - (-27 + 9) = 36.$$
Step 2: Set lower region area equal to $18$ and solve for $k$
Using $x = \pm\sqrt{y}$ (or $\int_{-\sqrt{k}}^{\sqrt{k}}(k - x^2)\,dx$): $$A_1(k) = \int_0^k 2\sqrt{y}\,dy = \left[\frac{4}{3}y^{3/2}\right]_0^k = \frac{4}{3}k^{3/2} = 18$$ $$\implies k^{3/2} = \frac{27}{2} \implies k = \left(\frac{27}{2}\right)^{2/3} = \frac{9}{\sqrt[3]{4}} \approx 5.6696.$$
Class Notes (Adib, Anton 6.1 Ex 37) Slanted Bisector $y = mx$
Find the value of $m$ such that the line $y = mx$ divides the region enclosed by $y = 2x - x^2$ and the $x$-axis into two equal areas.
Hint: The line $y = mx$ intersects $y = 2x - x^2$ at $x = 0$ and $x = 2 - m$. Integrate $[(2x - x^2) - mx]\,dx$ from $0$ to $2 - m$ and equate to half the total area ($\frac{2}{3}$).
Step 1: Compute Total Area under $y = 2x - x^2$ on $[0, 2]$
$$A_{\text{total}} = \int_0^2 (2x - x^2)\,dx = \left[x^2 - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^2 = 4 - \frac{8}{3} = \frac{4}{3} \implies \text{Half Area} = \frac{2}{3}.$$
Step 2: Find intersections with $y = mx$ and set $A_1(m) = \frac{2}{3}$
$2x - x^2 = mx \implies x(2 - m - x) = 0 \implies x = 0$ and $x = 2 - m$ (for $0 < m < 2$): $$A_1(m) = \int_0^{2-m} \big[(2 - m)x - x^2\big]\,dx = \left[\frac{(2-m)x^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^{2-m} = \frac{(2 - m)^3}{6}.$$ Setting $\dfrac{(2 - m)^3}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3} \implies (2 - m)^3 = 4 \implies m = 2 - \sqrt[3]{4} \approx 0.4126$.
