Section 3 • Exam-Ready Theory

Core Theory, Definite $u$-Substitution & ISRT Exam Traps

Every theorem statement, chain-rule extension, domain rule, and $u$-substitution limit conversion required for full marks.

1. FTC Part I & Total vs. Net Signed Area

If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and $F$ is any antiderivative of $f$ on $[a, b]$ ($F'(x) = f(x)$), then:

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a) = \big[F(x)\big]_a^b$$

For Total Area, find all roots of $f(x) = 0$ inside $[a, b]$ and integrate $|f(x)|$: $$\text{Total Area} = \int_a^b |f(x)|\,dx$$ splitting at every sign change so negative regions are negated.

2. FTC Part II & Leibniz / Chain Rule Extensions

If $f$ is continuous on an open interval $I$ containing $a$, then for every $x \in I$:

$$\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt = f(x)$$ $$\frac{d}{dx}\int_x^a f(t)\,dt = -\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt = -f(x)$$ $$\frac{d}{dx}\int_{g(x)}^{h(x)} f(t)\,dt = f\big(h(x)\big)h'(x) - f\big(g(x)\big)g'(x)$$

3. Maximal Open Interval of Antiderivatives

An integral $F(x) = \int_a^x f(t)\,dt$ represents an antiderivative of $f$ only on the largest open interval containing $a$ on which $f$ is continuous:

• $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_1^x \frac{dt}{t}$ has discontinuity at $t=0$ $\implies$ valid on $(0, \infty)$.
• $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_1^x \frac{dt}{t^2 - 9}$ has discontinuities at $t = \pm 3$ $\implies$ since $1 \in (-3, 3)$, valid on $(-3, 3)$.

$x$-Intercept Rule: Since $F(a) = \int_a^a f(t)\,dt = 0$, the graph of $y = F(x)$ always crosses the $x$-axis at $(a, 0)$.

4. MVT for Integrals, Average Value & Net Change

If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, there exists at least one $x^* \in [a, b]$ such that:

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = f(x^*)(b - a), \qquad f_{\text{ave}} = \frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$ $$\text{Net Change:}\quad \int_a^b F'(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a)$$

Integrating a rate of change (like velocity or birth minus death rate) over $[a, b]$ gives the exact net change in the quantity.

⚠ 4 High-Yield ISRT Exam Traps

Trap 1: Lower-Limit Minus Sign

In 1st In-course 2023 Q4 & Final 2023 Q1(d), $x$ is the lower limit: $F(x) = \int_x^a f(t)\,dt$. Always rewrite as $-\int_a^x f(t)\,dt$ first, so $F'(x) = -f(x)$ and $F''(x) = -f'(x)$!

Trap 2: Absolute-Value Root Splitting

Never integrate $|2 - x|$ or $1 - x^2$ straight across a sign change when asked for Total Area. Always locate the root ($x=2$ or $x=1$) and split into separate integrals.

Trap 3: Discontinuity / FTC Part I Trap

FTC Part I requires $f$ to be continuous on $[a,b]$. Blindly evaluating $\int_{-1}^1 x^{-2}\,dx = [-1/x]_{-1}^1 = -2$ is wrong because $1/x^2$ is unbounded at $x = 0$!

Trap 4: Forgetting to Convert $u$-Limits

When substituting $u = g(x)$ in a definite integral, immediately update $x=a \to u=g(a)$ and $x=b \to u=g(b)$ (never write $\int_0^1 u^{3/2}\,du$ with old $x$-limits).

Final 2025 Q1(d) • 2 Marks FTC Part II
Let $\displaystyle F(x) = \int_4^x \sqrt{t^2 + 9}\,dt$. Evaluate $F(4)$, $F'(4)$, and $F''(4)$ using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (Part II).
Hint: Since $x$ is the upper limit, $F'(x) = \sqrt{x^2+9}$ directly. Then differentiate $F'(x)$ once more using the chain rule to get $F''(x)$.
Step 1: Evaluate $F(4)$
$$F(4) = \int_4^4 \sqrt{t^2 + 9}\,dt = 0.$$
Step 2: Apply FTC Part II for $F'(x)$ and $F'(4)$
Since $f(t) = \sqrt{t^2 + 9}$ is continuous everywhere: $$F'(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + 9} \implies F'(4) = \sqrt{4^2 + 9} = \sqrt{25} = 5.$$
Step 3: Differentiate again for $F''(x)$ and $F''(4)$
$$F''(x) = \frac{d}{dx}(x^2 + 9)^{1/2} = \frac{2x}{2\sqrt{x^2 + 9}} = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}} \implies F''(4) = \frac{4}{5}.$$
1st In-course 2023 Q4 • 4 Marks FTC Part II (Reversed Limit)
Let $\displaystyle F(x) = \int_x^4 \sqrt{t^2 + 4}\,dt$. Find (a) $F(4)$, (b) $F'(4)$, and (c) $F''(4)$.
Hint: Watch the lower-limit trap! Rewrite $F(x) = -\int_4^x \sqrt{t^2 + 4}\,dt$ before applying FTC Part II.
Step 1: Rewrite with $x$ as the upper limit
$$F(x) = -\int_4^x \sqrt{t^2 + 4}\,dt.$$ (a) $F(4) = -\int_4^4 \sqrt{t^2 + 4}\,dt = 0$.
Step 2: Find $F'(4)$
(b) By FTC Part II: $$F'(x) = -\sqrt{x^2 + 4} \implies F'(4) = -\sqrt{4^2 + 4} = -\sqrt{20} = -2\sqrt{5}.$$
Step 3: Find $F''(4)$
(c) Differentiating $F'(x)$: $$F''(x) = -\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 4}} \implies F''(4) = -\frac{4}{2\sqrt{5}} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}.$$
Final 2023 Q1(d) • 2 Marks FTC Part II (Reversed Limit)
Let $\displaystyle F(x) = \int_x^0 t\sec t\,dt$. Evaluate $F(0)$, $F'(0)$, and $F''(0)$ using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (Part II).
Hint: Flip limits to get $F(x) = -\int_0^x t\sec t\,dt$, then use the product rule for $F''(x)$ and recall $\sec 0 = 1$, $\tan 0 = 0$.
Step 1: Evaluate $F(0)$ and $F'(0)$
Rewrite $F(x) = -\int_0^x t\sec t\,dt$. Note $t\sec t$ is continuous on $(-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2})$ containing $0$. $$F(0) = -\int_0^0 t\sec t\,dt = 0.$$ $$F'(x) = -x\sec x \implies F'(0) = -0 \cdot \sec 0 = 0.$$
Step 2: Apply Product Rule for $F''(0)$
$$F''(x) = -\frac{d}{dx}(x\sec x) = -\big(\sec x + x\sec x\tan x\big).$$ $$F''(0) = -(1 + 0 \cdot 1 \cdot 0) = -1.$$
Final 2024 Q4(a) • 3 Marks FTC Part II + Optimization
Let $\displaystyle F(x) = \int_0^x \frac{t - 3}{t^2 + 7}\,dt$ for $x \in (-\infty, \infty)$. Use the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (Part II) to find the value of $x$ where $F$ attains its minimum value.
Hint: Compute $F'(x)$ by FTC Part II, solve $F'(x) = 0$ for the critical point, and use the First Derivative Test (sign change from negative to positive) to prove it is a global minimum.
Step 1: Differentiate via FTC Part II
Since $f(t) = \frac{t - 3}{t^2 + 7}$ is continuous for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$ ($t^2 + 7 \ge 7 > 0$): $$F'(x) = \frac{x - 3}{x^2 + 7}.$$
Step 2: Locate critical point and apply First Derivative Test
Setting $F'(x) = 0$ gives $x - 3 = 0 \implies x = 3$. Since $x^2 + 7 > 0$ always, the sign of $F'(x)$ depends solely on $(x - 3)$:
• For $x < 3$: $F'(x) < 0$, so $F$ is strictly decreasing on $(-\infty, 3)$.
• For $x > 3$: $F'(x) > 0$, so $F$ is strictly increasing on $(3, \infty)$.
Therefore, $F$ attains its absolute minimum value at $x = 3$.
Final 2025 Q1(c) • 2 Marks Average Value & MVT
Find the average value of the function $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ over the interval $[1, 4]$.
Hint: Use $f_{\text{ave}} = \frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ with $a = 1, b = 4$ and $\int x^{1/2}\,dx = \frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}$.
Step 1: Apply the Average Value Formula
$$f_{\text{ave}} = \frac{1}{4 - 1}\int_1^4 x^{1/2}\,dx = \frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}\right]_1^4.$$
Step 2: Evaluate via FTC Part I
$$f_{\text{ave}} = \frac{2}{9}\left(4^{3/2} - 1^{3/2}\right) = \frac{2}{9}(8 - 1) = \frac{14}{9} \approx 1.5556.$$ (Bonus: MVT point $x^*$ satisfies $\sqrt{x^*} = \frac{14}{9} \implies x^* = \frac{196}{81} \approx 2.4198 \in [1, 4]$.)
Final 2024 Q1(d) & Class Notes • 2 Marks Total Area & FTC Part I
Find the total area between the curve $y = 1 - x^2$ and the $x$-axis over the interval $[0, 2]$.
Hint: $1 - x^2 = 0$ at $x = 1 \in [0, 2]$. Split $\int_0^2 |1 - x^2|\,dx$ into $[0, 1]$ (positive) and $[1, 2]$ (negative).
Step 1: Find root and split the integral
On $[0, 1]$, $1 - x^2 \ge 0$; on $[1, 2]$, $1 - x^2 \le 0$. Thus: $$\text{Total Area} = \int_0^2 |1 - x^2|\,dx = \int_0^1 (1 - x^2)\,dx + \int_1^2 (x^2 - 1)\,dx.$$
Step 2: Evaluate each piece via FTC Part I
$$= \left[x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1 + \left[\frac{x^3}{3} - x\right]_1^2 = \left(1 - \frac{1}{3}\right) + \left[\left(\frac{8}{3} - 2\right) - \left(\frac{1}{3} - 1\right)\right]$$ $$= \frac{2}{3} + \left(\frac{2}{3} + \frac{2}{3}\right) = 2.$$
1st In-course 2023 Q3 & Class Notes • 6 Marks Total Area & Curve Sketch
Sketch the curve of $\displaystyle y = \frac{x^2 - 1}{x^2}$ and find the total area between the curve and the interval $\left[\frac{1}{2}, 2\right]$ on the $x$-axis.
Hint: Rewrite $y = 1 - \frac{1}{x^2}$. Notice it crosses the $x$-axis at $x = 1$: it is negative on $[\frac{1}{2}, 1)$ (from $y = -3$ up to $0$) and positive on $(1, 2]$ (from $0$ up to $\frac{3}{4}$).
Step 1: Analyze & sketch $y = 1 - x^{-2}$ on $[1/2, 2]$
• At $x = \frac{1}{2}$: $y = 1 - 4 = -3$ (below $x$-axis).
• Root at $x = 1$: $y = 0$.
• At $x = 2$: $y = 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$ (above $x$-axis).
Since $y' = \frac{2}{x^3} > 0$, the curve strictly increases from $(\frac{1}{2}, -3)$ through $(1, 0)$ to $(2, \frac{3}{4})$.
Step 2: Split Total Area at $x = 1$ and integrate
$$\text{Total Area} = \int_{1/2}^{1} \left(\frac{1}{x^2} - 1\right)dx + \int_{1}^{2} \left(1 - \frac{1}{x^2}\right)dx$$ $$= \left[-\frac{1}{x} - x\right]_{1/2}^{1} + \left[x + \frac{1}{x}\right]_{1}^{2}$$ $$= \left[(-1 - 1) - \left(-2 - \frac{1}{2}\right)\right] + \left[\left(2 + \frac{1}{2}\right) - (1 + 1)\right] = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 1.$$
1st In-course 2024 Q5(a) & 2nd In-course 2022 Q1(a) Splitting & Definite $u$-Sub
Evaluate both definite integrals:
(i) $\displaystyle \int_1^4 \frac{|2 - x|}{x}\,dx$  (1st In-course 2024 Q5a / Final 2024 Q2b)
(ii) $\displaystyle \int_0^1 5x(1 - x^2)^{3/2}\,dx$  (2nd In-course 2022 Q1a)
Hint: For (i), split at $x = 2$ and watch the $\ln 2$ terms cancel! For (ii), let $u = 1 - x^2$ and convert limits $0 \to 1$ and $1 \to 0$.
Part (i): $\int_1^4 \frac{|2 - x|}{x}\,dx = 1$
Split at $x = 2$ (where $2 - x = 0$): $$\int_1^2 \frac{2 - x}{x}\,dx + \int_2^4 \frac{x - 2}{x}\,dx = \int_1^2 \left(\frac{2}{x} - 1\right)dx + \int_2^4 \left(1 - \frac{2}{x}\right)dx$$ $$= \big[2\ln x - x\big]_1^2 + \big[x - 2\ln x\big]_2^4 = (2\ln 2 - 2 + 1) + (4 - 4\ln 2 - 2 + 2\ln 2)$$ $$= (2\ln 2 - 1) + (2 - 2\ln 2) = 1.$$
Part (ii): $\int_0^1 5x(1 - x^2)^{3/2}\,dx = 1$
Let $u = 1 - x^2 \implies du = -2x\,dx \implies x\,dx = -\frac{1}{2}du$.
Convert limits: $x = 0 \implies u = 1$, and $x = 1 \implies u = 0$: $$\int_1^0 5u^{3/2}\left(-\frac{1}{2}du\right) = \frac{5}{2}\int_0^1 u^{3/2}\,du = \frac{5}{2}\left[\frac{2}{5}u^{5/2}\right]_0^1 = 1 - 0 = 1.$$
