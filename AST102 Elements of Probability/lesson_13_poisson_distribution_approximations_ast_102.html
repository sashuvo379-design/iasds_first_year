01 The Poisson Random Variable & Process Axioms

Counts in Time or Space · $X \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda)$

A discrete random variable $X$ follows a Poisson distribution with rate parameter $\lambda > 0$ if it models the number of rare events occurring in a fixed interval of time, length, area, or volume:

Probability Mass Function (PMF)
$$P(X = x) = \frac{e^{-\lambda}\,\lambda^x}{x!}, \qquad x = 0, 1, 2, \dots \;\; (\lambda > 0)$$
Rate Scaling Over Interval $t$
$$N(t) \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda t) \implies P(N(t) = x) = \frac{e^{-\lambda t}(\lambda t)^x}{x!}$$

If arrivals average $\lambda$ per hour, then in $t$ hours the mean is $\lambda t$.

Four Axioms of a Homogeneous Poisson Process
1. Independent Increments

Counts of events in non-overlapping (disjoint) time or space intervals are statistically independent.

2. Stationary Increments

The distribution of the count in an interval depends only on its length $t$, not on where it starts.

3. Orderliness (One-at-a-Time)

In an infinitesimal interval $h$, $P(N(h) \ge 2) = o(h)$—two events cannot occur simultaneously.

4. Constant Rate $\lambda$

Average intensity $\lambda > 0$ is constant per unit interval, with $P(N(h) = 1) = \lambda h + o(h)$.

02 Equidispersion, Moments, Cumulants & Mode

ISRT Year Final 2024 Q7(b,c)
Equidispersion
$E[X] = V(X) = \lambda$

Unlike Binomial ($V < E$), the Poisson distribution has its mean strictly equal to its variance.

MGF & CGF (All Cumulants $= \lambda$)
$M_X(t) = e^{\lambda(e^t - 1)}$
$K_X(t) = \lambda(e^t - 1) \implies \kappa_r = \lambda$

Every cumulant $\kappa_1, \kappa_2, \kappa_3, \kappa_4, \dots$ equals $\lambda$!

Shape & Mode Rule
$\gamma_1 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}, \qquad \gamma_2 = \dfrac{1}{\lambda}$
Mode: $\lfloor \lambda \rfloor$ (or bimodal $\lambda - 1, \lambda$ if $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^+$)

Always right-skewed & leptokurtic; becomes symmetric ($\gamma_1, \gamma_2 \to 0$) as $\lambda \to \infty$.

03 Two Fundamental Approximations

Connecting Binomial, Poisson & Normal
(a) Law of Rare Events $B(n, p) \approx \text{Pois}(\lambda = np)$

Poisson Approximation to the Binomial

When the number of trials $n$ is large and the success probability $p$ is small such that $\lambda = np$ stays moderate, Binomial probabilities converge to Poisson:

$$\binom{n}{x} p^x (1 - p)^{n-x} \approx \frac{e^{-\lambda}\,\lambda^x}{x!}, \qquad \lambda = np$$

Rule of Thumb: Accurate when $n \ge 20$ and $p \le 0.05$ (excellent when $n \ge 100$ and $np \le 10$).

(b) Large-Rate CLT Limit $\text{Pois}(\lambda) \approx N(\lambda, \lambda)$

Normal Approximation to the Poisson

Because $\text{Pois}(\lambda)$ can be decomposed as a sum of $\lambda$ i.i.d. $\text{Pois}(1)$ variables, the Central Limit Theorem applies for large $\lambda$ ($\lambda \ge 20$):

$$P(X \ge k) \approx P\!\left(Z \ge \frac{(k - 0.5) - \lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right) = 1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{k - 0.5 - \lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)$$

Continuity Correction: Use $k - 0.5$ for $P(X \ge k)$ and $k + 0.5$ for $P(X \le k)$ when $\lambda \ge 20$.

ISRT Exam-Trap Spotlight

Three Traps That Frequently Cost Marks

Trap 1: Forgetting to Scale $\lambda$

Always match the time/area unit of $\lambda$ to the question! If customers arrive at $4$ per hour, then for a $30$-minute window you MUST use $\lambda_{\text{new}} = 4 \times 0.5 = 2$, not $4$.

Trap 2: Strict vs. Inclusive Tails

In ISRT 2024 Q7(d), $P(X > 5) = 1 - P(X \le 5)$ (subtracting $x = 0,1,2,3,4,5$), NOT $1 - P(X \le 4)$! By contrast, $P(X \ge 5) = 1 - P(X \le 4)$.

Trap 3: $X_1 - X_2$ is NOT Poisson

For independent $X_1 \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda_1)$ and $X_2 \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda_2)$, the sum is $X_1 + X_2 \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda_1 + \lambda_2)$, but $X_1 - X_2$ can take negative values (Skellam distribution), so it is never Poisson!

Proof 1 · Maclaurin Series Verification
Valid PMF Verification: $\displaystyle\sum_{x=0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x}{x!} = 1$

For $\lambda > 0$ and $x \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$, clearly $P(X = x) = \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x}{x!} > 0$ (non-negativity holds). Factoring $e^{-\lambda}$ outside the sum and applying the Maclaurin series $e^{\lambda} = \sum_{x=0}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda^x}{x!}$:

$$\sum_{x=0}^{\infty} P(X = x) = e^{-\lambda} \sum_{x=0}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda^x}{x!} = e^{-\lambda} \cdot e^{\lambda} = e^{0} = 1 \quad \blacksquare$$
Proof 2 · ISRT Year Final 2022 Q7(b) & 2024 Q7(b)
MGF, CGF, Mean, Variance, Skewness & Excess Kurtosis of $\text{Poisson}(\lambda)$

1. MGF Derivation:

$$M_X(t) = E[e^{tX}] = \sum_{x=0}^{\infty} e^{tx}\frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x}{x!} = e^{-\lambda}\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda e^t)^x}{x!} = e^{-\lambda}\,e^{\lambda e^t} = \mathbf{e^{\lambda(e^t - 1)}}$$

2. CGF & Cumulants: Take $\ln$ to obtain $K_X(t) = \ln M_X(t) = \lambda(e^t - 1)$. Differentiating $r$ times yields $K_X^{(r)}(t) = \lambda e^t$, so at $t = 0$: $$\kappa_r = K_X^{(r)}(0) = \lambda \qquad \text{for all } r = 1, 2, 3, 4, \dots$$

$$E[X] = \kappa_1 = \lambda, \qquad V(X) = \kappa_2 = \lambda$$ $$\gamma_1 = \frac{\kappa_3}{\kappa_2^{3/2}} = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{3/2}} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}}, \qquad \gamma_2 = \frac{\kappa_4}{\kappa_2^2} = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda^2} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{\lambda}} \quad \blacksquare$$
Proof 3 · ISRT Year Final 2024 Q7(c)
Recurrence Relation of Central Moments: $\mu_{r+1} = r\lambda\mu_{r-1} + \lambda\dfrac{d\mu_r}{d\lambda}$

By definition, $\mu_r = \sum_{x=0}^{\infty} (x - \lambda)^r \dfrac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x}{x!}$. Differentiate both sides with respect to $\lambda$ using the product rule:

$$\frac{d\mu_r}{d\lambda} = \sum_{x=0}^{\infty} \left[ -r(x-\lambda)^{r-1}\frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x}{x!} + (x-\lambda)^r \frac{(-e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x + x e^{-\lambda}\lambda^{x-1})}{x!} \right]$$ $$= -r\mu_{r-1} + \frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{x=0}^{\infty} (x - \lambda)^{r+1}\frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x}{x!} = -r\mu_{r-1} + \frac{\mu_{r+1}}{\lambda}$$

Multiplying through by $\lambda$ and rearranging gives the recurrence relation:

$$\mu_{r+1} = r\lambda\,\mu_{r-1} + \lambda\frac{d\mu_r}{d\lambda}$$

Deriving $\mu_2, \mu_3, \mu_4$ from $\mu_0 = 1, \mu_1 = 0$:

  • r = 1: μ₂ = 1·λ·(1) + λ·d(0)/dλ = λ
  • r = 2: μ₃ = 2·λ·(0) + λ·d(λ)/dλ = λ
  • r = 3: μ₄ = 3·λ·(λ) + λ·d(λ)/dλ = 3λ² + λ = λ + 3λ² ∎
Proof 4 · Ross Section 4.7
Poisson Limit Theorem: $B(n, p) \longrightarrow \text{Pois}(\lambda)$ as $n \to \infty, p \to 0, np = \lambda$

Substitute $p = \lambda / n$ into the Binomial PMF for fixed $x \ge 0$:

$$P(X = x) = \frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-x+1)}{x!}\left(\frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^x \left(1 - \frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^{n-x}$$ $$= \frac{\lambda^x}{x!} \cdot \underbrace{1\left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)\cdots\left(1 - \frac{x-1}{n}\right)}_{\longrightarrow\, 1} \cdot \underbrace{\left(1 - \frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^n}_{\longrightarrow\, e^{-\lambda}} \cdot \underbrace{\left(1 - \frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^{-x}}_{\longrightarrow\, 1}$$

Taking the limit as $n \to \infty$ gives $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} P(X=x) = \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x}{x!}$. $\blacksquare$

Proof 5 · Reproductive Property & Mode Ratio
$X_1 + X_2 \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda_1 + \lambda_2)$ via MGF & Mode Proof via $P(X=x)/P(X=x-1) = \lambda/x$

1. Reproductive Property: For independent $X_1 \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda_1)$ and $X_2 \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda_2)$:

$$M_{X_1+X_2}(t) = M_{X_1}(t)M_{X_2}(t) = e^{\lambda_1(e^t-1)}\,e^{\lambda_2(e^t-1)} = e^{(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)(e^t-1)}$$

By the MGF Uniqueness Theorem, $X_1 + X_2 \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda_1 + \lambda_2)$.

2. Mode Proof: For $x \ge 1$, the ratio of consecutive probabilities is: $$\frac{P(X = x)}{P(X = x - 1)} = \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x / x!}{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^{x-1}/(x-1)!} = \frac{\lambda}{x}$$ Thus $P(X=x) \ge P(X=x-1) \iff \frac{\lambda}{x} \ge 1 \iff x \le \lambda$. Hence $P(X=x)$ increases up to $\lfloor \lambda \rfloor$; if $\lambda$ is an exact positive integer, equality holds at $x = \lambda$, making it bimodal at $\lambda - 1$ and $\lambda$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT Year Final 2024 · Q7(d) Poisson Approximation to Binomial · Website Hits

Suppose 1 in 1,000 visitors to a website subscribe to a newsletter. If the website received $n = 3{,}500$ hits yesterday, use the Poisson approximation to find the probability that more than 5 visitors subscribed.

Toggle Step-by-Step Solution

Here $X \sim B(n = 3500, p = 0.001)$. Since $n$ is very large and $p$ is very small, approximate by $\text{Pois}(\lambda)$ with $\lambda = np = 3500 \times 0.001 = \mathbf{3.5}$.

Careful with the strict inequality $X > 5$: $P(X > 5) = 1 - P(X \le 5) = 1 - \sum_{x=0}^{5} \dfrac{e^{-3.5}(3.5)^x}{x!}$:

e⁻³·⁵ ≈ 0.030197
Sum(x=0..5) = 1 + 3.5 + 6.125 + 7.1458 + 6.2526 + 4.3768 = 28.4003
P(X ≤ 5) = 0.030197 × 28.4003 ≈ 0.8576

Therefore: $P(X > 5) = 1 - 0.8576 = \mathbf{0.1424}$.

ISRT Year Final 2024 · Q7(b) & (c) MGF, Skewness, Kurtosis & Central Moment Recurrence

(b) Derive the MGF of a Poisson$(\lambda)$ random variable and use it to find $\gamma_1 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}$ and $\gamma_2 = \dfrac{1}{\lambda}$.
(c) Establish the recurrence relation for central moments: $\mu_{r+1} = r\lambda\mu_{r-1} + \lambda\dfrac{d\mu_r}{d\lambda}$.

Toggle Step-by-Step Solution

(b) $M_X(t) = e^{\lambda(e^t - 1)} \implies K_X(t) = \lambda(e^t - 1)$. Differentiating at $t=0$ gives $\kappa_1 = \kappa_2 = \kappa_3 = \kappa_4 = \lambda$. Thus $\gamma_1 = \frac{\kappa_3}{\kappa_2^{3/2}} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}}$ and $\gamma_2 = \frac{\kappa_4}{\kappa_2^2} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{\lambda}}$.

(c) Differentiate $\mu_r = \sum_{x=0}^{\infty} (x-\lambda)^r \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x}{x!}$ with respect to $\lambda$ to get $\frac{d\mu_r}{d\lambda} = -r\mu_{r-1} + \frac{\mu_{r+1}}{\lambda}$, then multiply by $\lambda$: $\mu_{r+1} = r\lambda\mu_{r-1} + \lambda\frac{d\mu_r}{d\lambda}$. ISRT Year Final · Q7(a) [4 Marks] Poisson Definition, Properties & Cafe Arrivals

Define a Poisson random variable and state its properties. In a cafe, customers arrive at a mean rate of $4$ per hour. Find the probability of arrival of $6$ customers in the next one hour.

Toggle Step-by-Step Solution

Definition & Properties: $X \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda)$ has PMF $e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x/x!$ ($x=0,1,2,\dots$); properties: equidispersion ($E[X]=V(X)=\lambda$), positively skewed ($\gamma_1 = 1/\sqrt{\lambda}$), reproductive under independent sums, limiting case of Binomial.

Cafe Calculation ($\lambda = 4$, $t = 1$ hr, $x = 6$): $$P(X = 6) = \frac{e^{-4} \cdot 4^6}{6!} = \frac{0.018316 \times 4096}{720} = \mathbf{0.1042}$$

ISRT Year Final · Q7(b) [4 Marks] Sum of i.i.d. Poissons & Scaling

Suppose $X_1, X_2, X_3, X_4$ are identically and independently distributed Poisson random variables with parameter $\lambda$. Let $Y = \sum_{i=1}^4 X_i$. Find $E(Y)$ and $E(kY)$ where $k$ is any constant.

Toggle Step-by-Step Solution

By the reproductive property of independent Poissons, $Y = X_1 + X_2 + X_3 + X_4 \sim \text{Pois}(4\lambda)$.

By linearity of expectation: $$E(Y) = E(X_1) + E(X_2) + E(X_3) + E(X_4) = \lambda + \lambda + \lambda + \lambda = \mathbf{4\lambda}$$ $$E(kY) = k\,E(Y) = \mathbf{4k\lambda}$$

Ross Ch. 8 Classic · Final Prep Normal Approximation to Poisson with Continuity Correction

The number of students enrolling in an introductory psychology course is a Poisson random variable $X \sim \text{Pois}(100)$. Using the Normal approximation with continuity correction, estimate $P(X \ge 120)$.

Toggle Step-by-Step Solution

Since $\lambda = 100 \ge 20$, approximate $X$ by $Y \sim N(\mu = 100, \sigma^2 = 100)$, so $\sigma = \sqrt{100} = 10$.

Apply the continuity correction for $X \ge 120$ (replace $120$ with $119.5$):

$$P(X \ge 120) \approx P(Y \ge 119.5) = P\!\left(Z \ge \frac{119.5 - 100}{10}\right) = 1 - \Phi(1.95) = 1 - 0.9744 = \mathbf{0.0256}$$
