A Continuous Uniform Distribution $U(a, b)$

Flat Density · Foundation of Simulation (Ross Ch. 10)

Models a completely random real number chosen over $[a, b]$ where every sub-interval of equal length carries identical probability. In computational statistics and Monte Carlo simulation (Ross Ch. 10), $U(0, 1)$ is the universal seed from which all other random variables are generated via inverse-CDF transforms.

PDF ($a \le x \le b$)
$f(x) = \dfrac{1}{b - a}$
CDF ($a \le x \le b$)
$F(x) = \dfrac{x - a}{b - a}$
Mean
$E[X] = \dfrac{a + b}{2}$
Variance
$\text{Var}(X) = \dfrac{(b - a)^2}{12}$

B Exponential Distribution $\text{Exp}(\lambda)$

Waiting Time Between Poisson($\lambda$) Events · Year Final 2022 & 2025

If events occur according to a Poisson process at rate $\lambda > 0$, the continuous waiting time $X$ until the first event (or between consecutive events) follows $\text{Exp}(\lambda)$. It is the unique continuous memoryless distribution and has a constant failure (hazard) rate $h(t) = \frac{f(t)}{S(t)} = \lambda$.

PDF, CDF, Survival & Moments ($x \ge 0$)
$f(x) = \lambda e^{-\lambda x}, \qquad F(x) = 1 - e^{-\lambda x}, \qquad S(x) = P(X > x) = e^{-\lambda x}$
$E[X] = \dfrac{1}{\lambda}, \qquad \text{Var}(X) = \dfrac{1}{\lambda^2}, \qquad h(t) = \lambda$
Memoryless & Series/Parallel Reliability
Memoryless: $P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = P(X > t) = e^{-\lambda t}$
Series System (Minimum): For i.i.d. $X_1, \dots, X_n \sim \text{Exp}(\lambda)$, $\min(X_1, \dots, X_n) \sim \text{Exp}(n\lambda)$ with $P(\min \ge a) = e^{-n\lambda a}$.

C Normal (Gaussian) Distribution $N(\mu, \sigma^2)$

Ross §5.4 · ISRT Finals 2022–2025 Core
$$f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right), \qquad -\infty < x < \infty \quad (\sigma > 0)$$
Geometry & 68–95–99.7 Rule

Symmetric bell centered at $\mu = \text{mean} = \text{median} = \text{mode}$. Inflection points sit at $\mu \pm \sigma$; peak height $\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}$ drops as $\sigma$ grows.

±1σ: 68.3% · ±2σ: 95.4% · ±3σ: 99.7%
Standard Normal & Symmetry

Standardize via $Z = \dfrac{X - \mu}{\sigma} \sim N(0, 1)$, so $P(X \le x) = \Phi\!\left(\dfrac{x - \mu}{\sigma}\right)$.

$\Phi(-z) = 1 - \Phi(z)$
Linear Combos & Binomial Approx

Independent $X_i \sim N(\mu_i, \sigma_i^2) \implies \sum c_i X_i \sim N\!\left(\sum c_i\mu_i,\; \sum c_i^2\sigma_i^2\right)$.

$B(n,p) \approx N(np, npq)$ when $np \ge 5, nq \ge 5$ (or $npq \ge 10$) with $\pm 0.5$ continuity correction.

!
ISRT Exam-Trap Spotlight

Three Critical Continuous-Distribution Traps

Trap 1: Variance vs. SD in $N(\mu, \sigma^2)$

ISRT exams write $X \sim N(2, 3)$ meaning variance $\sigma^2 = 3$ (so $\sigma = \sqrt{3}$), NOT $\sigma = 3$! Always verify whether the problem states "variance" or "standard deviation" before dividing by $\sigma$.

Trap 2: Continuity Correction Direction

To include $k$: $P(X \ge k) \approx P(Y > k - 0.5)$ and $P(X \le k) \approx P(Y < k + 0.5)$. To exclude $k$: $P(X > k) \approx P(Y > k + 0.5)$ and $P(X < k) \approx P(Y < k - 0.5)$.

Trap 3: Min vs. Max of i.i.d. Exponentials

$P(\min \ge a) = [P(X_1 \ge a)]^n = e^{-n\lambda a}$ (so $\min \sim \text{Exp}(n\lambda)$), whereas $P(\max \ge a) = 1 - P(\max < a) = 1 - (1 - e^{-\lambda a})^n$. Never confuse $\max$ with $\text{Exp}(\lambda/n)$!

Proof 1 · ISRT Year Final 2025 Q1(d)
Memoryless Property of the Exponential Distribution: $P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = P(X > t)$

Let $X \sim \text{Exp}(\lambda)$ so its survival function is $P(X > x) = \int_x^\infty \lambda e^{-\lambda u}\,du = e^{-\lambda x}$ for $x \ge 0$. For any $s, t \ge 0$, $\{X > s + t\} \subseteq \{X > s\}$:

$$P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = \frac{P(X > s + t \cap X > s)}{P(X > s)} = \frac{P(X > s + t)}{P(X > s)} = \frac{e^{-\lambda(s+t)}}{e^{-\lambda s}} = e^{-\lambda t} = P(X > t) \quad \blacksquare$$
Proof 2 · ISRT Year Final 2023 Q8(c)
Symmetry of the Standard Normal CDF: Prove $\Phi(-z) = 1 - \Phi(z)$

The Standard Normal PDF $\phi(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-t^2/2}$ is an even function ($\phi(-t) = \phi(t)$). Substitute $u = -t$ ($du = -dt$) into $\Phi(-z) = \int_{-\infty}^{-z} \phi(t)\,dt$:

$$\Phi(-z) = \int_{-\infty}^{-z} \phi(t)\,dt = \int_{\infty}^{z} \phi(-u)(-du) = \int_{z}^{\infty} \phi(u)\,du = 1 - \int_{-\infty}^{z} \phi(u)\,du = 1 - \Phi(z) \quad \blacksquare$$
Proof 3 · ISRT Year Final 2023 Q8(b)
Derivation of Mean ($\mu$) and Variance ($\sigma^2$) of $X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$

Write $X = \mu + \sigma Z$ where $Z \sim N(0, 1)$ has PDF $\phi(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-z^2/2}$.

1. Mean of $Z$: Since $z\phi(z)$ is an odd integrable function on $(-\infty, \infty)$, $E[Z] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} z\phi(z)\,dz = 0$.

2. Variance of $Z$ (Integration by Parts): Set $u = z$ and $dv = z e^{-z^2/2}\,dz$ (so $du = dz$ and $v = -e^{-z^2/2}$):

$$E[Z^2] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left[\Big[-z e^{-z^2/2}\Big]_{-\infty}^{\infty} + \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-z^2/2}\,dz\right] = 0 + 1 = 1 \implies \text{Var}(Z) = 1 - 0^2 = 1$$

3. Scale back to $X = \mu + \sigma Z$:

$$E[X] = \mu + \sigma E[Z] = \mathbf{\mu}, \qquad \text{Var}(X) = \sigma^2 \text{Var}(Z) = \mathbf{\sigma^2} \quad \blacksquare$$
Proof 4 · ISRT Year Final 2025 Q8(b) & 2024 Q8(b)
Linear Combination of Independent Normals $Z = aX + bY$ via MGF

Let $X \sim N(\mu_1, \sigma_1^2)$ and $Y \sim N(\mu_2, \sigma_2^2)$ be independent, with $M_X(t) = \exp(\mu_1 t + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_1^2 t^2)$ and $M_Y(t) = \exp(\mu_2 t + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_2^2 t^2)$. For $Z = aX + bY$:

$$M_Z(t) = E[e^{t(aX + bY)}] = M_X(at)\,M_Y(bt) = \exp\!\left(\mu_1(at) + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_1^2 a^2 t^2\right)\exp\!\left(\mu_2(bt) + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_2^2 b^2 t^2\right)$$ $$= \exp\!\left((a\mu_1 + b\mu_2)t + \frac{1}{2}(a^2\sigma_1^2 + b^2\sigma_2^2)t^2\right)$$

By the MGF Uniqueness Theorem, $Z = aX + bY \sim N(a\mu_1 + b\mu_2,\; a^2\sigma_1^2 + b^2\sigma_2^2)$. (For $\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ with i.i.d. $N(\mu, \sigma^2)$, setting $a_i = \frac{1}{n}$ gives $\bar{X} \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2/n)$.) $\blacksquare$

Proof 5 · ISRT Year Final 2022 Q8(c)
Survival Probabilities of $\min(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ and $\max(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ for i.i.d. $\text{Exp}(\lambda)$

1. Minimum (Series System): The minimum exceeds $a$ if and only if every component exceeds $a$:

$$P(\min(X_1, \dots, X_n) \ge a) = P(X_1 \ge a, \dots, X_n \ge a) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} P(X_i \ge a) = (e^{-\lambda a})^n = \mathbf{e^{-n\lambda a}}$$

Recognizing $S_{\min}(a) = e^{-(n\lambda)a}$ immediately proves $\min(X_1, \dots, X_n) \sim \text{Exp}(n\lambda)$.

2. Maximum (Parallel System): Use the complement—the maximum is less than $a$ iff every component is less than $a$:

$$P(\max(X_1, \dots, X_n) \ge a) = 1 - P(\max < a) = 1 - \prod_{i=1}^{n} P(X_i < a) = \mathbf{1 - (1 - e^{-\lambda a})^n} \quad \blacksquare$$

ISRT Year Final 2025 · Q8(c), Q8(e) & Q6(d) IQ Percentile · Symmetry · Sum of Independent Normals

Q8(c): IQ scores follow $N(100, 15^2)$. Find the 30th percentile.

Q8(e): For $X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$, suppose $P(X < 50) = P(X > 70)$ and $P(X > 80) = 0.0228$. Find $\mu$ and $\sigma$.

Q6(d): Let $X \sim N(2, 3)$, $Y \sim N(5, 1)$, and $Z \sim N(20, 21)$ be independent. Find $P(X + Y + Z < 25)$.

Toggle Step-by-Step Solution

Q8(c): We need $z_{0.30}$ such that $\Phi(z_{0.30}) = 0.30$. By symmetry, $\Phi(|z|) = 0.70 \implies z_{0.30} = -0.5244$. Thus: $$x = \mu + z\sigma = 100 + (-0.5244)(15) = \mathbf{92.13}$$

Q8(e): Equal tails $P(X < 50) = P(X > 70)$ imply $\mu$ is the exact midpoint: $\mu = \frac{50 + 70}{2} = \mathbf{60}$. Next, $P(X > 80) = 0.0228 \implies \Phi\!\left(\frac{80 - 60}{\sigma}\right) = 1 - 0.0228 = 0.9772 \implies \frac{20}{\sigma} = 2.00 \implies \sigma = \mathbf{10}$.

Q6(d): Let $W = X + Y + Z$. By independence: $\mu_W = 2 + 5 + 20 = 27$ and $\sigma_W^2 = 3 + 1 + 21 = 25 \implies \sigma_W = 5$, so $W \sim N(27, 25)$. $$P(W < 25) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{25 - 27}{5}\right) = \Phi(-0.40) = 1 - \Phi(0.40) = 1 - 0.6554 = \mathbf{0.3446}$$

ISRT Year Final 2024 · Q8(c) [6 Marks] Metal Rods · Inverse SD & Expected Profit

The length of a metal rod, $L$ cm, follows $N(8, c^2)$. Given $P(L < 7.902) = 2.5\%$: (i) Show that $c = 0.05$ to two decimal places. (ii) Find $P(7.94 < L < 8.09)$. (iii) Production cost is 20p/rod. Rods with $L \le 7.94$ are scrapped for 5p; $7.94 < L \le 8.09$ are sold for 50p; $L > 8.09$ are shortened for an extra 10p and then sold for 50p. Calculate the expected profit per 500 rods to the nearest pound.

Toggle Step-by-Step Solution

(i) $\Phi\!\left(\frac{7.902 - 8}{c}\right) = 0.025 \implies \frac{-0.098}{c} = -1.96 \implies c = \frac{0.098}{1.96} = \mathbf{0.05\text{ cm}}$ ✓.

(ii) $z_1 = \frac{7.94 - 8}{0.05} = -1.20$, $z_2 = \frac{8.09 - 8}{0.05} = 1.80$: $$P(7.94 < L < 8.09) = \Phi(1.80) - \Phi(-1.20) = 0.9641 - (1 - 0.8849) = 0.9641 - 0.1151 = \mathbf{0.8490}$$

(iii) Net profit per rod across the three regimes:

  • L ≤ 7.94 (p = 0.1151): 5p − 20p = −15p
  • 7.94 < L ≤ 8.09 (p = 0.8490): 50p − 20p = +30p
  • L > 8.09 (p = 1 − 0.9641 = 0.0359): 50p − 20p − 10p = +20p

$E[\text{Profit/rod}] = 0.1151(-15) + 0.8490(30) + 0.0359(20) = -1.7265 + 25.47 + 0.718 = \mathbf{24.46\text{p}}$. For 500 rods: $500 \times 24.46\text{p} = 12{,}230\text{p} \approx \mathbf{£122}$.

ISRT Year Final 2022 · Q6(b) [7] & Q6(c) [2] Student Grades & Unknown Mean $\mu$

Q6(b): Grades of 500 students follow $N(72, 10^2)$. Find (i) $P(X > 80)$, (ii) $P(X < 50)$, (iii) $P(50 < X < 80)$, and (iv) approximately how many students scored greater than 80.

Q6(c): Suppose $X \sim N(\mu, 9)$ (variance 9) and $P(X > 5) = 0.1$. Find $\mu$.

Toggle Step-by-Step Solution

Q6(b): (i) $P(X > 80) = 1 - \Phi(0.8) = 1 - 0.7881 = \mathbf{0.2119}$. (ii) $P(X < 50) = \Phi(-2.2) = 1 - 0.9861 = \mathbf{0.0139}$. (iii) $P(50 < X < 80) = 0.7881 - 0.0139 = \mathbf{0.7742}$. (iv) Expected count $= 500 \times 0.2119 \approx \mathbf{106}$ students.

Q6(c): Here $\sigma = \sqrt{9} = 3$. $P(X > 5) = 0.1 \implies \Phi\!\left(\frac{5 - \mu}{3}\right) = 0.9 \implies \frac{5 - \mu}{3} = 1.2816 \implies \mu = 5 - 3(1.2816) = \mathbf{1.155}$.

ISRT Year Final 2022 Q8(c) [4] & 2025 Q1(d) Min/Max of 5 i.i.d. Exponentials

If $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_5$ are i.i.d. $\text{Exp}(\lambda)$ random variables, compute $P(\min(X_1, \dots, X_5) \ge a)$ and $P(\max(X_1, \dots, X_5) \ge a)$ for $a > 0$, and identify the distribution of $\min(X_1, \dots, X_5)$.

Toggle Step-by-Step Solution

1. Minimum: $P(\min \ge a) = \prod_{i=1}^5 P(X_i \ge a) = (e^{-\lambda a})^5 = \mathbf{e^{-5\lambda a}}$. Since this is the survival function of an Exponential with rate $5\lambda$, $\min(X_1, \dots, X_5) \sim \mathbf{\text{Exp}(5\lambda)}$.

2. Maximum: $P(\max \ge a) = 1 - P(\max < a) = 1 - \prod_{i=1}^5 P(X_i < a) = \mathbf{1 - (1 - e^{-\lambda a})^5}$.

ISRT Year Final 2024 · Q1(c) [2 Marks] Normal Approximation to Binomial & Continuity Correction

When can we use the Normal approximation to the Binomial distribution? What is a continuity correction? Illustrate with a worked calculation.

Toggle Step-by-Step Solution

Conditions: Used when $n$ is large and $p$ is not too close to $0$ or $1$—standard rule of thumb: $np \ge 5$ and $nq \ge 5$ (or $npq \ge 10$). Then $X \sim B(n, p)$ is approximated by $Y \sim N(np, npq)$.

Continuity Correction: Because a discrete integer value $k$ occupies the unit-width histogram bar $[k - 0.5, k + 0.5]$, we adjust discrete endpoints by $\pm 0.5$ when integrating a continuous Normal curve.

Worked Example ($X \sim B(20, 0.5)$, find $P(X \ge 12)$): $\mu = 10$, $\sigma = \sqrt{5} \approx 2.2361$. With continuity correction: $$P(X \ge 12) \approx P(Y > 11.5) = 1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{11.5 - 10}{2.2361}\right) = 1 - \Phi(0.6708) = \mathbf{0.2512} \quad (\text{Exact Binomial} = 0.2517)$$
