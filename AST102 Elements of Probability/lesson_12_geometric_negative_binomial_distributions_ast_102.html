1.1 The Waiting-Time Paradigm

Flipping What Is Fixed vs. Random

In Lesson 11, we performed independent Bernoulli$(p)$ trials for a fixed number of trials $n$ and counted the random number of successes. In Lesson 12, we invert the experiment: we fix the target number of successes $r$ and count how many trials we must wait!

Binomial Paradigm (Lesson 11)

Fixed trials $n$ → Random successes $X \in \{0, 1, \dots, n\}$

"Shoot 10 free throws; how many go in?"

Waiting-Time Paradigm (Lesson 12)

Fixed target successes $r$ → Random trials $X \in \{r, r+1, \dots\}$

"Keep shooting until you make your 1st (Geometric) or 3rd (Negative Binomial) free throw; how many shots did it take?"

1.2 Geometric vs. Shifted Geometric Parameterizations

ISRT Year Final 2024 Q7(a)
Card A · Trials Until 1st Success $X \in \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$

Geometric Distribution $\text{Geom}(p)$

Counts the total number of trials up to and including the first success ($k - 1$ failures followed by $1$ success, where $q = 1 - p$).

PMF: $P(X = k) = q^{k-1} p, \quad k = 1, 2, 3, \dots$
CDF: $F(k) = P(X \le k) = 1 - q^k$
Survival (Tail): $P(X > k) = q^k$
$E[X] = \dfrac{1}{p}$ $V(X) = \dfrac{q}{p^2}$ $\text{Mode} = 1$
Card B · Failures Before 1st Success $Y = X - 1 \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$

Shifted Geometric Distribution

Counts only the number of failures observed strictly before the first success occurs ($Y = X - 1$).

PMF: $P(Y = y) = q^y p, \quad y = 0, 1, 2, \dots$
CDF: $F(y) = P(Y \le y) = 1 - q^{y+1}$
Survival (Tail): $P(Y \ge y) = q^y$
$E[Y] = \dfrac{q}{p} = \dfrac{1}{p} - 1$ $V(Y) = \dfrac{q}{p^2}$ $\text{Mode} = 0$
★

ISRT Exam Wording Note: Watch the wording carefully: "number of trials until 1st success" starts at $1$, while "number of failures before 1st success" starts at $0$. Both share the exact same variance $q/p^2$!

1.3 Negative Binomial Distribution ($r$-th Success)

Ross §4.8.2 · Year Final 2022 Q3(c)

Let $X$ be the total number of independent Bernoulli$(p)$ trials needed to achieve the $r$-th success. For $X = x$ to happen, two independent things must occur: (1) exactly $r - 1$ successes and $x - r$ failures occur in any order during the first $x - 1$ trials, and (2) the $x$-th trial is locked as a Success.

Anatomy of the $r$-th Success Landing on Trial $x$
First $(x - 1)$ Trials: Any Arrangement of $(r - 1)$ S's and $(x - r)$ F's
F S F ··· S F
$\dbinom{x-1}{r-1} p^{r-1} q^{x-r}$

$$P(X = x) = \binom{x-1}{r-1} p^r q^{x-r}, \qquad x = r,\; r+1,\; r+2,\; \dots$$
Sum of $r$ i.i.d. Geometrics

Waiting for the $r$-th success is simply waiting for the 1st success, then the 2nd, ..., up to the $r$-th:

$$X = G_1 + G_2 + \dots + G_r \implies E[X] = \frac{r}{p}, \quad V(X) = \frac{rq}{p^2}$$
Failures Parameterization ($Y = X - r$)

Counting failures $Y \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$ before the $r$-th success:

$$P(Y = y) = \binom{y + r - 1}{y} p^r q^y, \qquad E[Y] = \frac{rq}{p}, \quad V(Y) = \frac{rq}{p^2}$$

1.4 The Discrete Memoryless Property

Unique to Geometric Among Discrete RVs
Memoryless Identity ($s, t \ge 0$)
$$P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = P(X > t)$$

"No hot hand, no overdue luck": Suppose you have already failed $s$ times in a row (so $X > s$). Because Bernoulli trials are independent, the probability of waiting more than $t$ additional trials is $q^t$—identical to starting fresh from trial 1!

Uniqueness: The Geometric is the only discrete distribution with the memoryless property. Its continuous twin is the Exponential distribution (coming in Lesson 14).

Rose Exam-Trap Spotlight

3 Classic ISRT Pitfalls

1. $\binom{x}{r}$ vs. $\binom{x-1}{r-1}$ in Negative Binomial

Writing $\binom{x}{r}p^r q^{x-r}$ computes a Binomial probability (which allows trial $x$ to be a failure!). In Negative Binomial, the $r$-th success must land on trial $x$, leaving only $r - 1$ successes to place among the first $x - 1$ slots.

2. Off-by-One Trap ("before" vs. "on")

"Ask 5 women before one says she is literate" means 5 failures then 1 success on the 6th trial ($q^5 p$), whereas "first strike on the 2nd well" means 1 failure then 1 success ($q^1 p$).

3. Variance Under Shifting

Shifting $Y = X - 1$ decreases the mean by $1$ ($E[Y] = \frac{1}{p} - 1 = \frac{q}{p}$), but additive shifts never affect spread: $V(X - 1) = V(X) = \dfrac{q}{p^2}$!

Section 03 • Mathematical Rigor

Proof 1 · Infinite Geometric Series
Geometric PMF Sums to $1$ & Survival Formula $P(X > k) = q^k$

Since $0 < p \le 1$, we have $0 \le q = 1 - p < 1$. Summing $P(X = x) = q^{x-1}p$ over $x = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ gives a convergent geometric series:

$$\sum_{x=1}^{\infty} P(X = x) = p \sum_{x=1}^{\infty} q^{x-1} = p \cdot \frac{1}{1 - q} = \frac{p}{p} = 1 \;\checkmark$$

For the survival tail $P(X > k)$ (requiring all first $k$ trials to fail), sum from $x = k + 1$ to $\infty$:

$$P(X > k) = \sum_{x=k+1}^{\infty} q^{x-1} p = p q^k \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} q^j = p q^k \cdot \frac{1}{1 - q} = q^k \quad \blacksquare$$
Proof 2 · ISRT Year Final Q6(b) Style
Mean ($1/p$) & Variance ($q/p^2$) of the Geometric Distribution

Start with the geometric series identity $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} q^k = \dfrac{1}{1 - q}$ (valid for $|q| < 1$). Differentiate once with respect to $q$:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k q^{k-1} = \frac{1}{(1 - q)^2} = \frac{1}{p^2} \implies E[X] = p \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k q^{k-1} = p \cdot \frac{1}{p^2} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{p}}$$

Differentiate a second time with respect to $q$ to obtain the second factorial moment $E[X(X-1)]$:

$$\sum_{k=2}^{\infty} k(k - 1) q^{k-2} = \frac{2}{(1 - q)^3} = \frac{2}{p^3} \implies E[X(X - 1)] = p q \sum_{k=2}^{\infty} k(k - 1) q^{k-2} = \frac{2q}{p^2}$$

Using $E[X^2] = E[X(X-1)] + E[X] = \dfrac{2q}{p^2} + \dfrac{1}{p} = \dfrac{2q + p}{p^2} = \dfrac{1 + q}{p^2}$:

$$V(X) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2 = \frac{1 + q}{p^2} - \frac{1}{p^2} = \mathbf{\frac{q}{p^2}} \quad \blacksquare$$
Proof 3 · Conditional Survival
Proof of the Discrete Memoryless Property: $P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = P(X > t)$

Let $s, t \ge 0$ be integers. Since $\{X > s + t\} \subseteq \{X > s\}$, their intersection is simply $\{X > s + t\}$. Applying the definition of conditional probability and $P(X > k) = q^k$:

$$P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = \frac{P(X > s + t \cap X > s)}{P(X > s)} = \frac{P(X > s + t)}{P(X > s)} = \frac{q^{s+t}}{q^s} = q^t = P(X > t) \quad \blacksquare$$
Proof 4 · Generating Functions
PGF & MGF of the Geometric Distribution & Recovering $E[X]$

PGF ($|qt| < 1$):

$$P_X(t) = E[t^X] = \sum_{x=1}^{\infty} t^x q^{x-1} p = pt \sum_{x=1}^{\infty} (qt)^{x-1} = \mathbf{\frac{pt}{1 - qt}}$$

MGF ($qe^t < 1 \iff t < -\ln q$): Replace $t$ with $e^t$:

$$M_X(t) = E[e^{tX}] = \mathbf{\frac{pe^t}{1 - qe^t}}$$

Recovering $E[X] = M'_X(0)$: By the quotient rule:

$$M'_X(t) = \frac{pe^t(1 - qe^t) - pe^t(-qe^t)}{(1 - qe^t)^2} = \frac{pe^t}{(1 - qe^t)^2} \implies M'_X(0) = \frac{p}{(1 - q)^2} = \frac{p}{p^2} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{p}} \quad \blacksquare$$
Proof 5 · Sum of i.i.d. Geometrics
Negative Binomial Mean ($r/p$), Variance ($rq/p^2$) & MGF

Let $G_1$ be the trials until the 1st success, $G_2$ the additional trials from the 1st to the 2nd success, ..., and $G_r$ the additional trials from the $(r-1)$-th to the $r$-th success. Because Bernoulli trials are independent, $G_1, G_2, \dots, G_r \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \text{Geom}(p)$ and $X = \sum_{i=1}^r G_i$.

$$E[X] = \sum_{i=1}^{r} E[G_i] = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \frac{1}{p} = \mathbf{\frac{r}{p}}, \qquad V(X) = \sum_{i=1}^{r} V(G_i) = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \frac{q}{p^2} = \mathbf{\frac{rq}{p^2}}$$ $$M_X(t) = \prod_{i=1}^{r} M_{G_i}(t) = \left(\frac{pe^t}{1 - qe^t}\right)^r, \quad t < -\ln q \quad \blacksquare$$

Year Final 2024 · Q7(a) [3 Marks] Geometric vs. Shifted Geometric · Afghanistan Literacy

Distinguish between geometric and shifted geometric distribution. The literacy rate for women in Afghanistan is $12\%$. Let $X =$ the number of Afghani women you ask until one says that she is literate. (i) What is the probability distribution of $X$? (ii) What is the probability that you ask five women before one says she is literate? (iii) Find the mean and the standard deviation of $X$.

Toggle Model Solution

Distinction: Geometric counts total trials until the 1st success ($X \in \{1,2,\dots\}$, $P(X=k)=q^{k-1}p$, $E[X]=1/p$); Shifted Geometric counts failures strictly before the 1st success ($Y = X-1 \in \{0,1,2,\dots\}$, $P(Y=y)=q^y p$, $E[Y]=q/p$). Both share variance $q/p^2$.

(i) Here $p = 0.12$, $q = 0.88$. Using the trials parameterization: $X \sim \text{Geom}(0.12)$ with $P(X=k) = (0.88)^{k-1}(0.12)$ for $k=1,2,3,\dots$ (or Shifted Geometric $Y \in \{0,1,\dots\}$ if counting failures before the 1st literate woman).

(ii) Asking 5 women before one says she is literate means 5 failures followed by 1 success on the 6th woman ($X = 6$ or $Y = 5$): $$P = (0.88)^5 (0.12) = 0.527732 \times 0.12 = \mathbf{0.06333}$$

(iii) $E[X] = \dfrac{1}{0.12} = \mathbf{8.3333}$ (and $E[Y] = \frac{0.88}{0.12} = \mathbf{7.3333}$ for shifted). Standard deviation (identical for both): $$SD = \frac{\sqrt{q}}{p} = \frac{\sqrt{0.88}}{0.12} = \mathbf{7.8174}$$

Year Final 2022 · Q3(c) [6 Marks] Asif's Basketball Free Throws · Geom + NegBin

Asif is a high school basketball player who has a $70\%$ free throw percentage. Assume all free throw attempts are independent of one another (i.e., no hot hand). (i) What is the probability it takes more than 3 shots to get his first made free throw? (ii) What is the probability his first made free throw is on the third shot? (iii) What is the probability that his third made free throw is on his fifth shot?

Toggle Model Solution

Given $p = 0.70$ and $q = 0.30$:

(i) $X \sim \text{Geom}(0.70) \implies P(X > 3) = q^3 = (0.30)^3 = \mathbf{0.027}$.

(ii) $P(X = 3) = q^2 p = (0.30)^2 (0.70) = 0.09 \times 0.70 = \mathbf{0.063}$.

(iii) Let $W \sim \text{NegBin}(r = 3, p = 0.70)$ be the shot number of the 3rd make: $$P(W = 5) = \binom{5 - 1}{3 - 1}(0.70)^3(0.30)^{5 - 3} = \binom{4}{2}(0.343)(0.09) = 6 \times 0.03087 = \mathbf{0.18522}$$

Year Final · Q6(a,b) & Q7(c) [12 Marks] Oil Well Drilling · Geom + NegBin

An oil company conducts geological studies indicating that an exploratory oil well has a $30\%$ chance of striking oil independently at each site. (i) What is the probability that the first strike comes on the 2nd well drilled? (ii) What is the probability that the third strike comes on the 10th well drilled?

Toggle Model Solution

Here $p = 0.30$ and $q = 0.70$:

(i) First strike on 2nd well ($X \sim \text{Geom}(0.30)$): $$P(X = 2) = q^{2-1} p = (0.70)(0.30) = \mathbf{0.21}$$

(ii) Third strike on 10th well ($W \sim \text{NegBin}(r = 3, p = 0.30)$): $$P(W = 10) = \binom{10 - 1}{3 - 1}(0.30)^3(0.70)^{10 - 3} = \binom{9}{2}(0.027)(0.0823543) = 36 \times 0.0022236 \approx \mathbf{0.08005}$$

Year Final 2025 · Q1(c) [3 Marks] Intoxicated Drivers Road Check · Rare-Event Geom

Police check passing motorists at a roadblock where $1\%$ of drivers are intoxicated independently. Let $X$ be the number of motorists checked until the first intoxicated driver is found. Compute $E(X)$, $V(X)$, and $SD(X)$.

Toggle Model Solution

Here $X \sim \text{Geom}(p = 0.01)$ with $q = 0.99$:

  • Mean: $E(X) = \dfrac{1}{p} = \dfrac{1}{0.01} = \mathbf{100}$ motorists.
  • Variance: $V(X) = \dfrac{q}{p^2} = \dfrac{0.99}{(0.01)^2} = \dfrac{0.99}{0.0001} = \mathbf{9{,}900}$.
  • Standard Deviation: $SD(X) = \sqrt{9{,}900} \approx \mathbf{99.4987}$ motorists.
1st Incourse 2025 · Q4 [3 Marks] Heartless Card Draws With Replacement

Cards are drawn one at a time with replacement from a well-shuffled standard 52-card deck until a Heart appears. What is the probability that at least 3 draws are required?

Toggle Model Solution

With replacement, $p = \frac{13}{52} = \frac{1}{4}$ and $q = \frac{3}{4}$, so $X \sim \text{Geom}(1/4)$.

Requiring at least 3 draws ($X \ge 3$) is equivalent to $X > 2$ (meaning the first 2 draws are non-Hearts):

$$P(X \ge 3) = P(X > 2) = q^2 = \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^2 = \mathbf{\frac{9}{16} = 0.5625}$$
