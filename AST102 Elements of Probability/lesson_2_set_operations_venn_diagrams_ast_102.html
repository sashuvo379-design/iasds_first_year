Section 01

Core Concepts & Set Operations

Sources: Lectures 02–03, 06, 08 & Sheldon Ross Ch. 2.2

01 • Union ("OR") $A \cup B$

Union of Two Events

The union $A \cup B$ is the event containing all outcomes that belong to $A$, or $B$, or both. It occurs whenever at least one of $A$ or $B$ occurs.

Die Example (Class Note): $S = \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$. Let $E = \{2,4,6\}$ (even) and $T = \{3,4,5,6\}$ (greater than 2). Then:
$E \cup T = \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$
Two-Child Family (Class Note): $S = \{BB, BG, GB, GG\}$. Let $B = \{BB, BG, GB\}$ (at least one boy) and $M = \{BB, GG\}$ (genders match). Then:
$B \cup M = \{BB, BG, GB, GG\} = S$ (exhaustive!)
02 • Intersection ("AND") $A \cap B$ or $AB$

Intersection of Two Events

The intersection $A \cap B$ (often written compactly as $AB$ in Sheldon Ross) is the event containing all outcomes that are common to both $A$ and $B$. It occurs only when both $A$ and $B$ happen simultaneously.

Die Example (Class Note): With $E = \{2,4,6\}$ and $T = \{3,4,5,6\}$, the outcomes that are both even AND greater than 2 are:
$E \cap T = \{4, 6\}$
Disjoint Condition: If $A$ and $B$ share no common outcomes, then $A \cap B = \emptyset$, and $A, B$ are called mutually exclusive.
03 • Complement ("NOT") $A'$, $A^c$, $\bar{A}$

Complement of an Event

The complement of $A$ consists of all outcomes in $S$ that are NOT in $A$. Event $A^c$ occurs if and only if $A$ does not occur.

$S^c = \emptyset$
$\emptyset^c = S$
$(A^c)^c = A$
$A \cap A^c = \emptyset,\; A \cup A^c = S$
04 • Difference ("ONLY A") $A \cap B'$ or $A \setminus B$

Difference / Relative Complement

The set difference $A \setminus B = A \cap B'$ represents the event that "$A$ occurs, but $B$ does not occur." It removes the overlap $A \cap B$ from $A$.

Key Disjoint Partition of $A$:

$A = (A \cap B') \cup (A \cap B) \implies P(A \cap B') = P(A) - P(A \cap B)$

Example: For $E=\{2,4,6\}$ and $T=\{3,4,5,6\}$, $E \cap T' = \{2\}$ (even, but NOT greater than 2).

★
ISRT Year Final 2024 Q2(b) Favorite Distinction

Mutually Exclusive vs. Complementary vs. Exhaustive Events

Property Mutually Exclusive Complementary Collectively Exhaustive
Set Condition $A \cap B = \emptyset$ $A \cap B = \emptyset$ AND $A \cup B = S$ $A_1 \cup A_2 \cup \dots \cup A_k = S$
Probability Implication $P(A \cap B) = 0$; $P(A) + P(B) \le 1$ $P(A \cap B) = 0$ and $P(A) + P(B) = 1$ $P(\bigcup A_i) = 1$
Die Example ($S=\{1..6\}$) $A=\{1,2\}$, $B=\{5,6\}$ (leaves out $\{3,4\}$) $A=\{1,3,5\}$, $B=\{2,4,6\}$ $A=\{1,2,3,4\}$, $B=\{3,4,5,6\}$ (overlaps at $\{3,4\}$)
Exam Takeaway All complementary events are mutually exclusive AND exhaustive, but mutually exclusive events need NOT be exhaustive (so they are not necessarily complementary).

Exam Nuance Note — "Not More Than Two"

In probability, "not more than two occur simultaneously" means "not all three occur," which is $(A \cap B \cap C)^c$—this naturally includes 0, 1, or 2 events occurring (7 of the 8 Venn regions). If a question ever asks for "at least one, but not more than two" (strictly 1 or 2 events), you intersect with the union: $(A \cap B \cap C)^c \cap (A \cup B \cup C)$.

Section 04 • Theoretical Rigor

Set Laws & Proofs Drawer

Expand any derivation to review step-by-step proofs

  • Commutative Laws: $E \cup F = F \cup E$ and $EF = FE$.
  • Associative Laws: $(E \cup F) \cup G = E \cup (F \cup G)$ and $(EF)G = E(FG)$.
  • Distributive Laws:
    $$(E \cup F) \cap G = (E \cap G) \cup (F \cap G), \qquad (E \cap F) \cup G = (E \cup G) \cap (F \cup G)$$

Visual Verification (Ross Ch. 2.2): Shade $E \cup F$ and intersect with $G$ on a 3-circle Venn diagram; the resulting region is identical to taking the lens $E \cap G$ together with the lens $F \cap G$.

$$(A \cup B)^c = A^c \cap B^c \qquad \text{and} \qquad (A \cap B)^c = A^c \cup B^c$$

Element-wise Proof of $(A \cup B)^c = A^c \cap B^c$:

  1. Let $x \in (A \cup B)^c$. Then $x \notin (A \cup B)$, meaning $x$ is neither in $A$ nor in $B$. Thus $x \in A^c$ AND $x \in B^c$, so $x \in A^c \cap B^c$. Hence $(A \cup B)^c \subseteq A^c \cap B^c$.
  2. Conversely, let $x \in A^c \cap B^c$. Then $x \notin A$ and $x \notin B$, so $x$ cannot belong to $A \cup B$. Thus $x \in (A \cup B)^c$. Hence $A^c \cap B^c \subseteq (A \cup B)^c$.
  3. By mutual set inclusion, $(A \cup B)^c = A^c \cap B^c$. $\blacksquare$

Generalized to $n$ Events (Ross Ch. 2.2):

$$\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n} E_i\right)^c = \bigcap_{i=1}^{n} E_i^c \qquad \text{and} \qquad \left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n} E_i\right)^c = \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} E_i^c$$

First, decompose $A$ and $B$ into mutually exclusive parts:

$$A = (A \cap B') \cup (A \cap B) \implies P(A) = P(A \cap B') + P(A \cap B) \implies P(A \cap B') = P(A) - P(A \cap B)$$

Similarly, $P(B \cap A') = P(B) - P(A \cap B)$. Now write $A \cup B$ as the union of three mutually exclusive events:

$$A \cup B = (A \cap B') \cup (A \cap B) \cup (B \cap A')$$

Applying Axiom 3 (additivity for disjoint events):

$$P(A \cup B) = [P(A) - P(A \cap B)] + P(A \cap B) + [P(B) - P(A \cap B)] = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B) \quad \blacksquare$$

The event that exactly one (symmetric difference $A \triangle B$) of $A$ or $B$ occurs is the disjoint union $(A \cap B^c) \cup (A^c \cap B)$.

Since $(A \cap B^c) \cap (A^c \cap B) = \emptyset$, by Axiom 3 and the difference formula:

$$P\big((A \cap B^c) \cup (A^c \cap B)\big) = P(A \cap B^c) + P(A^c \cap B) = \big(P(A) - P(A \cap B)\big) + \big(P(B) - P(A \cap B)\big)$$ $$= P(A) + P(B) - 2P(A \cap B) \quad \blacksquare$$

1st Incourse 2024 Q3 & 2025 Q2 [3–6 pts] ISRT, University of Dhaka

Given $P(A) = \dfrac{1}{3}$, $P(B) = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and $P(A \cup B) = \dfrac{3}{4}$, find: (i) $P(A \cap B)$, (ii) $P(A' \cup B')$, (iii) $P(A' \cap B)$, and (iv) $P(A' \cap B')$.

(i) $P(A \cap B)$: Using the Addition Law $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$: $$P(A \cap B) = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2} - \frac{3}{4} = \frac{4 + 6 - 9}{12} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{12}}$$

(ii) $P(A' \cup B')$: By De Morgan's Law, $A' \cup B' = (A \cap B)'$: $$P(A' \cup B') = 1 - P(A \cap B) = 1 - \frac{1}{12} = \mathbf{\frac{11}{12}}$$

(iii) $P(A' \cap B)$: Using disjoint decomposition of $B$: $$P(A' \cap B) = P(B) - P(A \cap B) = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$$

(iv) $P(A' \cap B')$: By De Morgan's Law, $A' \cap B' = (A \cup B)'$: $$P(A' \cap B') = 1 - P(A \cup B) = 1 - \frac{3}{4} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{4}}$$

Year Final 2025 • Q2(c) [4 pts] ISRT, University of Dhaka

In a production process, items may have defect $A$ with probability $P(A) = 0.1$, defect $B$ with $P(B) = 0.2$, and both defects with $P(A \cap B) = 0.05$. Find the probability that a randomly selected item has: (i) at least one defect, (ii) defect $A$ but not $B$, (iii) none of the defects, and (iv) precisely one defect.

(i) At least one defect: $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B) = 0.1 + 0.2 - 0.05 = \mathbf{0.25}$.

(ii) Defect $A$ but not $B$: $P(A \cap B') = P(A) - P(A \cap B) = 0.1 - 0.05 = \mathbf{0.05}$.

(iii) None of the defects: $P(A' \cap B') = P((A \cup B)') = 1 - P(A \cup B) = 1 - 0.25 = \mathbf{0.75}$.

(iv) Precisely one defect: $P(A \triangle B) = P(A) + P(B) - 2P(A \cap B) = 0.1 + 0.2 - 2(0.05) = \mathbf{0.20}$.

Year Final 2025 • Q2(d) [3 pts] ISRT, University of Dhaka

A survey of whales on their fears revealed: 19 fear Giant Squids ($A$), 24 fear Captain Ahab ($B$), 20 fear Pollution ($C$), 6 fear all three, 11 fear both Ahab and Giant Squids, 3 fear Pollution and Ahab only, 4 fear Pollution only, and 5 fear none of these. Find the number of whales who fear: (i) at least one of the three, (ii) Captain Ahab but not Giant Squids, and (iii) Giant Squids or Pollution.

Step 1 — Fill all 8 Venn regions from the center outward:

  • All three: |A ∩ B ∩ C| = 6
  • A and B only: |A ∩ B ∩ C'| = 11 − 6 = 5
  • B and C only (given): |A' ∩ B ∩ C| = 3
  • C only (given): |A' ∩ B' ∩ C| = 4
  • A and C only: |A ∩ B' ∩ C| = |C| − (6 + 3 + 4) = 20 − 13 = 7
  • A only: |A ∩ B' ∩ C'| = |A| − (6 + 5 + 7) = 19 − 18 = 1
  • B only: |A' ∩ B ∩ C'| = |B| − (6 + 5 + 3) = 24 − 14 = 10
  • None (given): |A' ∩ B' ∩ C'| = 5 (Total surveyed = 36 + 5 = 41)

Step 2 — Answer the three questions:

  • (i) At least one ($|A \cup B \cup C|$): $1 + 10 + 4 + 5 + 7 + 3 + 6 = \mathbf{36}$.
  • (ii) Captain Ahab but not Giant Squids ($|B \cap A'|$): $10 + 3 = \mathbf{13}$ (equivalently $|B| - |A \cap B| = 24 - 11 = 13$).
  • (iii) Giant Squids or Pollution ($|A \cup C|$): $|A| + |C| - |A \cap C| = 19 + 20 - (6 + 7) = \mathbf{26}$.
