01 Raw Moments ($\mu'_r$) vs. Central Moments ($\mu_r$) & Shape

Origin vs. Mean · Skewness & Kurtosis
$r$-th Raw Moment (About Origin)
$$\mu'_r = E[X^r], \qquad \mu'_1 = E[X] = \mu$$

Captures location and powers of $X$ directly; easiest to compute from $M_X(t)$.

$r$-th Central Moment (About Mean)
$$\mu_r = E\big[(X - \mu)^r\big], \qquad \mu_1 = 0,\;\; \mu_2 = \sigma^2$$

Origin-free descriptors of spread ($\mu_2$), asymmetry ($\mu_3$), and tail weight ($\mu_4$).

Essential Raw → Central Conversion Formulas
$$\mu_1 = 0, \qquad \mu_2 = \mu'_2 - (\mu'_1)^2$$
$$\mu_3 = \mu'_3 - 3\mu'_2\mu'_1 + 2(\mu'_1)^3$$
$$\mu_4 = \mu'_4 - 4\mu'_3\mu'_1 + 6\mu'_2(\mu'_1)^2 - 3(\mu'_1)^4$$
Skewness ($\gamma_1$)
$$\gamma_1 = \frac{\mu_3}{\sigma^3} = \frac{\mu_3}{\mu_2^{3/2}}$$

$\gamma_1 = 0$: Symmetric · $\gamma_1 > 0$: Right (positively) skewed · $\gamma_1 < 0$: Left (negatively) skewed.

Excess Kurtosis ($\gamma_2$)
$$\gamma_2 = \frac{\mu_4}{\sigma^4} - 3 = \frac{\mu_4}{\mu_2^2} - 3$$

Mesokurtic ($\gamma_2 = 0$, Normal) · Leptokurtic ($\gamma_2 > 0$, heavy-tailed) · Platykurtic ($\gamma_2 < 0$, light-tailed).

02 Generating Functions & The PGF ($P_X(t)$)

Non-Negative Integer RVs · 2nd Inc. 2025 Q2

For a discrete random variable $X \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$, its Probability Generating Function (PGF) packs the entire probability distribution into a single power series in $t$:

$$P_X(t) = E[t^X] = \sum_{x=0}^{\infty} p_x\,t^x = p_0 + p_1 t + p_2 t^2 + p_3 t^3 + \dots$$

Why "Probability Generating"? Because expanding $P_X(t)$ in powers of $t$ directly generates every probability $p_k = P(X=k)$ as the coefficient of $t^k$—equivalently recoverable by differentiating at $t=0$: $P(X = k) = \dfrac{P_X^{(k)}(0)}{k!}$.

1. Normalization
$P_X(1) = \sum p_x = 1$

Used to solve for unknown constants $k$.

2. Mean at $t=1$
$P'_X(1) = E[X]$

First derivative evaluated at $t=1$.

3. Factorial Moment
$P''_X(1) = E[X(X-1)]$

Gives $V(X) = P''_X(1) + P'_X(1) - [P'_X(1)]^2$.

4. Independent Sums
$P_{X+Y}(t) = P_X(t)P_Y(t)$

Multiplies PGFs of independent RVs.

03 Moment Generating Function ($M_X(t)$) & Cumulant Generating Function ($K_X(t)$)

Discrete & Continuous · Ross Ch. 7.7
Moment Generating Function (MGF)
$$M_X(t) = E[e^{tX}] = 1 + \mu'_1 t + \frac{\mu'_2}{2!}t^2 + \frac{\mu'_3}{3!}t^3 + \dots$$
  • Defined on an open interval $(-h, h)$ around $t = 0$.
  • Moment Extraction: $\mu'_r = E[X^r] = M_X^{(r)}(0)$.
  • Linear Scaling: $M_{a+bX}(t) = e^{at} M_X(bt)$.
  • Independent Sums: $M_{X+Y}(t) = M_X(t)M_Y(t)$.
  • Uniqueness Theorem: If $M_X(t) = M_Y(t)$ near $0$, then $X$ and $Y$ have the exact same distribution.
Cumulant Generating Function (CGF)
$$K_X(t) = \ln M_X(t), \qquad \kappa_r = \left.\frac{d^r}{dt^r}K_X(t)\right|_{t=0}$$
$\kappa_1 = \mu$
$\kappa_2 = \sigma^2 = \mu_2$
$\kappa_3 = \mu_3$
$\kappa_4 = \mu_4 - 3\sigma^4$

Direct Shape Shortcut: $\gamma_1 = \dfrac{\kappa_3}{\kappa_2^{3/2}}$ and $\gamma_2 = \dfrac{\kappa_4}{\kappa_2^2}$! Taking $\ln$ turns exponential MGFs (like Normal & Poisson) into simple polynomials.

!
ISRT Exam Trap Spotlight

Two Classic Mistakes That Cost Easy Marks

Trap 1: Evaluating at $t = 1$ vs. $t = 0$!

PGF moments are evaluated at $t = 1$, and $P''_X(1)$ gives the factorial moment $E[X(X-1)]$, NOT $E[X^2]$! Forgetting to add $+P'_X(1)$ in $V(X) = P''_X(1) + P'_X(1) - [P'_X(1)]^2$ is the #1 PGF error. By contrast, MGF & CGF derivatives are evaluated at $t = 0$.

Trap 2: $X_1 - X_2$ is NOT Binomial!

Even if $X_1 \sim \text{Bin}(n_1, p)$ and $X_2 \sim \text{Bin}(n_2, p)$ are independent, $M_{X_1 - X_2}(t) = (q + pe^t)^{n_1}(q + pe^{-t})^{n_2}$, which cannot collapse into $(q + pe^t)^N$. Moreover, $X_1 - X_2$ can take negative values $\{-n_2, \dots, n_1\}$, whereas a Binomial RV is strictly non-negative!

Proofs & Derivations Drawer

5 core AST 102 generating-function & moment proofs

Proof 1 · ISRT 2nd Incourse 2025 Q2(a)
Mean & Variance from PGF: $E[X] = P'_X(1)$ and $V(X) = P''_X(1) + P'_X(1) - [P'_X(1)]^2$

Start with $P_X(t) = \sum_{x=0}^{\infty} p_x t^x$. Differentiate term-by-term with respect to $t$:

$$P'_X(t) = \sum_{x=1}^{\infty} x\,p_x t^{x-1} \implies P'_X(1) = \sum_{x=1}^{\infty} x\,p_x (1)^{x-1} = E[X]$$

Differentiate a second time and evaluate at $t = 1$:

$$P''_X(t) = \sum_{x=2}^{\infty} x(x-1)\,p_x t^{x-2} \implies P''_X(1) = \sum_{x=2}^{\infty} x(x-1)\,p_x = E[X(X-1)] = E[X^2] - E[X]$$

Therefore $E[X^2] = P''_X(1) + E[X] = P''_X(1) + P'_X(1)$. Substituting into $V(X) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2$:

$$V(X) = P''_X(1) + P'_X(1) - \big[P'_X(1)\big]^2 \quad \blacksquare$$
Proof 2 · ISRT Year Final 2018 Q1a(v) & 2025 Q6(c)
Linear Transformation of MGF: Prove $M_{a+bX}(t) = e^{at} M_X(bt)$

Let $Y = a + bX$ where $a, b$ are real constants. By definition of the MGF:

$$M_Y(t) = E\big[e^{tY}\big] = E\big[e^{t(a+bX)}\big] = E\big[e^{at} \cdot e^{(bt)X}\big] = e^{at}\,E\big[e^{(bt)X}\big] = e^{at}\,M_X(bt) \quad \blacksquare$$

Here $e^{at}$ is a non-random constant and factors straight out of the expectation operator.

Proof 3 · ISRT Year Final 2024 Q6(b–c)
MGF of Binomial$(n,p)$, Mean/Variance & Reproductive Property

1. MGF Derivation: Let $q = 1 - p$. By the Binomial Theorem:

$$M_X(t) = \sum_{x=0}^{n} e^{tx}\binom{n}{x}p^x q^{n-x} = \sum_{x=0}^{n} \binom{n}{x}(pe^t)^x q^{n-x} = (q + pe^t)^n$$

2. Mean & Variance:

$$M'_X(t) = n(q+pe^t)^{n-1}pe^t \implies E[X] = M'_X(0) = n(q+p)^{n-1}p = np$$ $$M''_X(t) = n(n-1)(q+pe^t)^{n-2}(pe^t)^2 + n(q+pe^t)^{n-1}pe^t \implies E[X^2] = n(n-1)p^2 + np$$ $$V(X) = n^2p^2 - np^2 + np - n^2p^2 = np(1 - p) = npq$$

3. Reproductive Property: For independent $X_1 \sim \text{Bin}(n_1, p)$ and $X_2 \sim \text{Bin}(n_2, p)$:

$$M_{X_1+X_2}(t) = M_{X_1}(t)M_{X_2}(t) = (q+pe^t)^{n_1}(q+pe^t)^{n_2} = (q+pe^t)^{n_1+n_2}$$

By the Uniqueness Theorem of MGFs, $X_1 + X_2 \sim \text{Bin}(n_1 + n_2, p)$. $\blacksquare$

Proof 4 · ISRT 2nd Incourse 2025 Q4
CGF of Normal$(\mu, \sigma^2)$: Proving $\gamma_1 = 0$ and $\gamma_2 = 0$

Given $M_X(t) = \exp\!\left(\mu t + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 t^2\right)$, take the natural logarithm to obtain the CGF:

$$K_X(t) = \ln M_X(t) = \mu t + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 t^2$$

Differentiate successively at $t = 0$ to extract cumulants:

$$\kappa_1 = K'_X(0) = \mu, \qquad \kappa_2 = K''_X(0) = \sigma^2, \qquad \kappa_3 = K'''_X(0) = 0, \qquad \kappa_4 = K^{(4)}_X(0) = 0$$ $$\gamma_1 = \frac{\kappa_3}{\kappa_2^{3/2}} = 0 \;\;(\text{Symmetric}), \qquad \gamma_2 = \frac{\kappa_4}{\kappa_2^2} = 0 \;\;(\text{Mesokurtic}) \quad \blacksquare$$
Proof 5 · Binomial Expansion of $(X - \mu'_1)^r$
Central Moments from Raw Moments: $\mu_2 = \mu'_2 - (\mu'_1)^2$ and $\mu_3 = \mu'_3 - 3\mu'_2\mu'_1 + 2(\mu'_1)^3$

Let $\mu = \mu'_1 = E[X]$. Expanding $(X - \mu'_1)^2$ and $(X - \mu'_1)^3$ inside expectation:

$$\mu_2 = E[(X - \mu'_1)^2] = E[X^2] - 2\mu'_1 E[X] + (\mu'_1)^2 = \mu'_2 - 2(\mu'_1)^2 + (\mu'_1)^2 = \mu'_2 - (\mu'_1)^2$$ $$\mu_3 = E[(X - \mu'_1)^3] = E[X^3 - 3\mu'_1 X^2 + 3(\mu'_1)^2 X - (\mu'_1)^3]$$ $$= \mu'_3 - 3\mu'_1 \mu'_2 + 3(\mu'_1)^2\mu'_1 - (\mu'_1)^3 = \mu'_3 - 3\mu'_2\mu'_1 + 2(\mu'_1)^3 \quad \blacksquare$$

ISRT Year Final 2025 · Q6(b) [5 Marks] PGF of Independent Sums · AST 102

Keira tosses 2 fair coins ($X$ = number of heads). Independently, Hassan tosses 2 biased coins with $P(H) = \frac{1}{3}$ and 1 biased coin with $P(H) = \frac{1}{4}$ ($Y$ = number of heads). Let $Z = X + Y$. Find $P_X(t)$, $P_Y(t)$, the expanded polynomial $P_Z(t)$, and $E(Z)$.

Toggle Model Solution

1. Keira's PGF ($X \sim \text{Bin}(2, 1/2)$): $$P_X(t) = \left(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}t\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4}(1 + 2t + t^2)$$

2. Hassan's PGF (independent sum of 3 coins): $$P_Y(t) = \left(\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{3}t\right)^2\left(\frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{4}t\right) = \frac{1}{36}(4 + 4t + t^2)(3 + t) = \frac{1}{36}(12 + 16t + 7t^2 + t^3)$$

3. Combined PGF ($Z = X + Y$, independent): $$P_Z(t) = P_X(t)P_Y(t) = \frac{1}{144}(1 + 2t + t^2)(12 + 16t + 7t^2 + t^3) = \mathbf{\frac{1}{144}(12 + 40t + 51t^2 + 31t^3 + 9t^4 + t^5)}$$

4. Expected Value $E(Z) = P'_Z(1)$: $$E(Z) = \frac{40 + 102 + 93 + 36 + 5}{144} = \frac{276}{144} = \mathbf{\frac{23}{12} \approx 1.9167}$$ (Quick check: $E[X] + E[Y] = 1 + \frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{23}{12}$ ✓)

ISRT 2nd Incourse 2025 Q2(b) & 2024 Q6(b) [5 Marks] PGF Normalization & Variance · AST 102

The probability generating function of a discrete random variable $X$ is given by $P_X(t) = k(3 + t + 2t^2)^2$. Show that $k = \dfrac{1}{36}$, find $P(X = 3)$, and show that $V(X) = \dfrac{29}{18}$.

Toggle Model Solution

1. Find $k$: Using $P_X(1) = 1$: $k(3 + 1 + 2)^2 = 36k = 1 \implies k = \mathbf{\frac{1}{36}}$.

2. Find $P(X = 3)$: Expand $P_X(t) = \frac{1}{36}(9 + 6t + 13t^2 + 4t^3 + 4t^4)$. The coefficient of $t^3$ is $P(X=3) = \frac{4}{36} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{9}}$.

3. Find $V(X)$ via Derivatives at $t = 1$: $$P'_X(t) = \frac{1}{18}(3+t+2t^2)(1+4t) \implies P'_X(1) = \frac{1}{18}(6)(5) = \frac{30}{18} = \frac{5}{3}$$ $$P''_X(t) = \frac{1}{18}\big[(1+4t)^2 + 4(3+t+2t^2)\big] \implies P''_X(1) = \frac{1}{18}(25 + 24) = \frac{49}{18}$$ $$V(X) = P''_X(1) + P'_X(1) - \big[P'_X(1)\big]^2 = \frac{49}{18} + \frac{30}{18} - \frac{25}{9} = \frac{79 - 50}{18} = \mathbf{\frac{29}{18}} \;\checkmark$$

ISRT 2nd Incourse 2025 · Q3 [3 Marks] MGF of Linear Combination · AST 102

Let $X$ and $Y$ be independent and identically distributed random variables with MGF $M_X(t) = e^{t^2 + 3t}$. Find the MGF of $Z = 3X - 3Y + 4$ and identify the distribution of $Z$.

Toggle Model Solution

By independence and $M_{a+bX}(t) = e^{at}M_X(bt)$:

$$M_Z(t) = E\big[e^{t(3X - 3Y + 4)}\big] = e^{4t}\,M_X(3t)\,M_Y(-3t)$$ $$= e^{4t}\cdot e^{(3t)^2 + 3(3t)} \cdot e^{(-3t)^2 + 3(-3t)} = e^{4t + 9t^2 + 9t + 9t^2 - 9t} = \mathbf{e^{18t^2 + 4t}}$$

Matching $e^{\mu_Z t + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_Z^2 t^2}$ gives $\mu_Z = 4$ and $\frac{1}{2}\sigma_Z^2 = 18 \implies \sigma_Z^2 = 36$. By the Uniqueness Theorem, $Z \sim \mathbf{N(4, 36)}$.

ISRT 2nd Incourse 2025 · Q4 [3 Marks] CGF & Distribution Shape · AST 102

Given the moment generating function $M_X(t) = e^{\mu t + \frac{1}{2}t^2\sigma^2}$, use the Cumulant Generating Function (CGF) to find $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$, and comment on the shape of the distribution.

Toggle Model Solution

The CGF is $K_X(t) = \ln M_X(t) = \mu t + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 t^2$. Taking derivatives at $t = 0$: $\kappa_1 = \mu$, $\kappa_2 = \sigma^2$, $\kappa_3 = 0$, $\kappa_4 = 0$.

Therefore $\gamma_1 = \frac{\kappa_3}{\kappa_2^{3/2}} = \mathbf{0}$ and $\gamma_2 = \frac{\kappa_4}{\kappa_2^2} = \mathbf{0}$.

Shape Comment: Since $\gamma_1 = 0$ and $\gamma_2 = 0$, the distribution is perfectly symmetric and mesokurtic (Normal).

ISRT Year Final 2024 · Q6(c) [4 Marks] Binomial Sum vs. Difference · AST 102

Let $X_1 \sim \text{Bin}(n_1, p)$ and $X_2 \sim \text{Bin}(n_2, p)$ be independent. Prove via MGF that $X_1 + X_2 \sim \text{Bin}(n_1 + n_2, p)$, and explain why $X_1 - X_2$ is not Binomial.

Toggle Model Solution

1. Sum $X_1 + X_2$: By independence, $M_{X_1+X_2}(t) = M_{X_1}(t)M_{X_2}(t) = (q+pe^t)^{n_1}(q+pe^t)^{n_2} = (q+pe^t)^{n_1+n_2}$. By the MGF Uniqueness Theorem, $X_1 + X_2 \sim \text{Bin}(n_1 + n_2, p)$.

2. Why $X_1 - X_2$ is NOT Binomial: $$M_{X_1-X_2}(t) = M_{X_1}(t)M_{X_2}(-t) = (q + pe^t)^{n_1}(q + pe^{-t})^{n_2}$$ This does not match the Binomial MGF form $(q^* + p^* e^t)^N$. Furthermore, $X_1 - X_2$ has support $\{-n_2, \dots, 0, \dots, n_1\}$, which includes negative integers, whereas a Binomial random variable can only take values in $\{0, 1, \dots, N\}$.
