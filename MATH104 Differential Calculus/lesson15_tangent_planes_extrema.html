Core Theory (Lecture 15 + Ashraful's Notes)

2.1 Tangent Planes, Normal Lines & Acute Angle Between Planes

For a level surface $F(x, y, z) = k$ passing through $P(x_0, y_0, z_0)$, the gradient $\nabla F(x_0, y_0, z_0) = \langle F_x, F_y, F_z\rangle$ is perpendicular (normal) to the surface:

Geometric Object Formula at $P(x_0, y_0, z_0)$ Note for Explicit $z = f(x, y)$
Tangent Plane $F_x(x - x_0) + F_y(y - y_0) + F_z(z - z_0) = 0$ Set $F(x, y, z) = f(x, y) - z = 0 \implies z - z_0 = f_x(x - x_0) + f_y(y - y_0)$.
Normal Line (Parametric) $x = x_0 + F_x t,\;\; y = y_0 + F_y t,\;\; z = z_0 + F_z t$ Direction vector is $\nabla F = \langle f_x, f_y, -1\rangle$.
Acute Angle Between Two Planes $\cos\theta = \dfrac{|\mathbf{n}_1 \cdot \mathbf{n}_2|}{\|\mathbf{n}_1\|\,\|\mathbf{n}_2\|} \implies \theta = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{|\mathbf{n}_1 \cdot \mathbf{n}_2|}{\|\mathbf{n}_1\|\,\|\mathbf{n}_2\|}\right)$ Use the absolute value (modulus) in the numerator to guarantee the acute angle $\theta \in [0^\circ, 90^\circ]$!

2.2 Critical Points & The Multivariable Second Derivative Test

A point $(a, b)$ is a critical point of $f(x, y)$ if $f_x(a, b) = 0$ and $f_y(a, b) = 0$ (or if either partial derivative does not exist). To classify a stationary critical point $(a, b)$, compute the Hessian Discriminant: $$D = D(a, b) = f_{xx}(a, b)\,f_{yy}(a, b) - \left[f_{xy}(a, b)\right]^2 = \begin{vmatrix} f_{xx} & f_{xy} \\ f_{yx} & f_{yy} \end{vmatrix}$$

Condition at $(a, b)$ Classification 3D Local Shape
$D > 0$ and $f_{xx}(a, b) > 0$ Local Minimum at $(a, b)$ Upward-opening bowl (cup)
$D > 0$ and $f_{xx}(a, b) < 0$ Local Maximum at $(a, b)$ Downward-opening dome (cap)
$D < 0$ Saddle Point at $(a, b)$ Curved up in one direction, down in another (Pringle chip!)
$D = 0$ Test Inconclusive Requires direct inspection of $f(x, y) - f(a, b)$.

Guided Worked Examples (Ashraful's Class Notes)

Ashraful's Notes • Explicit Surface

Example 1: Tangent Plane & Normal Line to $z = x^2 + y^2$ at $P(2, -3, 13)$

Find the tangent plane equation and parametric normal line equations to the paraboloid $z = x^2 + y^2$ at $P(2, -3, 13)$.

Step 1 — Write as Level Surface $F(x, y, z) = 0$: $F(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 - z = 0$.
Step 2 — Compute Normal Vector $\nabla F$ at $P(2, -3, 13)$: $F_x = 2x = 4$,   $F_y = 2y = -6$,   $F_z = -1$. So $\mathbf{n} = \langle 4, -6, -1\rangle$.
Step 3 — Tangent Plane Equation: $4(x - 2) - 6(y + 3) - 1(z - 13) = 0 \implies 4x - 8 - 6y - 18 - z + 13 = 0 \implies \mathbf{4x - 6y - z = 13}$.
Step 4 — Parametric Normal Line: $\mathbf{x = 2 + 4t,\;\; y = -3 - 6t,\;\; z = 13 - t}$.
Ashraful's Notes • Classic Extrema & Saddle

Example 2: Local Max, Min & Saddle Points of $f(x, y) = x^4 + y^4 - 4xy + 1$

Find and classify all critical points of $f(x, y) = x^4 + y^4 - 4xy + 1$.

Step 1 — Solve $f_x = 0$ and $f_y = 0$: $f_x = 4x^3 - 4y = 0 \implies y = x^3$. Substitute into $f_y = 4y^3 - 4x = 0 \implies x^9 - x = 0 \implies x(x^4 + 1)(x^2 + 1)(x - 1)(x + 1) = 0$.
Step 2 — List All Real Critical Points: $x = 0, 1, -1$ with $y = x^3$ gives three critical points: $(0, 0)$, $(1, 1)$, and $(-1, -1)$.
Step 3 — Compute Discriminant $D(x, y)$: $f_{xx} = 12x^2$, $f_{yy} = 12y^2$, $f_{xy} = -4 \implies D(x, y) = 144x^2 y^2 - 16$.
Step 4 — Classify Each Point:
• At $(0, 0)$: $D = -16 < 0 \implies$ Saddle Point at $(0, 0, 1)$.
• At $(1, 1)$: $D = 128 > 0$ and $f_{xx} = 12 > 0 \implies$ Local Minimum $f(1, 1) = -1$.
• At $(-1, -1)$: $D = 128 > 0$ and $f_{xx} = 12 > 0 \implies$ Local Minimum $f(-1, -1) = -1$.

Question: Consider the ellipsoid $x^2 + 4y^2 + z^2 = 18$.
(a) Find an equation of the tangent plane to the ellipsoid at the point $(1, 2, 1)$.
(b) Find parametric equations of the line that is normal to the ellipsoid at $(1, 2, 1)$.
(c) Find the acute angle that the tangent plane at $(1, 2, 1)$ makes with the $xy$-plane.

Hint: Set $F(x, y, z) = x^2 + 4y^2 + z^2$. For part (c), the normal vector to the $xy$-plane ($z = 0$) is simply $\mathbf{n}_2 = \langle 0, 0, 1\rangle$!
Full Solution:
Let $F(x, y, z) = x^2 + 4y^2 + z^2$. Then $\nabla F = \langle 2x, 8y, 2z\rangle$. At $(1, 2, 1)$: $\mathbf{n}_1 = \nabla F(1, 2, 1) = \langle 2, 16, 2\rangle$ (parallel to $\langle 1, 8, 1\rangle$).

(a) Tangent Plane: $$2(x - 1) + 16(y - 2) + 2(z - 1) = 0 \implies \mathbf{x + 8y + z = 18}$$ (b) Parametric Normal Line: $$\mathbf{x = 1 + 2t, \qquad y = 2 + 16t, \qquad z = 1 + 2t}$$ (Equivalently, $x = 1 + s, y = 2 + 8s, z = 1 + s$.)

(c) Acute Angle with the $xy$-Plane ($\mathbf{n}_2 = \langle 0, 0, 1\rangle$): $$\cos\theta = \frac{|\langle 1, 8, 1\rangle \cdot \langle 0, 0, 1\rangle|}{\sqrt{1^2 + 8^2 + 1^2}\cdot 1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{66}} \implies \mathbf{\theta = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{66}}\right) \approx 82.93^\circ}$$
Lecture 15 Application • Multivariable Optimization 6 Marks

Question: Find the dimensions $x, y, z$ of an open-top rectangular box of volume $32\text{ m}^3$ that minimizes the total surface area $S = xy + 2xz + 2yz$.

Full Solution:
1. Use volume constraint $xyz = 32 \implies z = \dfrac{32}{xy}$ to reduce $S$ to 2 variables ($x > 0, y > 0$): $$S(x, y) = xy + \frac{64}{y} + \frac{64}{x}$$ 2. Find critical points ($S_x = 0, S_y = 0$): $$S_x = y - \frac{64}{x^2} = 0 \implies x^2 y = 64; \qquad S_y = x - \frac{64}{y^2} = 0 \implies x y^2 = 64$$ Dividing gives $x = y$, so $x^3 = 64 \implies \mathbf{x = 4\text{ m},\; y = 4\text{ m}}$, and $z = \dfrac{32}{16} = \mathbf{2\text{ m}}$.
3. Verify minimum via Second Derivative Test at $(4, 4)$:
$S_{xx} = \dfrac{128}{x^3} = 2 > 0$, $S_{yy} = \dfrac{128}{y^3} = 2$, $S_{xy} = 1 \implies D = (2)(2) - 1^2 = 3 > 0$. Since $D > 0$ and $S_{xx} > 0$, $(4, 4, 2)$ minimizes surface area ($S_{\min} = 48\text{ m}^2$).
