Core Theory & Formulas (Lecture 9 + Ashraful's Notes)

2.1 Chain Rule, Implicit & Logarithmic Differentiation

Technique Core Formula / Procedure When to Use It
Chain Rule $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{dy}{du}\cdot\dfrac{du}{dx}$   or   $(f \circ g)'(x) = f'(g(x))g'(x)$ Composite functions like $\cos(2x)$, $e^{7x}$, $\sqrt{4+3x}$.
Implicit Differentiation Differentiate both sides w.r.t. $x$, attaching $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ whenever differentiating a $y$-term, then solve for $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$. Equations mixing $x$ and $y$ that can't easily be solved for $y$, e.g., $x^3 + y^3 = 3xy$ or $x^{2/3} + y^{2/3} = 4$.
Logarithmic Differentiation Take $\ln$ of both sides, expand using log laws, differentiate implicitly ($\frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx} = \dots$), and multiply by $y$. Variable exponents like $y = x^{\sin x}$, or complicated products/quotients with roots & powers.

2.2 Local Linear Approximation & Differentials (Lecture 9 Slides)

Near $x_0$, the tangent line $y = f(x_0) + f'(x_0)(x - x_0)$ is the best linear approximation to $f(x)$: $$f(x) \approx f(x_0) + f'(x_0)(x - x_0) \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad f(x_0 + \Delta x) \approx f(x_0) + f'(x_0)\Delta x$$ Setting $dx = \Delta x$, the differential $dy$ is the tangent-line rise: $$dy = f'(x)\,dx \qquad \text{which approximates the exact change} \qquad \Delta y = f(x + \Delta x) - f(x)$$

Error Metric Exact Formula Differential Approximation
Propagated Error $\Delta q = q_{\text{measured}} - q_{\text{true}}$ $dq = f'(x)\,dx$
Relative Error $\dfrac{\Delta q}{q}$ $\dfrac{dq}{q}$
Percentage Error $\dfrac{\Delta q}{q} \times 100\%$ $\dfrac{dq}{q} \times 100\%$
Important Engineering Approximation (Lecture 9 Ex. 34): For small $x$, $(1 + x)^k \approx 1 + kx$ (the first two terms of the Binomial expansion!). For example, $(1.001)^{37} \approx 1 + 37(0.001) = 1.037$ (exact: $1.03767$). Also memorize at $x_0 = 0$: $\sin x \approx x$, $\tan x \approx x$, $e^x \approx 1 + x$, and $\ln(1 + x) \approx x$.

Example 1: Propagated Error in Square Area & Sphere Volume

(a) A square's side is measured as $10\text{ in}$ with error at most $\pm\frac{1}{32}\text{ in}$. Estimate the area error.
(b) A polyurethane sphere's diameter is measured within $\pm 0.4\%$. Estimate the percentage error in its volume.

Part (a) — Differential of Area: $y = x^2 \implies dy = 2x\,dx$. At $x = 10$, $dy = 20\,dx$.
Part (a) — Apply Error Bound: Since $-\frac{1}{32} \le dx \le \frac{1}{32}$, multiplying by $20$ gives $-\frac{5}{8} \le dy \le \frac{5}{8}$. Estimated area error is within $\pm\dfrac{5}{8}\text{ in}^2$.
Part (b) — Relative Error of Volume: $V = \dfrac{\pi}{6}D^3 \implies dV = \dfrac{\pi}{2}D^2\,dD$. Dividing by $V$: $\dfrac{dV}{V} = \dfrac{\frac{\pi}{2}D^2\,dD}{\frac{\pi}{6}D^3} = 3\dfrac{dD}{D}$.
Part (b) — Percentage Error: Since $\left|\frac{dD}{D}\right| \le 0.4\%$, we get $\left|\frac{dV}{V}\right| \le 3(0.4\%) = \mathbf{\pm 1.2\%}$.

Example 2: Differentiate $y = x^{\sin x}$

Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for $y = x^{\sin x}$ ($x > 0$).

Step 1 — Take $\ln$ of Both Sides: $\ln y = \ln(x^{\sin x}) = (\sin x)\ln x$.
Step 2 — Differentiate Implicitly (Product Rule on RHS): $\dfrac{1}{y}\dfrac{dy}{dx} = (\sin x)\cdot\dfrac{1}{x} + (\cos x)\ln x$.
Step 3 — Multiply by $y = x^{\sin x}$: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = x^{\sin x}\left(\dfrac{\sin x}{x} + \cos x\ln x\right)$.

Example 3: Local Linear Approximation of $\sin 2^\circ$

Find the local linear approximation of $f(x) = \sin x$ at $x_0 = 0$ and use it to approximate $\sin 2^\circ$.

Step 1 — Tangent Line at $x_0 = 0$: $f(0) = \sin 0 = 0$ and $f'(0) = \cos 0 = 1$, so $\sin x \approx 0 + 1(x - 0) = x$ (for $x$ in radians!).
Step 2 — Convert Degrees to Radians: $2^\circ = 2 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{180} = \dfrac{\pi}{90} \approx \mathbf{0.034907}\text{ rad}$.
Step 3 — Compare with Calculator: Approximation $\sin 2^\circ \approx 0.034907$; calculator value is $0.034899$ (error $< 0.000008$!).

2nd In-course 2024 Q6 & Q7(a) + Final 2024 2 + 5 = 7 Marks

(a) Given that $f'(9) = 5$, $g(2) = 9$, and $g'(2) = -3$, find $(f \circ g)'(2)$.
(b) Use implicit differentiation to find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for the Folium of Descartes: $x^3 + y^3 = 3xy$.

Part (a) (Chain Rule):
$$(f \circ g)'(2) = f'(g(2)) \cdot g'(2) = f'(9) \cdot (-3) = 5(-3) = \mathbf{-15}$$ Part (b) (Implicit Differentiation):
Differentiate $x^3 + y^3 = 3xy$ with respect to $x$ (using Product Rule on $3xy$): $$3x^2 + 3y^2\frac{dy}{dx} = 3y + 3x\frac{dy}{dx}$$ Divide by $3$ and collect $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ terms: $$y^2\frac{dy}{dx} - x\frac{dy}{dx} = y - x^2 \implies (y^2 - x)\frac{dy}{dx} = y - x^2 \implies \mathbf{\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y - x^2}{y^2 - x}}$$
2nd In-course 2025 Q1(a) & Year Final 2025 3–4 Marks

Question: A $10\text{ ft}$ ladder leans against a wall at an angle $\theta$ with the horizontal. The top of the ladder is $x\text{ ft}$ above the ground. If the bottom of the ladder is pushed toward the wall, find the rate at which $x$ changes with respect to $\theta$ when $\theta = 60^\circ$.

Full Solution:
From the right triangle formed by the ladder (hypotenuse $= 10\text{ ft}$) and wall (opposite $= x$): $$\sin\theta = \frac{x}{10} \implies x = 10\sin\theta$$ Differentiate with respect to $\theta$ (in radians): $$\frac{dx}{d\theta} = 10\cos\theta$$ Evaluate at $\theta = 60^\circ = \dfrac{\pi}{3}\text{ rad}$: $$\left.\frac{dx}{d\theta}\right|_{\theta = 60^\circ} = 10\cos(60^\circ) = 10\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = \mathbf{5\text{ ft/rad}}$$
Year Final Exam 2025 • Q4(b) & Q4(d) 3 + 4 = 7 Marks

(b) If $y = \sqrt{x}$, find formulas for $\Delta y$ and $dy$. Evaluate $\Delta y$ and $dy$ at $x = 4$ with $dx = \Delta x = 3$ and sketch them.
(d) Coffee is poured at a uniform rate of $200\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$ into a cup shaped like a truncated cone (upper radius $4\text{ cm}$, lower radius $2\text{ cm}$, height $6\text{ cm}$). How fast is the coffee level rising when the coffee is halfway up?

Hint: Express radius $r$ linearly in terms of height $h$: at $h=0$, $r=2$; at $h=6$, $r=4 \implies r(h) = 2 + \frac{h}{3}$. Even faster: $dV/dh$ is simply the cross-sectional area $\pi r^2$, so $\frac{dV}{dt} = \pi r^2 \frac{dh}{dt}$!
Part (b) (Matches Lecture 9 Example 4!):
• $\Delta y = \sqrt{x + \Delta x} - \sqrt{x}$ and $dy = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}\,dx$.
• At $x = 4, dx = \Delta x = 3$: $\Delta y = \sqrt{7} - \sqrt{4} \approx \mathbf{0.646}$, and $dy = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{4}}(3) = \mathbf{0.75}$. *(See Tab 1 for the exact sketch!)*

Part (d) (Truncated Cone Coffee Cup):
At height $h \in [0, 6]$, the radius increases linearly from $2$ to $4$: $r(h) = 2 + \dfrac{4 - 2}{6}h = 2 + \dfrac{h}{3}$.
Volume of the frustum up to height $h$: $$V = \frac{\pi h}{3}\left(r^2 + 2r + 2^2\right) = \frac{\pi}{3}\left(\frac{h^3}{9} + 2h^2 + 12h\right)$$ Differentiate w.r.t. $t$: $$\frac{dV}{dt} = \pi\left(\frac{h^2}{9} + \frac{4h}{3} + 4\right)\frac{dh}{dt} = \pi\left(2 + \frac{h}{3}\right)^2 \frac{dh}{dt} = \pi r^2 \frac{dh}{dt}$$ Halfway up means $h = 3\text{ cm} \implies r = 2 + \frac{3}{3} = 3\text{ cm}$. With $\frac{dV}{dt} = 200$: $$200 = \pi(3)^2 \frac{dh}{dt} \implies \mathbf{\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{200}{9\pi} \approx 7.074\text{ cm/s}}$$
