Core Theory (Lecture 4 Slides + Ashraful's Notes)

2.1 Rational Functions, Lowest Terms & Holes vs. Vertical Asymptotes

A rational function has the form $R(x) = \dfrac{p(x)}{q(x)}$, where $p$ and $q$ are polynomials and $q(x)$ is not the zero polynomial. Its domain is all real numbers except where $q(x) = 0$.

CRITICAL RULE — Always Find Domain FIRST, Then Reduce to Lowest Terms! For example, $R(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}$ reduces to $x + 1$, but $R(x) \ne x + 1$ because $x = 1$ is excluded from the domain! Any common factor $(x - c)$ that cancels out from numerator and denominator creates a hole (removable discontinuity) at $x = c$ — not a vertical asymptote. Only the remaining real zeros of the denominator in lowest terms are vertical asymptotes.
Ashraful's Shortcut (VA Multiplicity Behavior): Denominator-এর zero $x = c$ এর multiplicity odd হলে একদিকে $+\infty$ এবং অন্যদিকে $-\infty$ যাবে। আর multiplicity even হলে দুই দিক থেকেই একই দিকে (হয় দুইদিকেই $+\infty$, নাহলে দুইদিকেই $-\infty$) যাবে!

2.2 The 4-Case Rule for Horizontal & Oblique (Slant) Asymptotes

Let $n = \deg(p)$ with leading coefficient $a_n$, and $m = \deg(q)$ with leading coefficient $b_m$:

Case Degree Comparison Asymptote / End Behavior Lecture 4 Slide Example
Case 1 $n < m$ (Proper) Horizontal Asymptote: $y = 0$ ($x$-axis) $R(x) = \dfrac{4x^3 - 5x + 2}{7x^5 + 2x^4 - 3x} \implies y = 0$
Case 2 $n = m$ Horizontal Asymptote: $y = \dfrac{a_n}{b_m}$ $R(x) = \dfrac{8x^2 - x + 2}{4x^2 - 1} \implies y = \dfrac{8}{4} = 2$
Case 3 $n = m + 1$ Oblique (Slant) Asymptote: $y = ax + b$ (linear quotient via polynomial long division) $H(x) = \dfrac{3x^4 - x^2}{x^3 - x^2 + 1} \implies y = 3x + 3$
Case 4 $n \ge m + 2$ No HA or OA. End behavior resembles the power function $y = \dfrac{a_n}{b_m}x^{n-m}$. $G(x) = \dfrac{2x^5 - x^3 + 2}{x^3 - 1} \sim y = 2x^2$
Can a Graph Cross Its Own Asymptote? • Vertical Asymptote: NEVER! The function is undefined at $x = c$.
• Horizontal / Oblique Asymptote: YES! Solve $R(x) = L$ (or $R(x) = ax + b$) to find any exact crossing points!

2.3 The 6-Step Rational Function Analysis Procedure

  1. Factor & Find Domain: Factor $p(x)$ and $q(x)$; exclude all zeros of $q(x)$.
  2. Reduce to Lowest Terms: Cancel common factors (record any holes!).
  3. Intercepts: $x$-intercepts from zeros of reduced numerator (check multiplicity: odd = cross, even = touch); $y$-intercept is $R(0)$ if $0 \in \text{Domain}$.
  4. Vertical Asymptotes: Zeros of reduced denominator + check odd/even multiplicity behavior.
  5. Horizontal / Oblique Asymptote & Intersections: Apply the 4-Case degree rule, then solve $R(x) = \text{asymptote}$ for crossings.
  6. Sign-Region Table & Sketch: Divide the $x$-axis using all $x$-intercepts and VAs, test one value per region (Above / Below $x$-axis), and connect smoothly!

Example 1: Full Analysis of $R(x) = \dfrac{x - 1}{x^2 - 4}$

Analyze $R(x) = \dfrac{x - 1}{x^2 - 4}$ step by step, including whether it crosses its horizontal asymptote.

Step 1 & 2 — Factor, Domain & Lowest Terms: $R(x) = \dfrac{x - 1}{(x + 2)(x - 2)}$. Domain $= \mathbb{R} - \{-2, 2\}$. No common factors, so it is already in lowest terms (no holes).
Step 3 — Intercepts: $x$-intercept: $x - 1 = 0 \implies (1, 0)$ (mult 1, crosses). $y$-intercept: $R(0) = \dfrac{-1}{-4} = \dfrac{1}{4} \implies \left(0, \dfrac{1}{4}\right)$.
Step 4 — Vertical Asymptotes: $x = -2$ and $x = 2$, both with odd multiplicity ($1$) → opposite infinities on each side.
Step 5 — HA & Crossing Check: Since $n = 1 < m = 2$, HA is $y = 0$. Solving $R(x) = 0 \implies x = 1$, so the graph crosses the HA $y = 0$ at $(1, 0)$.
Step 6 — Sign Regions: Split at $-2, 1, 2$: on $(-\infty, -2)$ test $-3 \implies (-)$ below; on $(-2, 1)$ test $0 \implies (+)$ above; on $(1, 2)$ test $1.5 \implies (-)$ below; on $(2, \infty)$ test $3 \implies (+)$ above.
Hole + HA Example (Ashraful's Notes)

Example 2: Analyze $R(x) = \dfrac{2x^2 - 5x + 2}{x^2 - 4}$

Find the domain, hole coordinates, intercepts, and asymptotes of $R(x) = \dfrac{2x^2 - 5x + 2}{x^2 - 4}$.

Step 1 — Factor & Domain: $R(x) = \dfrac{(2x - 1)(x - 2)}{(x + 2)(x - 2)}$. Domain $= \mathbb{R} - \{-2, 2\}$.
Step 2 — Lowest Terms & Hole: Cancel $(x - 2)$ to get $R(x) = \dfrac{2x - 1}{x + 2}$ for $x \ne 2$. Plugging $x = 2$ into the reduced form gives $y = \dfrac{3}{4}$, so there is a hole at $\left(2, \dfrac{3}{4}\right)$!
Step 3 — Intercepts & VA: $x$-intercept at $\left(\frac{1}{2}, 0\right)$ (crosses); $y$-intercept at $\left(0, -\frac{1}{2}\right)$. Only VA is $x = -2$ (odd mult 1).
Step 4 — HA & Crossing Check: $n = m \implies$ HA is $y = \dfrac{2}{1} = 2$. Check intersection: $\dfrac{2x - 1}{x + 2} = 2 \implies 2x - 1 = 2x + 4 \implies -1 = 4$ (impossible), so it never crosses $y = 2$.

Example 3: Oblique Asymptote of $H(x) = \dfrac{3x^4 - x^2}{x^3 - x^2 + 1}$

Find the oblique asymptote of $H(x) = \dfrac{3x^4 - x^2}{x^3 - x^2 + 1}$ using polynomial long division.

Step 1 — Check Degrees: Numerator degree $n = 4$, denominator degree $m = 3$. Since $n = m + 1$, an oblique asymptote exists.
Step 2 — First Division Term: $\dfrac{3x^4}{x^3} = 3x$. Multiply $3x(x^3 - x^2 + 1) = 3x^4 - 3x^3 + 3x$ and subtract from $3x^4 + 0x^3 - x^2 + 0x + 0$ to get $3x^3 - x^2 - 3x$.
Step 3 — Second Division Term: $\dfrac{3x^3}{x^3} = 3$. Multiply $3(x^3 - x^2 + 1) = 3x^3 - 3x^2 + 3$ and subtract to leave remainder $2x^2 - 3x - 3$.
Step 4 — Conclude: $H(x) = (3x + 3) + \dfrac{2x^2 - 3x - 3}{x^3 - x^2 + 1}$. As $x \to \pm\infty$, the remainder fraction $\to 0$, so the oblique asymptote is $y = 3x + 3$.

1st In-course Exam 2025 • Q1 7 Marks (High Weight!)

Question: Analyse the rational function $f(x) = \dfrac{x^3 - 1}{x^2 - 9}$ and hence sketch the function $f(x)$.

Hint: $n = 3$ and $m = 2$, so $n = m + 1$ gives an oblique asymptote! Divide $x^3 - 1$ by $x^2 - 9$ to get $x + \frac{9x - 1}{x^2 - 9}$, and don't forget to check where $f(x)$ crosses $y = x$.
Full 6-Step Exam Solution:
  • 1. Factor & Domain: $f(x) = \dfrac{(x - 1)(x^2 + x + 1)}{(x + 3)(x - 3)}$. Domain $= \mathbb{R} - \{-3, 3\}$.
  • 2. Lowest Terms: No common factors → already in lowest terms (no holes).
  • 3. Intercepts: $x$-intercept at $(1, 0)$ (mult 1, crosses; note $x^2+x+1=0$ has no real roots). $y$-intercept: $f(0) = \dfrac{-1}{-9} = \dfrac{1}{9} \implies \left(0, \dfrac{1}{9}\right)$.
  • 4. Vertical Asymptotes: $x = -3$ and $x = 3$ (both odd mult 1 → opposite infinities on each side).
  • 5. Oblique Asymptote & Crossing: Long division gives $f(x) = x + \dfrac{9x - 1}{x^2 - 9}$, so OA is $y = x$. Setting $f(x) = x \implies 9x - 1 = 0 \implies x = \dfrac{1}{9}$. Thus the graph crosses $y = x$ at $\left(\dfrac{1}{9}, \dfrac{1}{9}\right)$.
  • 6. Sign Regions: $(-\infty, -3)$: $(-)$ below; $(-3, 1)$: $(+)$ above; $(1, 3)$: $(-)$ below; $(3, \infty)$: $(+)$ above. *(Inspect this exact graph in Tab 1 of the Sandbox!)*
Past Year Final • Q1(a) & Q1(b) 3 + 3 = 6 Marks

(a) Find the domain, vertical asymptotes, and horizontal/oblique asymptotes of $F(x) = \dfrac{x^4 + x^2 + 1}{x^3 - 1}$.
(b) Write a rational function (smallest degree possible) whose graph has: VA at $x = -5$ (left $+\infty$, right $-\infty$), VA at $x = 2$ (both sides $-\infty$), touches the $x$-axis at $(-2, 0)$, crosses the $x$-axis at $(5, 0)$, and has HA $y = 2$.

Part (a):
• Factor denominator: $x^3 - 1 = (x - 1)(x^2 + x + 1)$. Since $x^2 + x + 1$ has discriminant $-3 < 0$, the only real zero is $x = 1$. Domain: $\mathbb{R} - \{1\}$.
• Check numerator at $x = 1$: $1^4 + 1^2 + 1 = 3 \ne 0$, so lowest terms → VA: $x = 1$.
• Since $n = 4 = m + 1$, perform long division: $F(x) = x + \dfrac{x^2 + x + 1}{x^3 - 1} \implies$ Oblique Asymptote: $y = x$ (no HA).

Part (b) (Also in Ashraful's Notes!):
• Numerator zeros: touches at $-2 \implies (x + 2)^2$; crosses at $5 \implies (x - 5)^1$.
• Denominator VAs: opposite sides at $-5 \implies (x + 5)^1$; same side ($-\infty$) at $2 \implies (x - 2)^2$.
• Notice $\deg(\text{num}) = 3 = \deg(\text{den})$, so the HA is $y = a$. Since HA is $y = 2$, we get $a = 2$:
$$R(x) = \frac{2(x + 2)^2(x - 5)}{(x + 5)(x - 2)^2}$$
Past Year Final • Q3(b) 6 Marks

Question: Define vertical asymptote and hole of a function. Find all asymptotes and holes of $f(x) = \dfrac{3x^2}{2x^2 - 5}$.

Definitions:
• Vertical Asymptote: A vertical line $x = c$ is a vertical asymptote of $f(x)$ if $|f(x)| \to \infty$ as $x \to c^-$ or $x \to c^+$. For a rational function in lowest terms, these occur at real zeros of the denominator.
• Hole (Removable Discontinuity): A point $(c, L)$ where $f(c)$ is undefined because $(x - c)$ cancels as a common factor from both numerator and denominator, yet $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L$ exists finitely.

For $f(x) = \dfrac{3x^2}{2x^2 - 5}$:
• Factor denominator: $2x^2 - 5 = 0 \implies x = \pm\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}}$. Neither makes the numerator $3x^2$ zero, so it is in lowest terms.
• Holes: None.
• Vertical Asymptotes: $x = \sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}}$ and $x = -\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}}$.
• Horizontal Asymptote: Since $n = m = 2$, $y = \dfrac{3}{2}$. (No oblique asymptote.)
