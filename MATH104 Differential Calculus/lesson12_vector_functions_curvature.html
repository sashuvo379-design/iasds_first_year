Core Theory & Formula Sheet (Lectures 12a–12c)

2.1 Vector-Valued Functions, Smoothness & Arc-Length Parametrization

A vector-valued function $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle f(t), g(t), h(t)\rangle = f(t)\hat{i} + g(t)\hat{j} + h(t)\hat{k}$ maps a scalar parameter $t$ to a position vector whose tip traces a curve as $t$ increases (giving its orientation).

  • Domain: Intersection of the domains of all component functions $f(t), g(t), h(t)$.
  • Limits & Continuity: Evaluated component-wise. If any single component limit DNE, the vector limit DNE.
  • Smoothness Condition: A parametrization $\mathbf{r}(t)$ is smooth on an interval if $\mathbf{r}'(t)$ is continuous and $\mathbf{r}'(t) \ne \mathbf{0}$ everywhere on the interval. (Smoothness depends on the parametrization, not just the shape!)
  • Orthogonality Property: If $\|\mathbf{r}(t)\| = c$ (constant length), then $\mathbf{r}(t) \cdot \mathbf{r}'(t) = 0$ (orthogonal!).
  • Arc Length Parameter: $s(t) = \displaystyle\int_{t_0}^{t} \|\mathbf{r}'(u)\|\,du$, so $\dfrac{ds}{dt} = \|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|$.

2.2 The T-N-B (Frenet) Frame & Curvature Formulas

Object General Parameter $t$ Formula Arc-Length $s$ / Geometric Meaning
Unit Tangent $\mathbf{T}(t)$ $\mathbf{T}(t) = \dfrac{\mathbf{r}'(t)}{\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|}$ $\mathbf{T}(s) = \mathbf{r}'(s)$; unit vector pointing along motion.
Principal Unit Normal $\mathbf{N}(t)$ $\mathbf{N}(t) = \dfrac{\mathbf{T}'(t)}{\|\mathbf{T}'(t)\|}$ $\mathbf{N}(s) = \dfrac{\mathbf{r}''(s)}{\|\mathbf{r}''(s)\|}$; perpendicular to $\mathbf{T}$, always points inward (concave side).
Binormal Vector $\mathbf{B}(t)$ $\mathbf{B}(t) = \mathbf{T}(t) \times \mathbf{N}(t) = \dfrac{\mathbf{r}'(t) \times \mathbf{r}''(t)}{\|\mathbf{r}'(t) \times \mathbf{r}''(t)\|}$ Perpendicular to the osculating plane spanned by $\mathbf{T}$ and $\mathbf{N}$.
Curvature $\kappa$ (3D Space) $\kappa(t) = \dfrac{\|\mathbf{T}'(t)\|}{\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|} = \dfrac{\|\mathbf{r}'(t) \times \mathbf{r}''(t)\|}{\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|^3}$ $\kappa(s) = \left\|\dfrac{d\mathbf{T}}{ds}\right\|$; measures how fast $\mathbf{T}$ turns per unit arc length.
Curvature (2D Parametric) $\kappa(t) = \dfrac{|x'y'' - y'x''|}{\left[(x')^2 + (y')^2\right]^{3/2}}$ Special case of 3D cross product with $z = 0$.
Curvature (Cartesian $y=f(x)$) $\kappa(x) = \dfrac{|y''|}{\left[1 + (y')^2\right]^{3/2}}$ Radius of curvature: $\rho = \dfrac{1}{\kappa}$. Center: $(x - \frac{y'(1+y'^2)}{y''},\; y + \frac{1+y'^2}{y''})$.
Curvature (Polar $r = r(\theta)$) $\kappa(\theta) = \dfrac{|r^2 + 2(r')^2 - r r''|}{\left[r^2 + (r')^2\right]^{3/2}}$ Obtained by setting $x = r\cos\theta,\; y = r\sin\theta$ in the 2D parametric formula.
Watch Out (Ashraful's Note): $\mathbf{N}(t) = \dfrac{\mathbf{T}'(t)}{\|\mathbf{T}'(t)\|}$ is always correct, whereas $\dfrac{\mathbf{r}''(t)}{\|\mathbf{r}''(t)\|}$ is generally NOT equal to $\mathbf{N}(t)$ unless the curve is parametrized by arc length $s$ (constant speed)!

Guided Worked Examples (Ashraful's Notes)

Ashraful's Notes • Parametric Curvature

Example 1: Curvature of the Ellipse $\mathbf{r}(t) = 2\cos t\,\hat{i} + 3\sin t\,\hat{j}$

Find $\kappa(t)$ for the ellipse and evaluate it at the minor-axis vertex $(2, 0)$ and major-axis vertex $(0, 3)$.

Step 1 — Derivatives: $\mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle -2\sin t, 3\cos t, 0\rangle$,   $\mathbf{r}''(t) = \langle -2\cos t, -3\sin t, 0\rangle$.
Step 2 — Cross Product & Speed: $\mathbf{r}'(t) \times \mathbf{r}''(t) = (6\sin^2 t + 6\cos^2 t)\hat{k} = 6\hat{k}$, so $\|\mathbf{r}' \times \mathbf{r}''\| = 6$. And $\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\| = \sqrt{4\sin^2 t + 9\cos^2 t} = \sqrt{4 + 5\cos^2 t}$.
Step 3 — Curvature Formula & Vertices: $\kappa(t) = \dfrac{6}{(4 + 5\cos^2 t)^{3/2}}$.
• At $(2, 0)$ ($t = 0$): $\kappa(0) = \dfrac{6}{9^{3/2}} = \dfrac{6}{27} = \mathbf{\dfrac{2}{9}}$ (flattest!).
• At $(0, 3)$ ($t = \frac{\pi}{2}$): $\kappa\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \dfrac{6}{4^{3/2}} = \dfrac{6}{8} = \mathbf{\dfrac{3}{4}}$ (sharpest bend!).
Ashraful's Notes • Complete T-N-B Frame

Example 2: Find $\mathbf{T}(t), \mathbf{N}(t), \mathbf{B}(t)$ for $\mathbf{r}(t) = e^t\sin t\,\hat{i} + e^t\cos t\,\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$

Compute the full moving Frenet frame for this logarithmic spiral in the plane $z = 3$.

Step 1 — $\mathbf{r}'(t)$ & Speed: $\mathbf{r}'(t) = e^t(\sin t + \cos t)\hat{i} + e^t(\cos t - \sin t)\hat{j}$. Using $(a+b)^2 + (a-b)^2 = 2(a^2+b^2)$, $\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\| = \sqrt{2}e^t$.
Step 2 — Unit Tangent $\mathbf{T}(t)$: Divide by $\sqrt{2}e^t$: $\mathbf{T}(t) = \dfrac{\sin t + \cos t}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{i} + \dfrac{\cos t - \sin t}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{j}$.
Step 3 — Unit Normal $\mathbf{N}(t)$: $\mathbf{T}'(t) = \dfrac{\cos t - \sin t}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{i} - \dfrac{\sin t + \cos t}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{j}$. Since $\|\mathbf{T}'(t)\| = 1$, $\mathbf{N}(t) = \mathbf{T}'(t)$.
Step 4 — Binormal $\mathbf{B}(t)$: $\mathbf{B}(t) = \mathbf{T}(t) \times \mathbf{N}(t) = \mathbf{-\hat{k}}$ (constant, because the curve lies completely in the horizontal plane $z = 3$!).
Ashraful's Notes • Arc-Length Parametrization

Example 3: Reparametrize the Helix $\mathbf{r}(t) = \cos t\,\hat{i} + \sin t\,\hat{j} + t\,\hat{k}$ by Arc Length $s$

Find $\mathbf{r}(s)$ measured from $t = 0$, and locate the point at arc-length distance $s = 10$.

Step 1 — Speed $\|\mathbf{r}'(u)\|$: $\mathbf{r}'(u) = \langle -\sin u, \cos u, 1\rangle \implies \|\mathbf{r}'(u)\| = \sqrt{\sin^2 u + \cos^2 u + 1} = \sqrt{2}$.
Step 2 — Integrate for $s(t)$: $s = \int_0^t \sqrt{2}\,du = \sqrt{2}\,t \implies t = \dfrac{s}{\sqrt{2}}$.
Step 3 — Substitute Back: $\mathbf{r}(s) = \cos\!\left(\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\hat{i} + \sin\!\left(\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\hat{j} + \dfrac{s}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{k}$. At $s = 10$: $\left(\cos\frac{10}{\sqrt{2}},\; \sin\frac{10}{\sqrt{2}},\; \frac{10}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$.

Year Final Exam 2025 • Q7(a) 4 Marks

Question: Given the vector-valued function $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle t, t^2, t^3\rangle$: (i) Find the unit tangent vector $\mathbf{T}(t)$; (ii) Determine the unit normal vector $\mathbf{N}(t)$ at $t = 1$.

Hint: To compute $\mathbf{N}(1)$ cleanly without messy quotient-rule algebra on all $t$, differentiate $\mathbf{T}(t) = \frac{\mathbf{r}'(t)}{\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|}$ using the product rule and plug in $t = 1$ right away!
(i) Unit Tangent $\mathbf{T}(t)$:
$\mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle 1, 2t, 3t^2\rangle$ and $\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\| = \sqrt{1 + 4t^2 + 9t^4}$. Therefore: $$\mathbf{T}(t) = \frac{\langle 1,\; 2t,\; 3t^2\rangle}{\sqrt{1 + 4t^2 + 9t^4}}$$ (ii) Unit Normal $\mathbf{N}(1)$:
Let $v(t) = \|\mathbf{r}'(t)\| = (1 + 4t^2 + 9t^4)^{1/2}$. At $t = 1$: $v(1) = \sqrt{14}$, and $v'(t) = \dfrac{4t + 18t^3}{v(t)} \implies v'(1) = \dfrac{22}{\sqrt{14}}$.
Also $\mathbf{r}'(1) = \langle 1, 2, 3\rangle$ and $\mathbf{r}''(1) = \langle 0, 2, 6\rangle$. By the quotient rule: $$\mathbf{T}'(1) = \frac{v(1)\mathbf{r}''(1) - v'(1)\mathbf{r}'(1)}{[v(1)]^2} = \frac{\sqrt{14}\langle 0, 2, 6\rangle - \frac{22}{\sqrt{14}}\langle 1, 2, 3\rangle}{14} = \frac{\langle -22,\; -16,\; 18\rangle}{14\sqrt{14}} = \frac{\langle -11,\; -8,\; 9\rangle}{7\sqrt{14}}$$ Normalize $\langle -11, -8, 9\rangle$: length is $\sqrt{121 + 64 + 81} = \sqrt{266}$. Thus: $$\mathbf{N}(1) = \frac{\langle -11,\; -8,\; 9\rangle}{\sqrt{266}}$$ *(Check: $\mathbf{T}(1) \cdot \mathbf{N}(1) \propto 1(-11) + 2(-8) + 3(9) = -11 - 16 + 27 = 0$ ✓!)*
Year Final Exam 2025 • Q7(b) & Q7(c) 4 + 4 = 8 Marks

(b) For the plane curve $y = \ln x$, derive the curvature $\kappa(x)$ and evaluate it at $x = 1$.
(c) A curve is given in polar coordinates by $r(\theta) = 1 + \cos\theta$. Find the radius of curvature at $\theta = \pi$, and determine the coordinates of the center of curvature at this point.

Part (b) ($y = \ln x$):
$y' = \dfrac{1}{x}$ and $y'' = -\dfrac{1}{x^2}$. Substitute into $\kappa(x) = \dfrac{|y''|}{[1 + (y')^2]^{3/2}}$: $$\kappa(x) = \frac{1/x^2}{\left(1 + \frac{1}{x^2}\right)^{3/2}} = \frac{1/x^2}{(x^2 + 1)^{3/2} / x^3} = \mathbf{\frac{x}{(x^2 + 1)^{3/2}}} \;\;(x > 0)$$ At $x = 1$: $\kappa(1) = \dfrac{1}{2^{3/2}} = \mathbf{\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}}$ (and radius $\rho(1) = 2\sqrt{2}$).

Part (c) (Cardioid $r(\theta) = 1 + \cos\theta$):
We have $r = 1 + \cos\theta$, $r' = -\sin\theta$, $r'' = -\cos\theta$. Compute the polar curvature numerator & denominator:
• $r^2 + (r')^2 = (1 + 2\cos\theta + \cos^2\theta) + \sin^2\theta = 2(1 + \cos\theta) = 4\cos^2(\theta/2)$.
• $r^2 + 2(r')^2 - rr'' = (1 + 2\cos\theta + \cos^2\theta) + 2\sin^2\theta + \cos\theta(1 + \cos\theta) = 3(1 + \cos\theta) = 6\cos^2(\theta/2)$.
Therefore, for $\theta \ne \pi$: $$\kappa(\theta) = \frac{6\cos^2(\theta/2)}{\left[4\cos^2(\theta/2)\right]^{3/2}} = \frac{3}{4\left|\cos(\theta/2)\right|} \implies \rho(\theta) = \frac{1}{\kappa(\theta)} = \mathbf{\frac{4}{3}\left|\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\right|}$$ At $\theta = \pi$ (the cardioid's cusp at the origin $(0,0)$): $\rho(\pi) = \dfrac{4}{3}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = \mathbf{0}$ (limiting radius of curvature is $0$, since $\kappa \to \infty$ at a sharp cusp!), so the limiting center of curvature collapses right onto the cusp at $(0, 0)$! *(Compare with $\theta = 0$ in Tab 2, where $\rho(0) = \frac{4}{3}$ and center is $(\frac{2}{3}, 0)$.)*
Past Year Final • Q8(a) 6 Marks (Core Derivation!)

Question: Define curvature and radius of curvature. Show that the curvature of a plane parametric curve $x = x(t),\; y = y(t)$ is $$\kappa(t) = \frac{|x'y'' - y'x''|}{\left[(x')^2 + (y')^2\right]^{3/2}}$$

Definitions:
• Curvature ($\kappa$): The rate at which the unit tangent vector $\mathbf{T}$ turns with respect to arc length $s$: $\kappa = \left\|\dfrac{d\mathbf{T}}{ds}\right\|$.
• Radius of Curvature ($\rho$): The reciprocal of curvature, $\rho = \dfrac{1}{\kappa}$ (for $\kappa \ne 0$), which equals the radius of the best-fitting osculating circle at that point.

Derivation: Embed the 2D curve in 3D space as $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle x(t), y(t), 0\rangle$.
1. Differentiate twice: $\mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle x', y', 0\rangle$ and $\mathbf{r}''(t) = \langle x'', y'', 0\rangle$.
2. Compute the cross product: $$\mathbf{r}'(t) \times \mathbf{r}''(t) = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ x' & y' & 0 \\ x'' & y'' & 0 \end{vmatrix} = (x'y'' - y'x'')\hat{k} \implies \|\mathbf{r}'(t) \times \mathbf{r}''(t)\| = |x'y'' - y'x''|$$ 3. Speed is $\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\| = \sqrt{(x')^2 + (y')^2}$, so $\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|^3 = \left[(x')^2 + (y')^2\right]^{3/2}$.
4. Substitute into $\kappa(t) = \dfrac{\|\mathbf{r}'(t) \times \mathbf{r}''(t)\|}{\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|^3}$: $$\kappa(t) = \frac{|x'y'' - y'x''|}{\left[(x')^2 + (y')^2\right]^{3/2}} \quad \blacksquare$$
