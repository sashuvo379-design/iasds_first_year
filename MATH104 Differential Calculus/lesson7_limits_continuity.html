Core Theory (Lecture 7, 7a + Ashraful's Notes)

2.1 Limit Existence, Indeterminate Forms & Limits at Infinity

A two-sided limit $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$ exists if and only if both one-sided limits exist and are equal: $$\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = L$$

Limit Situation Algebraic Strategy Key Formula / Example
Indeterminate $\dfrac{0}{0}$ Factor & cancel common factor $(x - a)$, or multiply by the radical conjugate. $\lim_{x\to 1}\dfrac{x-1}{\sqrt{x}-1} = \lim_{x\to 1}(\sqrt{x}+1) = 2$
Limits as $x \to \pm\infty$ Divide numerator & denominator by highest power of $x$. Watch $\sqrt{x^2} = |x| = \begin{cases} +x, & x>0 \\ -x, & x<0 \end{cases}$! $\lim_{x\to +\infty} \dfrac{4x^3 - x}{2x^3 - 5} = 2$;   $\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\dfrac{1}{x^p} = 0\;(p>0)$
Trigonometric Limits Rewrite using $\lim_{u\to 0}\dfrac{\sin u}{u} = 1$ and $\lim_{u\to 0}\dfrac{1 - \cos u}{u} = 0$. $\lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{\sin(kx)}{x} = k$;   $\lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{\tan(ax)}{\sin(bx)} = \dfrac{a}{b}$
Squeeze (Sandwich) Theorem If $g(x) \le f(x) \le h(x)$ near $a$ and $\lim_{x\to a} g(x) = \lim_{x\to a} h(x) = L$, then $\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = L$. $-x^2 \le x^2\sin(1/x) \le x^2 \implies \lim_{x\to 0} x^2\sin(1/x) = 0$
Ashraful's Note on Non-Existent Limits: • $\lim_{x\to 0} \sin(\pi/x)$ → কোথায় আছে তা বুঝা যায় না (oscillates infinitely between $-1$ and $1$, so DNE!).
• $\lim_{x\to 3^+} \frac{x}{x-3} = +\infty$ এবং $\lim_{x\to 3^-} \frac{x}{x-3} = -\infty$ → লিমিটের finite value নাই (DNE)।

2.2 Formal $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ (Def 1.4.1) & $\varepsilon$-$N$ Definitions

  • Finite Limit ($\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$): For every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists $\delta > 0$ such that $$0 < |x - a| < \delta \implies |f(x) - L| < \varepsilon$$
  • Limit at Infinity ($\lim_{x \to +\infty} f(x) = L$): For every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists $N > 0$ such that $$x > N \implies |f(x) - L| < \varepsilon$$

2.3 Continuity at a Point, on $[a, b]$, Discontinuities & IVT

A function $f(x)$ is continuous at $x = c$ if and only if all three conditions hold:

  1. $f(c)$ is defined;
  2. $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ exists (i.e., LHL $=$ RHL);
  3. $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = f(c)$.

Continuity on a closed interval $[a, b]$: Continuous on open $(a, b)$, continuous from the right at $a$ ($\lim_{x\to a^+} f(x) = f(a)$), and continuous from the left at $b$ ($\lim_{x\to b^-} f(x) = f(b)$).

Removable Discontinuity vs. Jump / Infinite Discontinuity: A discontinuity at $x = c$ is removable if $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L$ exists finitely, so redefining $f(c) = L$ makes $f$ continuous (e.g., a hole). If $\text{LHL} \ne \text{RHL}$ (jump) or $\pm\infty$, it is non-removable.
Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT) — Root Existence Shortcut: যদি $f(x)$ closed interval $[a, b]$-এ continuous হয় এবং $f(a)$ ও $f(b)$ বিপরীত চিহ্নের হয় (অর্থাৎ $f(a)\cdot f(b) < 0$), তবে $(a, b)$-এর মাঝে অন্তত একটি root $c$ আছে যেখানে $f(c) = 0$!

Ashraful's Notes • Definition 1.4.1

Example 1: Formal $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ Proof of $\lim_{x \to 2} (3x - 1) = 5$

Use Definition 1.4.1 to prove that $\lim_{x \to 2} (3x - 1) = 5$.

Step 1 — Set up the Target Inequality: Given any $\varepsilon > 0$, we need $\delta > 0$ such that $0 < |x - 2| < \delta \implies |(3x - 1) - 5| < \varepsilon$.
Step 2 — Factor $|f(x) - L|$: $|(3x - 1) - 5| = |3x - 6| = 3|x - 2|$.
Step 3 — Choose $\delta$: We want $3|x - 2| < \varepsilon \iff |x - 2| < \dfrac{\varepsilon}{3}$. So choose $\delta = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{3}$.
Step 4 — Formal Verification: Whenever $0 < |x - 2| < \delta = \frac{\varepsilon}{3}$, we have $|(3x - 1) - 5| = 3|x - 2| < 3\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\right) = \varepsilon$. Hence $\lim_{x \to 2}(3x - 1) = 5$. $\blacksquare$
Ashraful's Notes • Closed Interval Continuity

Example 2: Prove $f(x) = \sqrt{9 - x^2}$ is Continuous on $[-3, 3]$

Verify all three closed-interval continuity requirements on $[-3, 3]$.

Step 1 — Open Interval $(-3, 3)$: Let $c \in (-3, 3)$. Then $f(c) = \sqrt{9 - c^2}$ is defined, and $\lim_{x \to c} \sqrt{9 - x^2} = \sqrt{9 - c^2} = f(c)$. So $f$ is continuous on $(-3, 3)$.
Step 2 — Right-Continuity at $a = -3$: $f(-3) = \sqrt{9 - 9} = 0$, and $\lim_{x \to -3^+} \sqrt{9 - x^2} = 0 = f(-3)$.
Step 3 — Left-Continuity at $b = 3$: $f(3) = 0$, and $\lim_{x \to 3^-} \sqrt{9 - x^2} = 0 = f(3)$. Therefore, $f$ is continuous on $[-3, 3]$.
Ashraful's Notes • IVT Proof

Example 3: Crossing Curves Theorem via IVT

Prove: If $f$ and $g$ are continuous on $[a, b]$ with $f(a) > g(a)$ and $f(b) < g(b)$, then $f(x) = g(x)$ has at least one solution in $(a, b)$.

Step 1 — Define Helper Function: Let $h(x) = f(x) - g(x)$. Since $f$ and $g$ are continuous on $[a, b]$, their difference $h(x)$ is also continuous on $[a, b]$.
Step 2 — Check Endpoint Signs: At $x = a$, $h(a) = f(a) - g(a) > 0$. At $x = b$, $h(b) = f(b) - g(b) < 0$.
Step 3 — Apply IVT: Because $0$ lies between $h(a) > 0$ and $h(b) < 0$, the IVT guarantees at least one $c \in (a, b)$ such that $h(c) = 0 \implies f(c) = g(c)$. $\blacksquare$

1st In-course 2024 Q7 & Year Final 2024 3 Marks (Classic Repeat!)

Question: Find values of the constants $k$ and $m$, if possible, that will make the function $f$ continuous everywhere: $$f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2 + 5, & x > 2 \\ m(x + 1) + k, & -1 < x \le 2 \\ 2x^3 + x + 7, & x \le -1 \end{cases}$$

Hint: Each piece is a polynomial (already continuous on its interior), so you only need to match LHL $=$ RHL at the two boundary points $x = -1$ and $x = 2$!
Full Solution:
At $x = -1$:
• $\text{LHL} = \lim_{x \to -1^-} (2x^3 + x + 7) = 2(-1)^3 + (-1) + 7 = -2 - 1 + 7 = 4$.
• $\text{RHL} = \lim_{x \to -1^+} [m(x + 1) + k] = m(0) + k = k$.
Setting $\text{LHL} = \text{RHL} \implies \mathbf{k = 4}$.

At $x = 2$:
• $\text{LHL} = \lim_{x \to 2^-} [m(x + 1) + k] = 3m + k = 3m + 4$.
• $\text{RHL} = \lim_{x \to 2^+} (x^2 + 5) = 2^2 + 5 = 9$.
Setting $\text{LHL} = \text{RHL} \implies 3m + 4 = 9 \implies 3m = 5 \implies \mathbf{m = \dfrac{5}{3}}$.

1st In-course Exam 2025 • Q3 4 Marks

Question: A positive number $\varepsilon$ and the limit $L$ of a function at $x \to \infty$ are given: $$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1}{x^2} = 0; \qquad \varepsilon = 0.01$$ Find a positive number $N$ such that $|f(x) - L| < \varepsilon$ if $x > N$.

Full Solution:
We require $\left|\dfrac{1}{x^2} - 0\right| < 0.01$ for $x > N > 0$.
Since $x > 0$, $\dfrac{1}{x^2} < 0.01 = \dfrac{1}{100} \iff x^2 > 100 \iff x > \sqrt{100} = 10$.
Therefore, choosing $N = 10$ (or any $N \ge 10$) guarantees $\left|\dfrac{1}{x^2} - 0\right| < 0.01$ whenever $x > N$.
Year Final Exam 2024 • Q3(c) & Q3(d) 3 + 3 = 6 Marks

(c) Find the values of $x$ at which $f(x) = \begin{cases} 3x^2 + 5, & x \ne 1 \\ 6, & x = 1 \end{cases}$ is not continuous, and determine whether it is a removable discontinuity.
(d) Evaluate: (i) $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan(ax)}{\sin(bx)}$ ($a \ne 0, b \ne 0$)     (ii) $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin^2(kx)}{x^2}$ ($k \ne 0$).

Part (c):
For $x \ne 1$, $3x^2 + 5$ is a polynomial (continuous everywhere). At $x = 1$:
• $f(1) = 6$, while $\lim_{x \to 1} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 1} (3x^2 + 5) = 3(1)^2 + 5 = 8$.
• Since $\lim_{x \to 1} f(x) = 8 \ne 6 = f(1)$, $f$ is discontinuous at $x = 1$.
• Because the limit $8$ exists finitely, this is a removable discontinuity (redefining $f(1) = 8$ removes it).

Part (d):
(i) Rewrite $\tan(ax) = \dfrac{\sin(ax)}{\cos(ax)}$: $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan(ax)}{\sin(bx)} = \lim_{x \to 0} \left(\frac{\sin(ax)}{ax} \cdot \frac{bx}{\sin(bx)} \cdot \frac{a}{b\cos(ax)}\right) = 1 \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{a}{b(1)} = \mathbf{\frac{a}{b}}$$ (ii) Multiply numerator and denominator by $k^2$: $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin^2(kx)}{x^2} = k^2 \left(\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(kx)}{kx}\right)^2 = k^2 (1)^2 = \mathbf{k^2}$$
1st In-course Exam 2024 • Q6 3 Marks

Question: Show that $f(x) = |x|$ is continuous everywhere on $\mathbb{R}$.

Proof: Write $f(x) = |x| = \begin{cases} x, & x \ge 0 \\ -x, & x < 0 \end{cases}$.
• For $c > 0$: $f(x) = x$ near $c$ (polynomial → continuous on $(0, \infty)$).
• For $c < 0$: $f(x) = -x$ near $c$ (polynomial → continuous on $(-\infty, 0)$).
• At $c = 0$: $f(0) = 0$; $\text{LHL} = \lim_{x \to 0^-} (-x) = 0$; $\text{RHL} = \lim_{x \to 0^+} x = 0$. Since $\lim_{x \to 0} |x| = 0 = f(0)$, $f$ is continuous at $0$.
Hence $|x|$ is continuous everywhere on $(-\infty, \infty)$. $\blacksquare$
