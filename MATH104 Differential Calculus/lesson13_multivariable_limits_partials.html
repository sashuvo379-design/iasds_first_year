Core Theory (Lecture 13 Slides + Ashraful's Notes)

2.1 Multivariable Limits ($\varepsilon$-$\delta$ vs. Two-Path Test)

In 2D, $(x, y) \to (a, b)$ can happen along infinitely many paths (lines, parabolas, spirals). $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ Definition: $\lim_{(x,y)\to(a,b)} f(x,y) = L$ means for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $\delta > 0$ such that $$0 < \sqrt{(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2} < \delta \implies |f(x, y) - L| < \varepsilon$$

Ashraful's Limit Existence Rule (Two-Path Test): যেকোনো curve দিয়ে $(a, b)$-এর দিকে নিলে limit অবশ্যই একই (same) আসতে হবে! যদি দুটি ভিন্ন path (যেমন $y = 0$ আর $y = x$) বরাবর ভিন্ন ভিন্ন মান আসে, তবে limit exist করে না (DNE)।

2.2 Partial Derivatives & Clairaut's Theorem (Mixed Partials)

To differentiate $z = f(x, y)$ with respect to $x$, hold $y$ constant; to differentiate with respect to $y$, hold $x$ constant: $$f_x(x, y) = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{f(x + \Delta x, y) - f(x, y)}{\Delta x}, \qquad f_y(x, y) = \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = \lim_{\Delta y \to 0} \frac{f(x, y + \Delta y) - f(x, y)}{\Delta y}$$ Clairaut's Theorem (Equality of Mixed Partials): If $f_{xy}$ and $f_{yx}$ are both continuous on an open disk around $(a, b)$, then $f_{xy}(a, b) = f_{yx}(a, b)$ (order of differentiation doesn't matter!).

2.3 Total Differential & Differentiability (Lecture 13 Definitions & Theorem 13.4)

Concept (Lecture 13 Slides) Exact Mathematical Statement
Total Differential ($dz$) For $z = f(x, y)$ with $dx = \Delta x,\; dy = \Delta y$: $$dz = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x}dx + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y}dy = f_x(x, y)\,dx + f_y(x, y)\,dy$$ Extends naturally to 3+ variables: $dw = w_x dx + w_y dy + w_z dz + w_u du$.
Definition of Differentiability $z = f(x, y)$ is differentiable at $(x_0, y_0)$ if $\Delta z$ can be written as: $$\Delta z = f_x(x_0, y_0)\Delta x + f_y(x_0, y_0)\Delta y + \varepsilon_1 \Delta x + \varepsilon_2 \Delta y$$ where both $\varepsilon_1 \to 0$ and $\varepsilon_2 \to 0$ as $(\Delta x, \Delta y) \to (0, 0)$.
Theorem 13.4 (Sufficient Condition) If $f_x$ and $f_y$ are continuous in an open region $R$, then $f$ is differentiable on $R$.
Ashraful's Intuition for $dz$: $f_x$ দেয় $y$-কে stop করে $x$-এর change; $f_y$ দেয় $x$-কে stop করে $y$-এর change। কিন্তু reality-তে $x$ ও $y$ একসাথে change হয় — তাই total change-এর linear approximation হলো $dz = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x}dx + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y}dy$!

Guided Worked Examples (Lecture 13 & Class Notes)

Ashraful's Notes • 2D $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ Proof

Example 1: Prove $\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)} \frac{5x^2 y}{x^2 + y^2} = 0$

Use the 2D $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ definition to prove the limit equals $0$.

Step 1 — Write $|f(x, y) - 0|$: $\left|\dfrac{5x^2 y}{x^2 + y^2} - 0\right| = \dfrac{5x^2 |y|}{x^2 + y^2}$.
Step 2 — Use Key Inequality $\frac{x^2}{x^2+y^2} \le 1$: Since $0 \le x^2 \le x^2 + y^2$, we have $\dfrac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2} \le 1$, so $\dfrac{5x^2 |y|}{x^2 + y^2} \le 5|y|$.
Step 3 — Bound $|y|$ by Distance $\delta$: Since $|y| = \sqrt{y^2} \le \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} < \delta$, we get $|f(x, y) - 0| \le 5\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} < 5\delta$.
Step 4 — Choose $\delta = \frac{\varepsilon}{5}$: Whenever $0 < \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} < \delta = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{5}$, we have $|f(x, y) - 0| < 5\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon}{5}\right) = \varepsilon$. $\blacksquare$
Ashraful's Notes • Higher Mixed Partials

Example 2: Show $f_{xzz} = f_{zxz} = f_{zzx}$ for $f(x, y, z) = y e^x + x \ln z$

Verify mixed partial equality up to third order for $f(x, y, z) = y e^x + x \ln z$.

Step 1 — First Partials: $f_x = y e^x + \ln z$   and   $f_z = \dfrac{x}{z}$.
Step 2 — Second Partials: $f_{xz} = \dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}(y e^x + \ln z) = \dfrac{1}{z}$;   $f_{zx} = \dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left(\dfrac{x}{z}\right) = \dfrac{1}{z}$;   $f_{zz} = \dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}\!\left(\dfrac{x}{z}\right) = -\dfrac{x}{z^2}$.
Step 3 — Third Partials: $f_{xzz} = \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(\frac{1}{z}) = -\frac{1}{z^2}$,   $f_{zxz} = \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(\frac{1}{z}) = -\frac{1}{z^2}$,   $f_{zzx} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(-\frac{x}{z^2}) = -\frac{1}{z^2}$. All three equal $-\dfrac{1}{z^2}$! $\blacksquare$
Lecture 13 Slide • Definition of Differentiability

Example 3: Prove $f(x, y) = x^2 + 3y$ is Differentiable Everywhere

Use the definition of differentiability ($\Delta z = f_x \Delta x + f_y \Delta y + \varepsilon_1\Delta x + \varepsilon_2\Delta y$) directly.

Step 1 — Expand $\Delta z$: $\Delta z = f(x + \Delta x, y + \Delta y) - f(x, y) = (x^2 + 2x\Delta x + \Delta x^2) + 3(y + \Delta y) - (x^2 + 3y) = 2x\Delta x + 3\Delta y + (\Delta x)\Delta x + 0(\Delta y)$.
Step 2 — Match with Definition: Since $f_x(x, y) = 2x$ and $f_y(x, y) = 3$, we identify $\varepsilon_1 = \Delta x$ and $\varepsilon_2 = 0$.
Step 3 — Check Limits: As $(\Delta x, \Delta y) \to (0, 0)$, both $\varepsilon_1 = \Delta x \to 0$ and $\varepsilon_2 = 0 \to 0$. Hence $f$ is differentiable everywhere in $\mathbb{R}^2$. $\blacksquare$

Year Final Exam 2025 Q6(c) & Lecture 13 Slide 4 Marks (Exact Slide Repeat!)

Question: Use the differential $dz$ to approximate the change in $z = \sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2}$ as $(x, y)$ moves from $(1, 1)$ to $(1.01, 0.97)$. Compare this approximation with the exact change $\Delta z$.

Hint: $dx = \Delta x = 1.01 - 1 = +0.01$ and $dy = \Delta y = 0.97 - 1 = -0.03$. Compute $z_x$ and $z_y$ at $(1, 1)$ where $\sqrt{4 - 1 - 1} = \sqrt{2}$!
Full Solution:
1. Compute partial derivatives: $$\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = \frac{-x}{\sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2}}, \qquad \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} = \frac{-y}{\sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2}}$$ 2. At $(1, 1)$ with $dx = 0.01$ and $dy = -0.03$: $$dz = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0.01) - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(-0.03) = \frac{0.02}{\sqrt{2}} = 0.01\sqrt{2} \approx \mathbf{0.01414}$$ 3. Exact change $\Delta z$: $$\Delta z = \sqrt{4 - (1.01)^2 - (0.97)^2} - \sqrt{4 - 1^2 - 1^2} = \sqrt{2.0390} - \sqrt{2} \approx 1.42794 - 1.41421 \approx \mathbf{0.01372}$$ The differential $dz \approx 0.0141$ closely approximates the exact change $\Delta z \approx 0.0137$ (difference $\approx 0.0004$)!
Lecture 13 Slide • Error Propagation 4 Marks

Question: The possible error in measuring each dimension of a rectangular box is $\pm 0.1\text{ mm}$. The dimensions are $x = 50\text{ cm}$, $y = 20\text{ cm}$, and $z = 15\text{ cm}$. Use $dV$ to estimate the propagated error and relative error in the volume $V = xyz$.

Full Solution:
Convert units first: $0.1\text{ mm} = 0.01\text{ cm}$, so $dx = dy = dz = \pm 0.01\text{ cm}$.
Total differential of $V = xyz$: $$dV = yz\,dx + xz\,dy + xy\,dz$$ Substitute $x = 50, y = 20, z = 15$: $$dV = (20)(15)(\pm 0.01) + (50)(15)(\pm 0.01) + (50)(20)(\pm 0.01) = (300 + 750 + 1000)(\pm 0.01) = \mathbf{\pm 20.5\text{ cm}^3}$$ Exact volume is $V = 50 \times 20 \times 15 = 15{,}000\text{ cm}^3$, so the relative error is $\dfrac{dV}{V} = \dfrac{\pm 20.5}{15{,}000} \approx \mathbf{\pm 0.00137}$ (or $\pm 0.14\%$).
Ashraful's Notes • Two-Path Limit DNE 3 Marks

Question: Show that $\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)} \left(\frac{x^2 - y^2}{x^2 + y^2}\right)^2$ does not exist.

Proof:
• Path 1 (Along the $x$-axis, $y = 0$, $x \ne 0$): $$\lim_{x \to 0} \left(\frac{x^2 - 0}{x^2 + 0}\right)^2 = \lim_{x \to 0} (1)^2 = \mathbf{1}$$ • Path 2 (Along the line $y = x$, $x \ne 0$): $$\lim_{x \to 0} \left(\frac{x^2 - x^2}{x^2 + x^2}\right)^2 = \lim_{x \to 0} \left(\frac{0}{2x^2}\right)^2 = \mathbf{0}$$ Since two different paths toward $(0, 0)$ yield different limits ($1 \ne 0$), the limit does not exist. $\blacksquare$
