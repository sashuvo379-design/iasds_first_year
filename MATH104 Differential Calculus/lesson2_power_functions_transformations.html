Core Theory (Lecture 2 Slides + Ashraful's Notes)

2.1 Power Functions & Fractional Exponents

Understanding the shapes of parent power functions $y = x^p$ lets you sketch complicated curves instantly:

  • Even integer powers ($y = x^2, x^4, x^6, \dots$): U-shaped, symmetric about the $y$-axis, pass through $(-1,1), (0,0), (1,1)$.
  • Odd integer powers ($y = x^3, x^5, x^7, \dots$): S-shaped, symmetric about the origin, pass through $(-1,-1), (0,0), (1,1)$.
  • Reciprocal powers ($y = 1/x^n$): Vertical asymptote at $x = 0$, horizontal asymptote at $y = 0$. Odd $n$ lives in Quadrants I & III; even $n$ lives in Quadrants I & II ($y > 0$ always).
  • Fractional powers: $y = \sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$ starts at $(0,0)$ in Quadrant I; $y = x^{3/2} = (\sqrt{x})^3$ curves upward steeper than a line; $y = x^{2/3} = (x^{1/3})^2$ is defined for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ and forms a sharp cusp at $(0,0)$.
Ashraful's Class Note Shortcut (Power Compression): $y = x^n$ এর ক্ষেত্রে (even হোক বা odd), power বাড়লে গ্রাফটি $y$-axis এর দিকে compress হয় (for $|x| > 1$, higher powers shoot up faster, while for $|x| < 1$ they flatten closer to the $x$-axis).

2.2 Complete Summary of Graphing Techniques (Transformations)

Transformation Type Equation Geometric Effect on Graph of $y = f(x)$ Coordinate Change
Vertical Shift $y = f(x) + k$ ($k>0$)
$y = f(x) - k$ ($k>0$)		 Shift up $k$ units
Shift down $k$ units		 $(x, y) \to (x, y \pm k)$
Horizontal Shift $y = f(x + h)$ ($h>0$)
$y = f(x - h)$ ($h>0$)		 Shift left $h$ units
Shift right $h$ units		 $(x, y) \to (x \mp h, y)$
Vertical Stretch / Compress $y = a f(x)$ ($a > 0$) Stretch vertically if $a > 1$
Compress vertically if $0 < a < 1$		 $(x, y) \to (x, ay)$
Horizontal Stretch / Compress $y = f(ax)$ ($a > 0$) Compress horizontally if $a > 1$
Stretch horizontally if $0 < a < 1$		 $(x, y) \to \left(\dfrac{x}{a}, y\right)$
Reflections $y = -f(x)$
$y = f(-x)$		 Reflect about the $x$-axis
Reflect about the $y$-axis		 $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$
$(x, y) \to (-x, y)$
The #1 Transformation Trap — Always Factor Inside $f(\dots)$! Horizontal shifts require the form $x - h$. For $y = \sqrt{1 - x} + 2$, do not shift left 1! First factor out the negative sign: $$\sqrt{1 - x} + 2 = \sqrt{-(x - 1)} + 2$$ Now you see the true sequence: start with $\sqrt{x}$, reflect about the $y$-axis ($\sqrt{-x}$), shift right 1 unit ($\sqrt{-(x-1)}$), then shift up 2 units!

2.3 Sinusoidal Family: $y = A\sin(Bx - C)$ and $y = A\cos(Bx - C)$

Rewrite the inside by factoring out $B$: $$y = A\sin\left[B\left(x - \frac{C}{B}\right)\right] \qquad \text{and} \qquad y = A\cos\left[B\left(x - \frac{C}{B}\right)\right]$$

Property Formula Meaning
Amplitude $|A|$ Half the distance between max and min height. (If $A < 0$, graph is reflected across the $x$-axis.)
Period $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{|B|}$ Length of one full oscillation cycle along the $x$-axis.
Frequency $f = \dfrac{|B|}{2\pi}$ Reciprocal of the period (cycles per unit length).
Phase Shift (Horizontal Translation) $\dfrac{C}{B}$ Shift right if $C/B > 0$; shift left if $C/B < 0$.

Guided Worked Examples (Lecture 2 Slides)

Lecture 2 • Example 11

Series of Transformations on $y = |x|$

Find the final function obtained after applying these three transformations in order to $y = |x|$: (1) Shift left 2 units, (2) Shift up 3 units, (3) Reflect about the $y$-axis.

Step 1 — Shift left 2 units: Replace $x$ by $x + 2 \implies y = |x + 2|$.
Step 2 — Shift up 3 units: Add 3 to the function $\implies y = |x + 2| + 3$.
Step 3 — Reflect about the $y$-axis: Replace $x$ by $-x \implies y = |-x + 2| + 3$. (Notice this equals $|x - 2| + 3$, whose vertex moved from $(-2, 3)$ to $(2, 3)$!)
Lecture 2 • Example 12

Graphing $f(x) = \dfrac{3}{x - 2} + 1$ & Finding Domain/Range

Rewrite $f(x) = 3\left(\dfrac{1}{x - 2}\right) + 1$ and trace each step from the reciprocal parent function $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$.

Step 1 — Parent Curve: Start with $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ (key points $(1,1), (-1,-1)$; asymptotes $x=0, y=0$).
Step 2 — Vertical Stretch by 3: $y = \dfrac{3}{x}$ stretches points to $(1,3)$ and $(-1,-3)$.
Step 3 — Shift Right 2 Units: Replace $x$ by $x - 2 \implies y = \dfrac{3}{x - 2}$. Vertical asymptote moves to $x = 2$; points move to $(3,3)$ and $(1,-3)$.
Step 4 — Shift Up 1 Unit & Domain/Range: Add 1 $\implies y = \dfrac{3}{x - 2} + 1$. Horizontal asymptote moves to $y = 1$; points move to $(3,4)$ and $(1,-2)$. Thus $\text{Domain} = \mathbb{R} - \{2\}$ and $\text{Range} = \mathbb{R} - \{1\}$.
Lecture 2 • Example 13

Graphing $f(x) = \sqrt{1 - x} + 2$ (Factoring Inside!)

Sketch $f(x) = \sqrt{1 - x} + 2$ using transformations and determine its domain and range.

Step 1 — Factor Inside: Rewrite $f(x) = \sqrt{-(x - 1)} + 2$ so the horizontal shift is clearly visible.
Step 2 — Start & Reflect about $y$-axis: Start with $y = \sqrt{x}$ (points $(0,0), (1,1), (4,2)$). Replace $x$ by $-x$ to get $y = \sqrt{-x}$ (points $(0,0), (-1,1), (-4,2)$).
Step 3 — Shift Right 1 Unit: Replace $x$ by $x - 1$ to get $y = \sqrt{-(x - 1)} = \sqrt{1 - x}$. Points move to $(1,0), (0,1), (-3,2)$.
Step 4 — Shift Up 2 Units: Add 2 to get $y = \sqrt{1 - x} + 2$. Key points are $(1,2), (0,3), (-3,4)$. From the graph: $\text{Domain} = (-\infty, 1]$ and $\text{Range} = [2, \infty)$.

1st In-course Exam 2025 • Q2(c) (Also Lecture 2 Example 3) 3 Marks

Question: Sketch the graph of the function $y = 3\cos\left(2x + \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$. Find the amplitude and period of $y$, and state how $y = 3\cos 2x$ is translated to obtain it.

Hint: Factor $2$ out of $\left(2x + \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$ so it matches $A\cos\left[B\left(x - \frac{C}{B}\right)\right]$. Be careful: dividing $\frac{\pi}{2}$ by $2$ gives $\frac{\pi}{4}$, not $\frac{\pi}{2}$!
Full Solution:
Rewrite the equation in standard factored form: $$y = 3\cos\left[2x - \left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\right] = 3\cos\left[2\left(x - \left(-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\right)\right]$$ Comparing with $y = A\cos\left[B\left(x - \frac{C}{B}\right)\right]$:
  • Amplitude: $|A| = 3$ (oscillates between $-3$ and $+3$).
  • Period: $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{|B|} = \dfrac{2\pi}{2} = \pi$.
  • Translation (Phase Shift): $\dfrac{C}{B} = -\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, so translate the graph of $y = 3\cos 2x$ left by $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ units.
  • Quick Sketch Check: At $x = -\frac{\pi}{4}$, $y = 3\cos(0) = 3$ (peak); at $x = 0$, $y = 3\cos(\pi/2) = 0$; at $x = \frac{\pi}{4}$, $y = 3\cos(\pi) = -3$ (trough). (Try the preset in the Wave Lab above!)
Lecture 2 Slides • Exercise 11 Power & Root Shape Matching

Question: Identify the key visual signature (domain, symmetry, and behavior) for each of these 6 equations:
(a) $y = \sqrt[5]{x}$    (b) $y = 2x^5$    (c) $y = -1/x^8$    (d) $y = \sqrt{x^2 - 1}$    (e) $y = \sqrt[4]{x - 2}$    (f) $y = -\sqrt[5]{x^2}$

Complete Breakdown (Matches Graphs I–VI in Lecture 2):
  • (a) $y = \sqrt[5]{x}$ (Graph VI): Odd root → domain $\mathbb{R}$, origin symmetry, vertical tangent at $(0,0)$, grows slowly.
  • (b) $y = 2x^5$ (Graph IV): Odd power → domain $\mathbb{R}$, origin symmetry, flat horizontal tangent at $(0,0)$, shoots up/down rapidly.
  • (c) $y = -1/x^8$ (Graph III): Negative even reciprocal → vertical asymptote at $x=0$, both branches plunge to $-\infty$ in Quadrants III & IV.
  • (d) $y = \sqrt{x^2 - 1}$ (Graph V): Requires $x^2 - 1 \ge 0 \implies |x| \ge 1$. Two symmetric V-like arms starting at $(-1,0)$ and $(1,0)$ going upward.
  • (e) $y = \sqrt[4]{x - 2}$ (Graph I): Even root shifted right 2 → domain $[2, \infty)$, single curve starting at $(2,0)$ in Quadrant I.
  • (f) $y = -\sqrt[5]{x^2} = -x^{2/5}$ (Graph II): Even numerator → $y$-axis symmetry, negative sign puts it below the $x$-axis with a sharp downward cusp at $(0,0)$.
Lecture 2 Slides • Exercise 31 Reverse-Engineering Trig Equations

Question: Find an equation of the form $y = A\sin Bx$ or $y = A\cos Bx$ for each wave:
(a) Starts at $(0,0)$, rises to max $3$, completes one full cycle at $x = 4\pi$.
(b) Starts at peak $(0,4)$, drops to $-4$, completes one full cycle at $x = \pi$.
(c) Starts at $(0,0)$, drops first to min $-5$, completes one full cycle at $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$.

Full Solution:
Use $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{B} \implies B = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$:
  • (a): Starts at $0$ going up → positive sine. $A = 3$, $T = 4\pi \implies B = \frac{2\pi}{4\pi} = \frac{1}{2}$. Equation: $y = 3\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}x\right)$.
  • (b): Starts at peak $+4$ → positive cosine. $A = 4$, $T = \pi \implies B = \frac{2\pi}{\pi} = 2$. Equation: $y = 4\cos(2x)$.
  • (c): Starts at $0$ going down → negative sine. $A = -5$, $T = \frac{\pi}{2} \implies B = \frac{2\pi}{\pi/2} = 4$. Equation: $y = -5\sin(4x)$.
