Section 01

The Power Mean Chain ($QM \ge AM \ge GM \ge HM$)

For any $n$ strictly positive real numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n \in \mathbb{R}^+$, we define four classical averages that always satisfy the fundamental chain: $$\text{QM} \ge \text{AM} \ge \text{GM} \ge \text{HM}$$

QUADRATIC MEAN (RMS)
$$QM = \sqrt{\frac{a_1^2 + \dots + a_n^2}{n}}$$

Largest of the four; power $p = 2$.

ARITHMETIC MEAN
$$AM = \frac{a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n}{n}$$

Standard average; power $p = 1$.

GEOMETRIC MEAN
$$GM = \left(a_1 a_2 \cdots a_n\right)^{1/n}$$

Multiplicative average; limit $p \to 0$.

HARMONIC MEAN
$$HM = \frac{n}{\frac{1}{a_1} + \frac{1}{a_2} + \dots + \frac{1}{a_n}}$$

Reciprocal average; power $p = -1$.

🎯

Equality Condition (Mandatory for Full ISRT Marks!)

In every link of $QM \ge AM \ge GM \ge HM$, equality holds if and only if $a_1 = a_2 = \dots = a_n$. Notice how ISRT exams (e.g., 1st Incourse 2019 & 2020, Finals 2019 & 2024) almost always append "When does equality occur?"—and when the problem states the numbers are unequal, the inequality becomes strict ($>$)!

Part A · Exact Proof for $n = 2$ ($a, b > 0$):

1. $QM \ge AM$: $QM^2 - AM^2 = \frac{a^2+b^2}{2} - \left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{2a^2+2b^2 - (a^2+2ab+b^2)}{4} = \frac{(a-b)^2}{4} \ge 0$. Since both are positive, $QM \ge AM$ (equality iff $a = b$).

2. $AM \ge GM$: $AM - GM = \frac{a+b}{2} - \sqrt{ab} = \frac{(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})^2}{2} \ge 0 \implies AM \ge GM$ (equality iff $a = b$).

3. $GM \ge HM$: Applying $AM \ge GM$ to $\frac{1}{a}, \frac{1}{b}$ gives $\frac{1/a + 1/b}{2} \ge \frac{1}{\sqrt{ab}}$. Taking reciprocals flips the sign: $\sqrt{ab} \ge \frac{2}{1/a + 1/b} = HM$ (equality iff $a = b$). $\blacksquare$

Part B · General $n$ (Cauchy's Forward–Backward Induction for AM–GM):

Forward Step (Powers of 2): Base case $n = 2$ holds. If true for $n = 2^k$, split $2^{k+1}$ terms into two halves of size $2^k$, apply the hypothesis to each half, then apply $n = 2$ to the two halves to prove it for $2^{k+1}$.

Backward Step ($n \to n - 1$): Given $n - 1$ positive numbers $a_1, \dots, a_{n-1}$, set $a_n = A = \frac{a_1+\dots+a_{n-1}}{n-1}$. Applying the $n$-variable AM–GM gives $A \ge (a_1\cdots a_{n-1} \cdot A)^{1/n} \implies A^n \ge a_1\cdots a_{n-1} \cdot A \implies A^{n-1} \ge a_1\cdots a_{n-1}$. Thus AM–GM holds for all $n \ge 1$!

Completing the Chain: Apply AM–GM to reciprocals $1/a_i$ to get $GM \ge HM$, and apply Cauchy–Schwarz with $b_i = 1$ to get $n\sum a_i^2 \ge (\sum a_i)^2 \implies QM \ge AM$. $\blacksquare$

Section 02

Cauchy–Schwarz, Chebyshev & Weierstrass Inequalities

Theorem 2.1 · Cauchy's (Cauchy–Schwarz) Inequality ISRT Final 2020, 2023, 2024

For any real numbers $a_1, \dots, a_n$ and $b_1, \dots, b_n$: $$\left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i^2\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^n b_i^2\right) \ge \left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i b_i\right)^2$$ Equality holds if and only if the vectors $(a_1, \dots, a_n)$ and $(b_1, \dots, b_n)$ are proportional (i.e., $a_i = k b_i$ for a fixed constant $k$, or one vector is zero).

Method 1 (Non-Negative Quadratic): Consider the function $f(t) = \sum_{i=1}^n (a_i t + b_i)^2 \ge 0$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$. Expanding gives a quadratic in $t$: $$f(t) = \left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i^2\right) t^2 + 2\left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i b_i\right) t + \left(\sum_{i=1}^n b_i^2\right) \ge 0$$ Because a non-negative quadratic cannot have two distinct real roots, its discriminant $\Delta \le 0$: $$4\left(\sum a_i b_i\right)^2 - 4\left(\sum a_i^2\right)\left(\sum b_i^2\right) \le 0 \implies \left(\sum a_i^2\right)\left(\sum b_i^2\right) \ge \left(\sum a_i b_i\right)^2 \quad \blacksquare$$

Method 2 (Lagrange's Identity): Directly expanding verifies the exact deficit formula: $$\left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i^2\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^n b_i^2\right) - \left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i b_i\right)^2 = \sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} (a_i b_j - a_j b_i)^2 \ge 0$$ Equality holds iff $a_i b_j = a_j b_i$ for every pair $(i, j)$, i.e., $\frac{a_1}{b_1} = \frac{a_2}{b_2} = \dots = \frac{a_n}{b_n}$. $\blacksquare$

Theorem 2.2 · Chebyshev's Sum Inequality ISRT Year Final 2017 Q1b

If $x_1 \ge x_2 \ge \dots \ge x_n$ and $y_1 \ge y_2 \ge \dots \ge y_n$ (similarly ordered), then: $$n\left(x_1 y_1 + x_2 y_2 + \dots + x_n y_n\right) \ge (x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n)(y_1 + y_2 + \dots + y_n)$$ If the sequences are oppositely ordered ($x_1 \ge \dots \ge x_n$ and $y_1 \le \dots \le y_n$), the inequality reverses ($\le$).

Since $x$ and $y$ are similarly ordered, $(x_i - x_j)$ and $(y_i - y_j)$ always have the same sign (or one is zero), so $(x_i - x_j)(y_i - y_j) \ge 0$ for all $1 \le i, j \le n$.

Summing over all $n^2$ pairs $(i, j)$:

$$0 \le \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n (x_i - x_j)(y_i - y_j) = \sum_{i,j} x_i y_i - \sum_{i,j} x_i y_j - \sum_{i,j} x_j y_i + \sum_{i,j} x_j y_j$$

Note that $\sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n x_i y_i = n \sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i$ and $\sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n x_i y_j = \left(\sum x_i\right)\left(\sum y_j\right)$. Therefore:

$$2n\sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i - 2\left(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^n y_i\right) \ge 0 \implies n\sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i \ge \left(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^n y_i\right) \quad \blacksquare$$
Theorem 2.3 · Weierstrass Product Inequality ISRT Year Final 2023 Q1b

For any real numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n \in (0, 1)$: $$1 - \sum_{i=1}^n a_i \le \prod_{i=1}^n (1 - a_i) \qquad \text{and} \qquad 1 + \sum_{i=1}^n a_i \le \prod_{i=1}^n (1 + a_i)$$

Proof of $1 - \sum_{i=1}^n a_i \le \prod_{i=1}^n (1 - a_i)$ by Mathematical Induction:

Base Case ($n = 1$): $1 - a_1 \le 1 - a_1$ holds with equality.

Inductive Step: Assume true for $n = k$: $\prod_{i=1}^k (1 - a_i) \ge 1 - \sum_{i=1}^k a_i$. Since $a_{k+1} \in (0, 1)$, we have $1 - a_{k+1} > 0$. Multiplying both sides by $(1 - a_{k+1})$:

$$\prod_{i=1}^{k+1} (1 - a_i) \ge \left(1 - \sum_{i=1}^k a_i\right)(1 - a_{k+1}) = 1 - \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} a_i + \underbrace{a_{k+1}\sum_{i=1}^k a_i}_{> 0} \ge 1 - \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} a_i$$

The proof for $1 + \sum a_i \le \prod (1 + a_i)$ is identical since $a_{k+1} \sum_{i=1}^k a_i > 0$. $\blacksquare$

Section 03

Signature ISRT Inequality Proof Patterns

Almost every inequality proof on ISRT MATH 103 / AST 104 exams falls into one of three recurring strategic patterns. Recognize the structure immediately to write a 4-line proof:

Pattern 1

Cyclic AM–GM Pairing

Apply $u + v \ge 2\sqrt{uv}$ to each cyclic factor and multiply them together:

$$(b+c)(c+a)(a+b) \ge (2\sqrt{bc})(2\sqrt{ca})(2\sqrt{ab}) = 8abc$$

Also solves $\left(a+\frac{1}{b}\right)\left(b+\frac{1}{c}\right)\left(c+\frac{1}{a}\right) \ge 8$ (ISRT 2019 Q6)!

Pattern 2

Ravi / Semi-Perimeter Substitution

When terms like $(s-a), (s-b), (s-c)$ or $(y+z-x)$ appear, substitute $u = s-a, v = s-b, w = s-c$ (where $2s = a+b+c$):

$$u + v + w = s \implies \left(\frac{s}{3}\right)^3 > uvw \implies s^3 > 27(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)$$

Turns ISRT 2019, 2021 & 2024's $xyz > (y+z-x)(z+x-y)(x+y-z)$ into a one-liner!

Pattern 3

AM–HM / Titu's Lemma (Cauchy–Engel)

For sums of fractions with positive denominators $d_i > 0$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives Titu's Lemma:

$$\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{x_i^2}{d_i} \ge \frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\right)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^n d_i}$$

Setting $x_i = 1, d_i = s - a_i$ immediately proves $\sum \frac{s}{s-a_i} > \frac{n^2}{n-1}$!

Method of Finite Differences & Newton's Forward Formula

ISRT Final 2020 Q4a(i)

If the $d$-th order finite differences $\Delta^d u_r$ of a sequence $u_1, u_2, u_3, \dots$ are constant, then $u_n$ is a polynomial of degree $d$, given directly by Newton's forward difference formula: $$u_n = u_1 + \binom{n-1}{1}\Delta^1 u_1 + \binom{n-1}{2}\Delta^2 u_1 + \binom{n-1}{3}\Delta^3 u_1 + \dots$$

Telescoping Series ($V_r - V_{r-1}$ Method)

The Core Telescoping Principle

If the $r$-th term $u_r$ of a series can be expressed as a difference $u_r = V_r - V_{r-1}$, then all interior terms cancel pairwise in the sum: $$S_n = \sum_{r=1}^n u_r = (V_1 - V_0) + (V_2 - V_1) + (V_3 - V_2) + \dots + (V_n - V_{n-1}) = V_n - V_0$$

Class Note Derivation

Sum of $1\cdot 2 + 2\cdot 3 + 3\cdot 4 + \dots + n(n+1)$

Here $u_r = r(r+1)$. Multiply and divide by the gap between one factor ahead $(r+2)$ and one factor behind $(r-1)$, which is $(r+2) - (r-1) = 3$: $$u_r = \frac{1}{3} r(r+1)\big[(r+2) - (r-1)\big] = \underbrace{\frac{1}{3}r(r+1)(r+2)}_{V_r} - \underbrace{\frac{1}{3}(r-1)r(r+1)}_{V_{r-1}}$$ Since $V_0 = \frac{1}{3}(0)(1)(2) = 0$, telescoping immediately yields: $$S_n = V_n - V_0 = \frac{1}{3}n(n+1)(n+2)$$

Rule 5.1 · Products of $m$ Consecutive AP Factors

If $u_r = a_r a_{r+1} \cdots a_{r+m-1}$ where $a_k$ is an AP with common difference $d$: $$S_n = \frac{a_n a_{n+1}\cdots a_{n+m} - a_0 a_1 \cdots a_m}{(m+1)d}$$

Memory aid: "Append one factor at the end, divide by (new count × step $d$), subtract $V_0$."

Rule 5.2 · Reciprocal AP Factors

If $u_r = \dfrac{1}{a_r a_{r+1} \cdots a_{r+m-1}}$ ($m \ge 2$, AP step $d$): $$S_n = \frac{1}{(m-1)d}\left[\frac{1}{a_1\cdots a_{m-1}} - \frac{1}{a_{n+1}\cdots a_{n+m-1}}\right]$$

Example: $\sum_{r=1}^n \frac{1}{r(r+1)} = \frac{1}{1}\left(\frac{1}{1} - \frac{1}{n+1}\right) = \frac{n}{n+1}$.

Section 06

Trigonometric & Inverse-Tangent Telescoping Series

1. Inverse-Tangent ($\tan^{-1}$) Telescoping

ISRT Final 2024 & 2nd Incourse 2019

Rewrite the fraction inside $\tan^{-1}(\cdot)$ into the difference formula ($AB > -1$): $$\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{A - B}{1 + AB}\right) = \tan^{-1} A - \tan^{-1} B$$

ISRT 2024 Pattern: For $u_r = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{2x}{1 + (2r-1)(2r+1)x^2}\right)$, set $A = (2r+1)x$ and $B = (2r-1)x$.

Then $A - B = 2x$ and $1 + AB = 1 + (2r-1)(2r+1)x^2$, so $u_r = \tan^{-1}((2r+1)x) - \tan^{-1}((2r-1)x)$.

Telescoping sum: $S_n = \tan^{-1}((2n+1)x) - \tan^{-1}(x) = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{2nx}{1 + (2n+1)x^2}\right)$.

2. Sine & Cosine Series with Angles in AP

To sum $\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \sin(\alpha + k\beta)$ or $\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \cos(\alpha + k\beta)$ ($\beta \neq 2m\pi$), multiply every term by the telescoping factor $2\sin\!\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right)$ and apply product-to-sum identities:

SINE AP SUM
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \sin(\alpha + k\beta) = \frac{\sin\!\left(\alpha + \frac{n-1}{2}\beta\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\beta}{2}\right)}{\sin(\beta/2)}$$
COSINE AP SUM
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \cos(\alpha + k\beta) = \frac{\cos\!\left(\alpha + \frac{n-1}{2}\beta\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\beta}{2}\right)}{\sin(\beta/2)}$$

ISRT Final 2019 & 2024 [3 Marks]

1. Let $x, y, z > 0$ with $x + y + z = 1$. Prove that $\dfrac{xy}{z} + \dfrac{yz}{x} + \dfrac{zx}{y} \ge 1$. When does equality occur?

Hint: Apply AM–GM to pairs of the three terms: $\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{xy}{z} + \frac{yz}{x}\right) \ge \sqrt{\frac{xy}{z}\cdot\frac{yz}{x}} = y$. Repeat cyclically and add!

Since $x, y, z > 0$, each term $\frac{xy}{z}, \frac{yz}{x}, \frac{zx}{y}$ is positive. Applying $AM \ge GM$ to each pair:

$$\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{xy}{z} + \frac{yz}{x}\right) \ge \sqrt{\frac{xy}{z}\cdot\frac{yz}{x}} = y, \qquad \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{yz}{x} + \frac{zx}{y}\right) \ge z, \qquad \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{zx}{y} + \frac{xy}{z}\right) \ge x$$

Adding all three inequalities gives: $\dfrac{xy}{z} + \dfrac{yz}{x} + \dfrac{zx}{y} \ge x + y + z = 1$.

Equality Condition: Holds iff $\frac{xy}{z} = \frac{yz}{x} = \frac{zx}{y} \iff x = y = z$. Combined with $x+y+z=1$, equality occurs iff $x = y = z = \frac{1}{3}$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT Final 2019, 2024 & 2nd Incourse 2021 [3 Marks]

2. If $a, b, c$ are unequal positive integers, prove that $(b+c)(c+a)(a+b) > 8abc$ and hence prove $xyz > (y+z-x)(z+x-y)(x+y-z)$.

Hint: For the first part, multiply $b+c \ge 2\sqrt{bc}$ cyclically (strict since unequal). For the second part, set $a = y+z-x, b = z+x-y, c = x+y-z$ and observe $b+c = 2x$.

Part 1: By AM–GM, $b+c \ge 2\sqrt{bc}$, $c+a \ge 2\sqrt{ca}$, $a+b \ge 2\sqrt{ab}$. Since $a, b, c$ are not all equal, at least one inequality is strict ($>$). Multiplying gives $(b+c)(c+a)(a+b) > 8\sqrt{a^2 b^2 c^2} = 8abc$.

Part 2: Let $a = y+z-x$, $b = z+x-y$, $c = x+y-z$. Notice $b+c = 2x$, $c+a = 2y$, $a+b = 2z$.

Case 1: If $a, b, c$ are all positive (and unequal since $x, y, z$ are unequal), substituting into Part 1 yields $(2x)(2y)(2z) > 8abc \implies 8xyz > 8abc \implies xyz > (y+z-x)(z+x-y)(x+y-z)$.

Case 2: If any factor (say $a = y+z-x$) is $\le 0$, then $a + b = 2z > 0 \implies b > 0$ and $a + c = 2y > 0 \implies c > 0$. Hence at most one factor is $\le 0$, so the RHS $abc \le 0 < xyz$ holds trivially! $\blacksquare$

ISRT Final 2020 & 2nd Incourse 2021 [6 Marks]

3. If $s = a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n$ ($a_i > 0$, not all equal), show that $\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{s}{s - a_i} > \frac{n^2}{n - 1}$, and deduce that for $a,b,c,d > 0$: $\dfrac{3}{b+c+d} + \dfrac{3}{c+d+a} + \dfrac{3}{d+a+b} + \dfrac{3}{a+b+c} \ge \dfrac{16}{a+b+c+d}$.

Hint: Apply $AM > HM$ (or Cauchy–Schwarz) to the $n$ positive numbers $(s - a_1), (s - a_2), \dots, (s - a_n)$, whose sum is $(n-1)s$. Then set $n = 4$!

Main Proof: Let $b_i = s - a_i > 0$ for $i = 1, \dots, n$. Since $a_i$ are not all equal, $b_i$ are not all equal. Note $\sum_{i=1}^n b_i = ns - \sum_{i=1}^n a_i = (n-1)s$.

By $AM > HM$ on $b_1, \dots, b_n$ (equivalently Cauchy–Schwarz with $\sqrt{b_i}$ and $1/\sqrt{b_i}$):

$$\left(\sum_{i=1}^n (s - a_i)\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{1}{s - a_i}\right) > n^2 \implies (n-1)s \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{1}{s - a_i} > n^2 \implies \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{s}{s - a_i} > \frac{n^2}{n - 1}$$

Deduction for $n = 4$ ($a, b, c, d > 0$): Let $s = a + b + c + d$. Allowing equality (when $a=b=c=d$), the result for $n = 4$ gives: $$\frac{s}{b+c+d} + \frac{s}{c+d+a} + \frac{s}{d+a+b} + \frac{s}{a+b+c} \ge \frac{4^2}{4 - 1} = \frac{16}{3}$$ Multiplying both sides by $\frac{3}{s}$ yields $\dfrac{3}{b+c+d} + \dfrac{3}{c+d+a} + \dfrac{3}{d+a+b} + \dfrac{3}{a+b+c} \ge \dfrac{16}{a+b+c+d}$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT Final 2017 · Q1b [4 Marks]

4. Prove Chebyshev's Inequality: if $x_1 \ge x_2 \ge \dots \ge x_n$ and $y_1 \ge y_2 \ge \dots \ge y_n$, then $n(x_1 y_1 + \dots + x_n y_n) \ge (x_1 + \dots + x_n)(y_1 + \dots + y_n)$.

Hint: Sum the non-negative expression $(x_i - x_j)(y_i - y_j) \ge 0$ over all $i = 1, \dots, n$ and $j = 1, \dots, n$.

For every pair $(i, j)$, if $i \le j$ then $x_i - x_j \ge 0$ and $y_i - y_j \ge 0$; if $i > j$ then both are $\le 0$. In all cases, $(x_i - x_j)(y_i - y_j) \ge 0$.

Summing over all $1 \le i, j \le n$:

$$\sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n (x_i y_i - x_i y_j - x_j y_i + x_j y_j) \ge 0 \implies n\sum x_i y_i - (\sum x_i)(\sum y_j) - (\sum x_j)(\sum y_i) + n\sum x_j y_j \ge 0$$

Dividing by $2$ gives $n\sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i \ge \left(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^n y_i\right)$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT 2nd Incourse 2022 & Final 2020 [4 + 4 Marks]

5. Find the sum to $n$ terms of: (a) $1\cdot 5\cdot 9 + 2\cdot 6\cdot 10 + 3\cdot 7\cdot 11 + \dots$  and  (b) $12 + 40 + 90 + 168 + 280 + 432 + \dots$

Hint: In (a), $u_r = r(r+4)(r+8) = r^3 + 12r^2 + 32r$. In (b), use a finite difference table to get $u_r = r(r+2)(r+3) = r^3 + 5r^2 + 6r$. Then apply standard power sums!

(a) $u_r = r(r+4)(r+8) = r^3 + 12r^2 + 32r$. Summing from $r = 1$ to $n$: $$S_n = \frac{n^2(n+1)^2}{4} + 12\cdot\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} + 32\cdot\frac{n(n+1)}{2} = \frac{n(n+1)}{4}\big[n^2 + n + 16n + 8 + 64\big] = \mathbf{\frac{n(n+1)(n+8)(n+9)}{4}}$$

(b) From the finite difference table ($\Delta^3 = 6$ constant), $u_r = r^3 + 5r^2 + 6r$. Summing from $r = 1$ to $n$: $$S_n = \frac{n^2(n+1)^2}{4} + 5\cdot\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} + 6\cdot\frac{n(n+1)}{2} = \frac{n(n+1)}{12}\big[3n(n+1) + 10(2n+1) + 36\big] = \mathbf{\frac{n(n+1)(3n^2 + 23n + 46)}{12}}$$

ISRT Final 2024 · Group A [3 Marks]

6. Sum to $n$ terms: $\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{2x}{1 + 1\cdot 3x^2}\right) + \tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{2x}{1 + 3\cdot 5x^2}\right) + \tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{2x}{1 + 5\cdot 7x^2}\right) + \dots$

Hint: Write the $r$-th term's numerator $2x$ as $(2r+1)x - (2r-1)x$ and apply $\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{A-B}{1+AB}\right) = \tan^{-1}A - \tan^{-1}B$.

The $r$-th term is: $$u_r = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{2x}{1 + (2r-1)(2r+1)x^2}\right) = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{(2r+1)x - (2r-1)x}{1 + [(2r+1)x][(2r-1)x]}\right) = \tan^{-1}((2r+1)x) - \tan^{-1}((2r-1)x)$$

Summing from $r = 1$ to $n$, all intermediate terms telescope and cancel:

$$S_n = \tan^{-1}((2n+1)x) - \tan^{-1}(x) = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{2nx}{1 + (2n+1)x^2}\right) \quad \blacksquare$$
