Lesson 5: Theory of Equations

Master the structure of $n$-th degree polynomial equations: Remainder & Factor Theorems, the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, Horner's Synthetic Division, Vieta's root-coefficient relations, symmetric functions, cubics with roots in AP/GP/HP, equation transformations, Descartes' Rule of Signs, and Newton's Method of Divisors.

Prerequisites: Lesson 4 (Complex Numbers & Roots of Unity) — complex conjugate pairs & polynomial factorization over $\mathbb{C}$.

Section 01

Polynomials, Remainder & Factor Theorems, Fundamental Theorem of Algebra

Standard Polynomial Form

A polynomial equation of degree $n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$) has the standard form: $$f(x) = a_0 x^n + a_1 x^{n-1} + a_2 x^{n-2} + \dots + a_{n-1} x + a_n = 0 \qquad (a_0 \neq 0)$$ If $a_0 = 1$, $f(x)$ is called a monic polynomial. A number $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}$ is a root (or zero) of $f(x) = 0$ if $f(\alpha) = 0$.

Remainder Theorem

When a polynomial $f(x)$ is divided by $(x - c)$, the remainder $r$ is equal to $f(c)$. $$f(x) = (x - c)q(x) + r \implies f(c) = r$$

Factor Theorem

$(x - c)$ is a factor of $f(x)$ if and only if $f(c) = 0$. $$f(c) = 0 \iff (x - c) \mid f(x)$$

Part 1: Existence of $n$ Roots (Factorization)

Let $f(x) = a_0 x^n + a_1 x^{n-1} + \dots + a_n = 0$ ($a_0 \neq 0$). By the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, every non-constant polynomial has at least one root $\alpha_1 \in \mathbb{C}$.

By the Factor Theorem, $(x - \alpha_1)$ divides $f(x)$, so $f(x) = (x - \alpha_1)q_1(x)$, where $q_1(x)$ has degree $n - 1$ and leading coefficient $a_0$.

If $n - 1 \ge 1$, then $q_1(x) = 0$ also has a root $\alpha_2$, giving $f(x) = (x - \alpha_1)(x - \alpha_2)q_2(x)$. Repeating this process $n$ times yields a constant quotient $q_n(x) = a_0$:

$$f(x) = a_0(x - \alpha_1)(x - \alpha_2)\cdots(x - \alpha_n)$$

Thus $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n$ are $n$ roots of $f(x) = 0$ (counting multiplicities).

Part 2: No More Than $n$ Roots (Proof by Contradiction)

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that $f(x) = 0$ has an $(n+1)$-th root $\alpha_{n+1}$ distinct from $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n$. Substituting $x = \alpha_{n+1}$ into the factorization gives:

$$0 = f(\alpha_{n+1}) = a_0(\alpha_{n+1} - \alpha_1)(\alpha_{n+1} - \alpha_2)\cdots(\alpha_{n+1} - \alpha_n)$$

However, $a_0 \neq 0$ and every factor $(\alpha_{n+1} - \alpha_k) \neq 0$ since $\alpha_{n+1}$ is distinct from each $\alpha_k$. In the field $\mathbb{C}$ (which has no zero divisors), a product of nonzero numbers cannot equal $0$—a contradiction! Hence $f(x) = 0$ has exactly $n$ roots and no more. $\blacksquare$

Conjugate & Irrational Root Pairing Theorems

• Complex Conjugates: If $f(x) = 0$ has real coefficients and $p + iq$ ($q \neq 0$) is a root, then $p - iq$ is also a root. (Consequence: every odd-degree real polynomial has at least one real root!)

• Surd Conjugates: If $f(x) = 0$ has rational coefficients and $p + \sqrt{q}$ ($\sqrt{q}$ irrational) is a root, then $p - \sqrt{q}$ is also a root.

Section 02 · Class Notes (Prottasha)

Synthetic Division (Horner's Scheme)

Synthetic division is a compact, tabular algorithm for dividing a polynomial $f(x) = a_0 x^n + a_1 x^{n-1} + \dots + a_n$ by a linear binomial $(x - c)$ to obtain the quotient $q(x) = b_0 x^{n-1} + b_1 x^{n-2} + \dots + b_{n-1}$ and remainder $r = f(c)$.

Algebraic Derivation (Comparing Coefficients)

Expanding $f(x) = (x - c)q(x) + r$ gives: $$(x - c)(b_0 x^{n-1} + b_1 x^{n-2} + \dots + b_{n-1}) + r = b_0 x^n + (b_1 - c b_0)x^{n-1} + (b_2 - c b_1)x^{n-2} + \dots + (r - c b_{n-1})$$

Equating coefficients with $a_0 x^n + a_1 x^{n-1} + \dots + a_n$ yields the recursive Horner rule:

$$b_0 = a_0, \qquad b_k = a_k + c b_{k-1} \;\;(1 \le k \le n-1), \qquad r = a_n + c b_{n-1}$$
⚠️

Exam Trap Alert: Missing Powers & Sign of $c$!

1. Zero Placeholders: Always insert 0 for any missing power of $x$! For example, $x^3 + qx + r$ has coefficients 1, 0, q, r (not 1, q, r).
2. Divisor Sign: Synthetic division uses $c$ from $(x - c)$. When dividing by $(x + c)$, you must use $-c$ as the multiplier!

Worked Example (ISRT Final 2019 Q1b): Repeated Synthetic Division of $x^3 - 5x^2 - 2x + 24 = 0$

4 Marks

Divide $x^3 - 5x^2 - 2x + 24$ by $(x - 4)$, then divide the quotient by $(x - 3)$ to find all roots:

Step 1: Divide by $(x - 4)$ ($c = 4$)
 4 |  1   -5   -2    24
   |       4   -4   -24
     1   -1   -6  |  0
Quotient: $q_1(x) = x^2 - x - 6$, $r = 0$.
Step 2: Divide $q_1(x)$ by $(x - 3)$ ($c = 3$)
 3 |  1   -1   -6
   |       3    6
     1    2  |  0
Final factor: $x + 2 = 0 \implies$ Roots: $4, 3, -2$.

Section 03

Vieta's Formulas (Relations Between Roots & Coefficients)

Since $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n$ are the $n$ roots of $a_0 x^n + a_1 x^{n-1} + \dots + a_n = 0$, we have the identity:

$$x^n + \frac{a_1}{a_0}x^{n-1} + \frac{a_2}{a_0}x^{n-2} + \dots + \frac{a_n}{a_0} = (x - \alpha_1)(x - \alpha_2)\cdots(x - \alpha_n)$$

Expanding the right-hand side by grouping powers of $x$ gives $x^n - (\sum \alpha_1)x^{n-1} + (\sum \alpha_1\alpha_2)x^{n-2} - \dots + (-1)^n \alpha_1\alpha_2\cdots\alpha_n$. Equating coefficients of like powers of $x$ yields Vieta's Formulas:

$$S_1 = \sum \alpha_1 = -\frac{a_1}{a_0}, \qquad S_2 = \sum \alpha_1\alpha_2 = \frac{a_2}{a_0}, \qquad S_3 = \sum \alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3 = -\frac{a_3}{a_0}, \qquad \dots, \qquad \alpha_1\alpha_2\cdots\alpha_n = (-1)^n\frac{a_n}{a_0}$$
Quick-Reference Cubic Card

$x^3 + px^2 + qx + r = 0$  (roots $a, b, c$)

• $S_1 = a + b + c = -p$
• $S_2 = ab + bc + ca = q$
• $S_3 = abc = -r$

Depressed Cubic ($p = 0$): For $x^3 + qx + r = 0$, $a + b + c = 0$, which makes $b + c = -a$, $c + a = -b$, $a + b = -c$!

$\sum a^3 = -p^3 + 3pq - 3r$
$\sum \dfrac{1}{a} = -\dfrac{q}{r}$
$\sum \dfrac{1}{a^2} = \dfrac{q^2 - 2pr}{r^2}$

Section 04

Solving Cubics with Roots in AP, GP, and HP

AP

Arithmetic Progression

Choose symmetric roots: $$a - d,\quad a,\quad a + d$$ Shortcut: Sum gives $3a = -p \implies a = -p/3$. Substitute $a$ or deflate by $(x - a)$ immediately!

GP

Geometric Progression

Choose symmetric roots: $$\frac{a}{r},\quad a,\quad ar$$ Shortcut: Product gives $a^3 = -r_{\text{const}} \implies a = (-r_{\text{const}})^{1/3}$. Deflate by $(x - a)$!

HP

Harmonic Progression

Reciprocals are in AP: $$y = \frac{1}{x} \text{ has roots in AP}$$ Shortcut: Transform via $x = 1/y$ (reversing coefficients), then apply the AP rule!

Worked Derivation (ISRT Final 2019 Q4a): Roots of $x^3 + 3px^2 + 3qx + r = 0$ in HP

3 Marks

Step 1: Set $x = 1/y$. Multiplying through by $y^3$ gives $ry^3 + 3qy^2 + 3py + 1 = 0$, whose roots are in AP: $\alpha - d, \alpha, \alpha + d$.

Step 2: Sum of $y$-roots: $3\alpha = -\frac{3q}{r} \implies \alpha = -\frac{q}{r}$. Since $\alpha$ is a root of the $y$-equation, substitute $y = -q/r$: $$r\left(-\frac{q}{r}\right)^3 + 3q\left(-\frac{q}{r}\right)^2 + 3p\left(-\frac{q}{r}\right) + 1 = 0 \implies \frac{2q^3}{r^2} - \frac{3pq}{r} + 1 = 0$$

Multiplying by $r^2$: $2q^3 - 3pqr + r^2 = 0 \implies 2q^3 = r(3pq - r)$. $\blacksquare$

Section 05

Transformation of Equations

To construct a new polynomial equation whose roots are $y = \phi(x)$ (where $x$ ranges over the roots of $f(x) = 0$), solve for $x$ in terms of $y$ and substitute into $f(x) = 0$:

Desired Roots $y$ Substitution Transformed Equation Quick Rule
Negated: $y = -x$ $x = -y$ $f(-y) = 0$ Flip signs of alternate terms (starting from $a_1$).
Reciprocal: $y = 1/x$ $x = 1/y$ $y^n f(1/y) = 0$ Reverse the order of coefficients: $a_n y^n + \dots + a_0 = 0$.
Scaled: $y = kx$ $x = y/k$ $k^n f(y/k) = 0$ Multiply coefficients by $1, k, k^2, \dots, k^n$.
Diminished: $y = x - h$ $x = y + h$ $f(y + h) = 0$ Successive synthetic division by $h$; remainders give new coeffs!

The Symmetric-Function Substitution Trick (ISRT 2019–2025 Favorite)

When asked to form an equation with symmetric multi-root expressions like $y = \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b}$ or $y = \beta\gamma + \frac{1}{\alpha}$, use Vieta's relations to collapse $y$ into a function of a single root $x$ (e.g., $y = x^2/r$ or $y = (r+1)/x$), solve for $x$ (or $x^2$) in terms of $y$, and substitute into $f(x) = 0$!

Section 06

Descartes' Rule of Signs & Newton's Method of Divisors

Descartes' Rule of Signs ISRT 2nd Incourse 2021 Q3

For a real polynomial $P(x)$ arranged in descending powers (ignoring zero terms):

  • Positive real roots: At most $V_+$ (the number of sign changes in $P(x)$), or less than $V_+$ by a positive even integer ($V_+, V_+ - 2, \dots$).
  • Negative real roots: At most $V_-$ (sign changes in $P(-x)$), or less than $V_-$ by an even integer.
  • Non-real complex roots: Any remaining roots up to degree $n$ (always an even count of conjugate pairs).
Newton's Method of Divisors ISRT Final 2017 Q4a

For a monic integer polynomial $f(x) = x^n + a_1 x^{n-1} + \dots + a_n$, any integer root $c$ must divide $a_n$. Instead of testing every divisor of $a_n$, Newton's filter eliminates bad candidates immediately:

$$(c - 1) \mid f(1) \qquad \text{AND} \qquad (c + 1) \mid f(-1)$$

Verify surviving candidates via Synthetic Division to find all integer roots!

ISRT Final 2025 Q3(a) / 2024 / 2019 / 2021 [3–4 Marks]

1. If $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are the roots of the equation $x^3 + qx + r = 0$, find the value of $\displaystyle\sum \frac{2\beta\gamma - \alpha^2}{\beta + \gamma - \alpha}$.

Hint: Use $\beta + \gamma = -\alpha$, $\beta\gamma = -r/\alpha$, and $\alpha^3 = -q\alpha - r$ to reduce the general term to $\frac{r}{2\alpha^2} - \frac{q}{2\alpha}$.

From $x^3 + qx + r = 0$: $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 0$, $\sum\alpha\beta = q$, and $\alpha\beta\gamma = -r$. Thus $\beta + \gamma = -\alpha$, $\beta\gamma = -\frac{r}{\alpha}$, and $\alpha^3 = -q\alpha - r$.

Simplify the general term: $$\frac{2\beta\gamma - \alpha^2}{\beta + \gamma - \alpha} = \frac{-\frac{2r}{\alpha} - \alpha^2}{-2\alpha} = \frac{2r + \alpha^3}{2\alpha^2} = \frac{2r + (-q\alpha - r)}{2\alpha^2} = \frac{r - q\alpha}{2\alpha^2} = \frac{r}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\alpha^2} - \frac{q}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\alpha}$$

Now $\sum\frac{1}{\alpha} = \frac{\sum\alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta\gamma} = -\frac{q}{r}$, and $\sum\frac{1}{\alpha^2} = \left(\sum\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)^2 - 2\frac{\sum\alpha}{\alpha\beta\gamma} = \frac{q^2}{r^2} - 0 = \frac{q^2}{r^2}$.

Summing over all 3 roots: $\displaystyle\sum \frac{2\beta\gamma - \alpha^2}{\beta + \gamma - \alpha} = \frac{r}{2}\left(\frac{q^2}{r^2}\right) - \frac{q}{2}\left(-\frac{q}{r}\right) = \frac{q^2}{2r} + \frac{q^2}{2r} = \frac{q^2}{r}$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT Final 2025 Q3(b) / 2024 / 2020 / 2019 [3–6 Marks]

2. If $a, b, c$ are the roots of $x^3 + qx + r = 0$, form the equation whose roots are $\left(\dfrac{1}{a} + \dfrac{1}{b}\right),\; \left(\dfrac{1}{b} + \dfrac{1}{c}\right),\; \left(\dfrac{1}{c} + \dfrac{1}{a}\right)$.

Hint: Express $y = \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} = \frac{a+b}{ab} = \frac{-c}{-r/c} = \frac{c^2}{r}$, so $c^2 = ry$. Square $c(c^2+q) = -r$ and replace $c^2$ with $ry$!

From $x^3 + qx + r = 0$, $a + b + c = 0 \implies a + b = -c$ and $abc = -r \implies ab = -r/c$.

Let $y = \dfrac{1}{a} + \dfrac{1}{b} = \dfrac{a+b}{ab} = \dfrac{-c}{-r/c} = \dfrac{c^2}{r} \implies c^2 = ry$.

Since $c$ satisfies $c^3 + qc + r = 0$, rewrite as $c(c^2 + q) = -r$ and square both sides: $$c^2(c^2 + q)^2 = r^2$$

Substitute $c^2 = ry$: $ry(ry + q)^2 = r^2 \implies y(r^2 y^2 + 2qry + q^2) = r$ (dividing by $r \neq 0$).

Required Equation: $r^2 y^3 + 2qr y^2 + q^2 y - r = 0$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT Final 2025 · Q4(b) [4 Marks]

3. Solve $x^3 + x^2 + 2x + 8 = 0$, the roots being in geometrical progression (GP).

Hint: Set roots to $\frac{a}{r}, a, ar$. Their product is $a^3 = -8 \implies a = -2$. Deflate by $(x + 2)$ using synthetic division!

Let the roots in GP be $\frac{a}{r}, a, ar$. By Vieta's product formula: $\frac{a}{r} \cdot a \cdot ar = a^3 = -8 \implies a = -2$.

Dividing $x^3 + x^2 + 2x + 8$ by $(x + 2)$ via synthetic division ($c = -2$) yields quotient $x^2 - x + 4$ and remainder $0$.

Solving $x^2 - x + 4 = 0$ via the quadratic formula: $x = \dfrac{1 \pm \sqrt{1 - 16}}{2} = \dfrac{1 \pm i\sqrt{15}}{2}$.

Roots: $-2,\; \dfrac{1 + i\sqrt{15}}{2},\; \dfrac{1 - i\sqrt{15}}{2}$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT Final 2025 · Q4(c) [4 Marks]

4. If $a, b, c$ are the roots of the equation $x^3 + px^2 + qx + r = 0$, find the value of $\displaystyle\sum \frac{b^2 + c^2}{b + c}$.

Hint: Split $\frac{b^2+c^2}{b+c} = (b+c) - \frac{2bc}{b+c}$. For the second sum, use $(c+a)(a+b) = (-p-b)(-p-c) = bc - pa$ and denominator $(b+c)(c+a)(a+b) = r - pq$.

Rewrite each term: $\dfrac{b^2 + c^2}{b + c} = \dfrac{(b+c)^2 - 2bc}{b + c} = (b + c) - 2\dfrac{bc}{b + c}$.

Summing over all 3 cyclic terms: $\sum(b+c) = 2(a+b+c) = -2p$.

For $\sum \frac{bc}{b+c}$, the common denominator is $(b+c)(c+a)(a+b) = r - pq$. Since $(c+a)(a+b) = (-p-b)(-p-c) = p^2 + p(b+c) + bc = p^2 + p(-p-a) + bc = bc - pa$, the numerator is: $$\sum bc(bc - pa) = \sum b^2 c^2 - 3pabc = (q^2 - 2pr) + 3pr = q^2 + pr$$

Combining both parts: $\displaystyle\sum \frac{b^2+c^2}{b+c} = -2p - 2\frac{q^2 + pr}{r - pq} = \frac{2p^2q - 2q^2 - 4pr}{r - pq}$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT 2nd Incourse 2021 · Q3 [6 Marks]

5. Define synthetic division. If $a, b, c$ are the roots of the equation $x^3 + px^2 + qx + r = 0$, find the value of $(b+c)(c+a)(a+b)$ in terms of $p, q, r$.

Hint: Since $a + b + c = -p$, rewrite $(b+c)(c+a)(a+b) = (-p-a)(-p-b)(-p-c) = f(-p)$!

Method 1 (Slick $f(-p)$ Substitution): Since $f(x) = x^3 + px^2 + qx + r = (x-a)(x-b)(x-c)$ and $a+b+c = -p$, we have $b+c = -p-a$, $c+a = -p-b$, and $a+b = -p-c$. Therefore: $$(b+c)(c+a)(a+b) = (-p-a)(-p-b)(-p-c) = f(-p) = (-p)^3 + p(-p)^2 + q(-p) + r = \mathbf{r - pq}$$

Method 2 (Direct Expansion): $(b+c)(c+a)(a+b) = (a+b+c)(ab+bc+ca) - abc = (-p)(q) - (-r) = \mathbf{r - pq}$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT 2nd Incourse 2021 / 2024 · Q3 [2 Marks]

6. Use Descartes' Rule of Signs to determine the number of possible positive and negative real solutions of $P(x) = 2x^7 + 15x^6 + 31x^5 - x^4 - 49x^3 - 52x^2 - 78x - 36 = 0$.

Hint: Count sign changes in $P(x)$ (only 1!) and in $P(-x)$ (flip signs of odd powers $x^7, x^5, x^3, x$).

Positive Real Roots: Signs of $P(x)$ are $+, +, +, -, -, -, -, -$, giving $V_+ = 1$ sign change. Hence there is exactly $1$ positive real root.

Negative Real Roots: $P(-x) = -2x^7 + 15x^6 - 31x^5 - x^4 + 49x^3 - 52x^2 + 78x - 36$. Signs are $-, +, -, -, +, -, +, -$, giving $V_- = 6$ sign changes. Hence there are $6, 4, 2,$ or $0$ negative real roots.

Summary: Exactly 1 positive real root; 6, 4, 2, or 0 negative real roots (and 0, 2, 4, or 6 non-real complex roots respectively). $\blacksquare$
