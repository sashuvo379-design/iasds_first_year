Section 10.1

Structure of Higher-Order Linear ODEs: $y_c$ vs. $y_p$

Reading…

An $n$-th order linear differential equation with constant coefficients $a_0, a_1, \dots, a_n \in \mathbb{R}$ ($a_0 \neq 0$) is written compactly using the differential operator $D = \dfrac{d}{dx}$ (so $D^k y = \dfrac{d^k y}{dx^k}$): $$a_0 \frac{d^n y}{dx^n} + a_1 \frac{d^{n-1} y}{dx^{n-1}} + \dots + a_{n-1}\frac{dy}{dx} + a_n y = Q(x) \iff f(D)\,y = Q(x)$$ where $f(D) = a_0 D^n + a_1 D^{n-1} + \dots + a_n$ is a polynomial operator of degree $n$.

Exam Definition Box · Superposition Principle

ISRT Final 2025 Q1(c) & 2nd Incourse 2022 Q1
Complementary Function / Solution ($y_c$)

The general solution of the associated homogeneous equation $f(D)y = 0$. For an $n$-th order ODE, $y_c$ contains exactly $n$ essential arbitrary constants $C_1, \dots, C_n$.

Particular Integral / Solution ($y_p$)

Any single function satisfying the complete non-homogeneous equation $f(D)y_p = Q(x)$ that contains no arbitrary constants.

Complete General Solution: $\quad y(x) = y_c(x) + y_p(x) \qquad \big(\text{since } f(D)(y_c + y_p) = 0 + Q(x) = Q(x)\big)$

Linear Independence & The Wronskian Determinant

Solutions $y_1, y_2, \dots, y_n$ of $f(D)y = 0$ form a fundamental set (linearly independent) on an interval $I$ if and only if their Wronskian $W(y_1, \dots, y_n) \neq 0$:

2nd-Order Wronskian
$$W(y_1, y_2) = \begin{vmatrix} y_1 & y_2 \\ y_1' & y_2' \end{vmatrix} = y_1 y_2' - y_2 y_1'$$
3rd-Order Wronskian
$$W(y_1, y_2, y_3) = \begin{vmatrix} y_1 & y_2 & y_3 \\ y_1' & y_2' & y_3' \\ y_1'' & y_2'' & y_3'' \end{vmatrix}$$
Section 10.2

Homogeneous Constant-Coefficient ODEs & The Auxiliary Equation

Reading…

Substituting the exponential trial $y = e^{mx}$ into $f(D)y = 0$ gives $f(m)e^{mx} = 0$. Since $e^{mx} \neq 0$, we obtain the Auxiliary (Characteristic) Equation $f(m) = 0$. The form of $y_c(x)$ depends entirely on its roots:

Case Roots of $f(m) = 0$ Contribution to $y_c(x)$
Case 1 · Distinct Real $m_1, m_2, \dots, m_n \in \mathbb{R}$ (all distinct) $C_1 e^{m_1 x} + C_2 e^{m_2 x} + \dots + C_n e^{m_n x}$
Case 2 · Repeated Real $m$ repeated $k$ times $(C_1 + C_2 x + C_3 x^2 + \dots + C_k x^{k-1})\,e^{mx}$
Case 3 · Complex Conjugate $m = \alpha \pm i\beta$ ($\beta > 0$) $e^{\alpha x}\big(C_1 \cos\beta x + C_2 \sin\beta x\big)$
Case 4 · Repeated Complex $m = \alpha \pm i\beta$ repeated twice $e^{\alpha x}\big[(C_1 + C_2 x)\cos\beta x + (C_3 + C_4 x)\sin\beta x\big]$
Overdamped
$\Delta > 0$, both $m < 0$. Smooth decay.
Critically Damped
$\Delta = 0$, $m < 0$. Fastest decay.
Underdamped
$\Delta < 0$, $\alpha < 0$. Decaying waves.
Undamped
$\alpha = 0$, $\pm i\beta$. Pure oscillation.
Unstable Growth
Any $\operatorname{Re}(m) > 0$. Diverges!

Section 10.3 · Core ISRT Method

Particular Integrals via the Inverse $D$-Operator $\left(y_p = \dfrac{1}{f(D)} Q(x)\right)$

Reading…
Type 1 · Exponential $Q(x) = e^{ax}$ ISRT Final 2025 Q5(ii)

Replace $D \to a$: $\dfrac{1}{f(D)} e^{ax} = \dfrac{e^{ax}}{f(a)}$ provided $f(a) \neq 0$.
Case of Failure ($f(a) = 0$): Multiply by $x$ and differentiate denominator: $x\dfrac{1}{f'(a)} e^{ax}$ (if $f'(a)=0$ too, repeat: $x^r \frac{1}{f^{(r)}(a)} e^{ax}$).
Exam Example: For $y'' + 4y' + 3y = e^{-3x}$, $f(-3) = 0$ and $f'(-3) = -2 \implies y_p = -\dfrac{1}{2}xe^{-3x}$.

Type 2 · Sine or Cosine $Q(x) = \sin(ax + b)$ or $\cos(ax + b)$

Replace $D^2 \to -a^2$ (leaving single $D$ untouched!): $\dfrac{1}{f(D^2)}\sin(ax+b) = \dfrac{1}{f(-a^2)}\sin(ax+b)$ if $f(-a^2) \neq 0$. If $D$ remains in the denominator (e.g., $\frac{1}{pD + q}$), rationalize by multiplying top and bottom by $(pD - q)$ so $D^2$ appears again! If $f(-a^2) = 0$ (resonance), multiply by $x$ and differentiate denominator w.r.t. $D$.

Type 3 · Polynomial $Q(x) = x^k$ ISRT Final 2025 Q5(i)

Factor out the lowest-degree term of $f(D)$ to rewrite $\frac{1}{f(D)} x^k = \frac{1}{cD^r}[1 + \phi(D)]^{-1} x^k$, then expand $[1 + \phi(D)]^{-1}$ as a binomial series in ascending powers of $D$ up to $D^k$ (since $D^{m}(x^k) = 0$ for all $m > k$).
Exam Example: $y'' - 4y' + 4y = x^2 \implies \frac{1}{4}\left(1 - \frac{D}{2}\right)^{-2} x^2 = \frac{1}{4}\left(1 + D + \frac{3}{4}D^2\right)x^2 = \frac{1}{4}x^2 + \frac{1}{2}x + \frac{3}{8}$.

Type 4 · Exponential Shift $Q(x) = e^{ax} V(x)$ Final 2025 Q5(iii) & Incourse 2025 Q6(ii)

Pull $e^{ax}$ to the left of the operator by shifting $D \to D + a$: $$\frac{1}{f(D)}\big[e^{ax} V(x)\big] = e^{ax}\frac{1}{f(D + a)} V(x)$$ Then evaluate $\frac{1}{f(D+a)} V(x)$ using Type 2 or Type 3! (Directly solves $e^x\sin x$ and $xe^{-x}$.)

Type 5 · Product $Q(x) = x\,V(x)$

Use the operator commutator formula: $$\frac{1}{f(D)}\big[x\,V(x)\big] = \left[x - \frac{f'(D)}{f(D)}\right]\frac{1}{f(D)}V(x)$$

⚠️

Critical Exam Traps in Inverse $D$-Operator

1. Never substitute $D = a$ inside $\sin(ax)$ or $\cos(ax)$! Only $D^2 = -a^2$ is valid (because $D^2\sin(ax) = -a^2\sin(ax)$, whereas $D\sin(ax) = a\cos(ax) \neq a\sin(ax)$).

2. Constant RHS $Q(x) = c$: Always rewrite a constant $c$ as $c\,e^{0x}$ and apply Type 1 with $a = 0$ (watch out if $m = 0$ is a root of $f(m) = 0$!).

Section 10.4 Explicitly Required in ISRT Final 2023 Q6(c)

Method of Undetermined Coefficients (Trial-Function Method)

Reading…
Forcing Term $Q(x)$ Initial Trial Function $y_p$
Constant $c$ $A$
Polynomial $c_k x^k + \dots + c_0$ $A_k x^k + A_{k-1} x^{k-1} + \dots + A_1 x + A_0$
Exponential $c e^{ax}$ $A e^{ax}$
$\sin(\beta x)$ or $\cos(\beta x)$ (or both) $A\cos(\beta x) + B\sin(\beta x)$ (always include BOTH!)
$e^{\alpha x}\sin(\beta x)$ or $e^{\alpha x}\cos(\beta x)$ $e^{\alpha x}\big(A\cos\beta x + B\sin\beta x\big)$
Golden Modification Rule (Resonance / Clash Check): If any term in your initial trial $y_p$ duplicates a term already present in the complementary function $y_c$, multiply that entire trial group by $x^s$, where $s$ is the smallest positive integer that eliminates all duplication with $y_c$.

1. Complementary Solution ($y_c$): Auxiliary equation $m^2 - m = m(m - 1) = 0 \implies m = 0, 1$. Thus $y_c = C_1 + C_2 e^x$.

2. Trial Function with Modification Check: For $\sin x$, trial is $A\cos x + B\sin x$ (no clash). For the constant $1$, the initial trial $C$ clashes with $C_1$ ($m = 0$) in $y_c$! Multiply by $x \implies Cx$. Combined trial: $$y_p = A\cos x + B\sin x + Cx$$

3. Differentiate & Substitute: $y_p' = -A\sin x + B\cos x + C$, and $y_p'' = -A\cos x - B\sin x$. Substituting into $y_p'' - y_p' = \sin x + 1$: $$(-A - B)\cos x + (A - B)\sin x - C = 0\cdot\cos x + 1\cdot\sin x + 1$$

4. Solve for $A, B, C$: $-C = 1 \implies C = -1$; $-A - B = 0 \implies B = -A$; $A - B = 1 \implies 2A = 1 \implies A = \frac{1}{2},\; B = -\frac{1}{2}$.

General Solution: $y = C_1 + C_2 e^x + \dfrac{1}{2}\cos x - \dfrac{1}{2}\sin x - x$. $\blacksquare$

Section 10.5 ★ 4× Repeated ISRT Question (2020, 2022, 2025)

Cauchy–Euler (Homogeneous Linear Variable-Coefficient) Equations

Reading…

A Cauchy–Euler equation ($x > 0$) has each $k$-th derivative multiplied by $x^k$: $$a_0 x^n \frac{d^n y}{dx^n} + a_1 x^{n-1}\frac{d^{n-1} y}{dx^{n-1}} + \dots + a_{n-1} x\frac{dy}{dx} + a_n y = Q(x)$$ Substituting $x = e^z \iff z = \ln x$ and defining $\theta = \dfrac{d}{dz}$ converts it into a constant-coefficient linear ODE in $z$:

1st Derivative
$xD = \theta$
2nd Derivative
$x^2 D^2 = \theta(\theta - 1)$
3rd Derivative
$x^3 D^3 = \theta(\theta - 1)(\theta - 2)$

Since $z = \ln x$, we have $\dfrac{dz}{dx} = \dfrac{1}{x}$. By the Chain Rule: $$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{dz}\frac{dz}{dx} = \frac{1}{x}\frac{dy}{dz} \implies x\frac{dy}{dx} = \theta y$$

Differentiating $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{x}\frac{dy}{dz}$ with respect to $x$ via the product and chain rules: $$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -\frac{1}{x^2}\frac{dy}{dz} + \frac{1}{x}\left(\frac{d^2y}{dz^2}\cdot\frac{1}{x}\right) = \frac{1}{x^2}\left(\frac{d^2y}{dz^2} - \frac{dy}{dz}\right) \implies x^2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = (\theta^2 - \theta)y = \theta(\theta - 1)y \quad \blacksquare$$

ISRT Exam Favorite Spotlight: $x^2 y'' - x y' + y = \ln x^2$

2020, 2022, Incourse '25 & Final '25

Key Reminder: Don't get tricked by $\ln x^2$! By log laws, $\ln x^2 = 2\ln x = 2z$ (NOT $z^2$!). Substituting $x^2 D^2 = \theta(\theta-1)$ and $xD = \theta$ yields: $$\big[\theta(\theta - 1) - \theta + 1\big]y = 2z \implies (\theta - 1)^2 y = 2z \implies y = (C_1 + C_2 \ln x)x + 2\ln x + 4$$

Section 10.6

Method of Variation of Parameters (General RHS)

Reading…

Unlike the Inverse $D$-Operator or Undetermined Coefficients, Variation of Parameters works for any continuous forcing function $Q(x)$—including $\sec x, \tan x, \ln x, \frac{1}{1+e^x}$—provided we normalize the leading coefficient $a_0 = 1$ first!

2nd-Order Formula ($y'' + py' + qy = Q(x)$)

Given $y_c = C_1 y_1 + C_2 y_2$, set $y_p = u_1(x)y_1 + u_2(x)y_2$ where: $$u_1' = \frac{-y_2\,Q(x)}{W(y_1, y_2)}, \qquad u_2' = \frac{y_1\,Q(x)}{W(y_1, y_2)}$$

3rd-Order Generalization (Cramer's Rule)

Given $y_c = C_1 y_1 + C_2 y_2 + C_3 y_3$, set $y_p = \sum_{k=1}^3 u_k(x)y_k$ where: $$u_k' = \frac{W_k(x)\,Q(x)}{W(y_1, y_2, y_3)} \qquad (k = 1, 2, 3)$$ with $W_k$ replacing column $k$ of $W$ by $[0, 0, 1]^T$.

1. Homogeneous Solution: $m^3 - 6m^2 + 11m - 6 = (m-1)(m-2)(m-3) = 0 \implies y_1 = e^x,\; y_2 = e^{2x},\; y_3 = e^{3x}$.

2. Wronskian: $W(e^x, e^{2x}, e^{3x}) = e^{6x}\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 4 & 9 \end{vmatrix} = 2e^{6x}$.

3. Cramer Determinants ($Q(x) = e^x$):

  • $W_1 = e^{5x}(3 - 2) = e^{5x} \implies u_1' = \frac{e^{5x}\cdot e^x}{2e^{6x}} = \frac{1}{2} \implies u_1 = \frac{1}{2}x$.
  • $W_2 = -e^{4x}(3 - 1) = -2e^{4x} \implies u_2' = \frac{-2e^{5x}}{2e^{6x}} = -e^{-x} \implies u_2 = e^{-x}$.
  • $W_3 = e^{3x}(2 - 1) = e^{3x} \implies u_3' = \frac{e^{4x}}{2e^{6x}} = \frac{1}{2}e^{-2x} \implies u_3 = -\frac{1}{4}e^{-2x}$.

4. Assemble $y_p$: $y_p = \frac{1}{2}xe^x + e^{-x}e^{2x} - \frac{1}{4}e^{-2x}e^{3x} = \frac{1}{2}xe^x + \frac{3}{4}e^x$. Absorbing $\frac{3}{4}e^x$ into $C_1 e^x$ yields $y = C_1 e^x + C_2 e^{2x} + C_3 e^{3x} + \dfrac{1}{2}xe^x$. $\blacksquare$

Method Best Used When… Speed / Limitation
Inverse $D$-Operator Constant coeffs; $Q = e^{ax}, \sin ax, x^k, e^{ax}V(x)$ Fastest on ISRT finals; watch $D^2=-a^2$ & failures
Undetermined Coeffs Constant coeffs; explicitly requested (e.g., 2023 Q6c) Must check clashes with $y_c$; fails for $\sec x, \ln x$
Cauchy–Euler Variable coeffs matching $x^k y^{(k)}$ ($x > 0$) Always reduce via $x = e^z$ first!
Variation of Params Any continuous $Q(x)$ ($\sec x, \tan x$) or requested Universal, but requires Wronskian & integration

ISRT Final 2025 Q5(i) & 2nd Incourse 2022 Q2(a) [4 Marks]

1. Solve: $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 4\dfrac{dy}{dx} + 4y = x^2$.

Hint: Auxiliary roots are $m = 2, 2$. For $y_p$, factor out $4$ from $(D-2)^2$ to expand $\frac{1}{4}(1 - D/2)^{-2} x^2$ up to $D^2$.

1. Complementary Solution: $(m - 2)^2 = 0 \implies m = 2, 2 \implies y_c = (C_1 + C_2 x)e^{2x}$.

2. Particular Integral: $y_p = \dfrac{1}{(D-2)^2}x^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}\left(1 - \dfrac{D}{2}\right)^{-2}x^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}\left(1 + 2\cdot\dfrac{D}{2} + 3\cdot\dfrac{D^2}{4} + \dots\right)x^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}\left(x^2 + 2x + \dfrac{3}{2}\right) = \dfrac{1}{4}x^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{3}{8}$.

General Solution: $y = (C_1 + C_2 x)e^{2x} + \dfrac{1}{4}x^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{3}{8}$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT Final 2025 Q5(ii) & 2nd Incourse 2022 Q2(b) [4 Marks]

2. Solve: $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 4\dfrac{dy}{dx} + 3y = e^{-3x}$.

Hint: Substituting $D = -3$ gives $0$ (case of failure!). Multiply by $x$ and differentiate the denominator: $f'(D) = 2D + 4$.

1. Complementary Solution: $m^2 + 4m + 3 = (m+1)(m+3) = 0 \implies m = -1, -3 \implies y_c = C_1 e^{-x} + C_2 e^{-3x}$.

2. Particular Integral (Case of Failure): $f(-3) = (-3)^2 + 4(-3) + 3 = 0$. Applying the failure rule with $f'(D) = 2D + 4$: $$y_p = x\cdot\frac{1}{2(-3) + 4}e^{-3x} = -\frac{1}{2}x e^{-3x}$$

General Solution: $y = C_1 e^{-x} + C_2 e^{-3x} - \dfrac{1}{2}x e^{-3x}$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT Final 2025 Q5(iii) & 2nd Incourse 2024 Q2(c) [4 Marks]

3. Solve: $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 2\dfrac{dy}{dx} + y = e^x \sin x$.

Hint: Apply the Exponential Shift Rule: pull $e^x$ left and replace $D \to D + 1$ in $(D - 1)^2$, leaving $\frac{1}{D^2}\sin x$.

1. Complementary Solution: $(m - 1)^2 = 0 \implies m = 1, 1 \implies y_c = (C_1 + C_2 x)e^x$.

2. Particular Integral (Exponential Shift): $$y_p = \frac{1}{(D - 1)^2}\big(e^x \sin x\big) = e^x \frac{1}{((D + 1) - 1)^2}\sin x = e^x \frac{1}{D^2}\sin x = e^x \frac{1}{-1^2}\sin x = -e^x \sin x$$

General Solution: $y = (C_1 + C_2 x)e^x - e^x \sin x$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT Final 2025 Q5(iv) & 1st Incourse 2025 Q6(i) [4 Marks]

4. Solve the Cauchy–Euler equation: $x^2 \dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} - x\dfrac{dy}{dx} + y = \ln x^2$.

Hint: Set $x = e^z$ ($z = \ln x$). Notice $\ln x^2 = 2\ln x = 2z$, and $[\theta(\theta-1) - \theta + 1]y = (\theta - 1)^2 y = 2z$.

1. Transform via $x = e^z$ ($\theta = d/dz$): $\big[\theta(\theta - 1) - \theta + 1\big]y = 2z \implies (\theta - 1)^2 y = 2z$.

2. Complementary Solution: $(m - 1)^2 = 0 \implies m = 1, 1 \implies y_c = (C_1 + C_2 z)e^z = (C_1 + C_2 \ln x)x$.

3. Particular Integral: $y_p = \dfrac{1}{(1 - \theta)^2}(2z) = (1 + 2\theta + \dots)(2z) = 2z + 4 = 2\ln x + 4$.

General Solution: $y = (C_1 + C_2 \ln x)x + 2\ln x + 4$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT 1st Incourse 2025 Q6(ii) & Final 2020 Q8(ii) [4 Marks]

5. Solve: $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 4\dfrac{dy}{dx} - 5y = x e^{-x}$.

Hint: Shift $e^{-x}$ left by replacing $D \to D - 1$, yielding $e^{-x}\frac{1}{D^2 - 6D}x$. Factor out $-6D$, expand $(1 - D/6)^{-1}x$, and integrate once via $\frac{1}{D}$.

1. Complementary Solution: $m^2 - 4m - 5 = (m-5)(m+1) = 0 \implies m = 5, -1 \implies y_c = C_1 e^{5x} + C_2 e^{-x}$.

2. Particular Integral: $$y_p = e^{-x}\frac{1}{(D-1)^2 - 4(D-1) - 5}x = e^{-x}\frac{1}{D^2 - 6D}x = -\frac{e^{-x}}{6D}\left(1 - \frac{D}{6}\right)^{-1}x = -\frac{e^{-x}}{6D}\left(x + \frac{1}{6}\right) = -e^{-x}\left(\frac{x^2}{12} + \frac{x}{36}\right)$$

General Solution: $y = C_1 e^{5x} + C_2 e^{-x} - e^{-x}\left(\dfrac{x^2}{12} + \dfrac{x}{36}\right)$. $\blacksquare$

ISRT Final 2020 · Q7(a) [6 Marks]

6. Solve $y''' - 6y'' + 11y' - 6y = e^x$ by the Method of Variation of Parameters (and verify with the Inverse $D$-operator).

Hint: Roots are $1, 2, 3$ with $W = 2e^{6x}$, $W_1 = e^{5x}$, $W_2 = -2e^{4x}$, $W_3 = e^{3x}$. Integrate $u_k' = W_k e^x / W$ and absorb pure $e^x$ into $y_c$.

1. Auxiliary Roots: $(m-1)(m-2)(m-3) = 0 \implies y_1 = e^x,\; y_2 = e^{2x},\; y_3 = e^{3x}$.

2. Variation of Parameters: $W = 2e^{6x}$; $u_1' = \frac{1}{2} \implies u_1 = \frac{1}{2}x$; $u_2' = -e^{-x} \implies u_2 = e^{-x}$; $u_3' = \frac{1}{2}e^{-2x} \implies u_3 = -\frac{1}{4}e^{-2x}$.

Thus $y_p = \frac{1}{2}xe^x + e^x - \frac{1}{4}e^x = \frac{1}{2}xe^x + \frac{3}{4}e^x$. Absorbing $\frac{3}{4}e^x$ into $C_1 e^x$ gives $y_p = \frac{1}{2}xe^x$.

3. Inverse $D$-Operator Verification: $f(1) = 0$ and $f'(1) = 3(1) - 12(1) + 11 = 2 \implies y_p = x\frac{1}{2}e^x = \frac{1}{2}xe^x$ ✓

General Solution: $y = C_1 e^x + C_2 e^{2x} + C_3 e^{3x} + \dfrac{1}{2}xe^x$. $\blacksquare$
